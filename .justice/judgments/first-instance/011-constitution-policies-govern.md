## Question
Should the law of this court state what governs an organisation's work, or defer to that organisation's own policies?

## Facts
{"direction":"the law should say defer to the policies, and the policies should be documents we can edit at any time (versioned)","constraint":"this should be the last edit to VJS; everything else is local law local to the org","observed":"a jurisdiction pinned from this court had a statute book describing only the court own machinery; asked whether an agent could send an invoice it returned the act constituting the court"}

## Ruling
Defer. The law of this court states nothing about what governs an organisation's work; the governing law is that organisation's own policies, provided each is a versioned document and any ruling applying one cites the version in force at the time of the facts. An unversioned policy cannot be applied: deferral fails closed rather than falling back on this court's constituting text. Where no policy addresses the act in question, the answer is that the question is not ready — not that the act is permitted, and not that this court's own machinery governs it. Policies govern the organisation's work only: they cannot supersede this law, and nothing entrenched here may be repointed at a document that may be edited.

## Reasoning
The choice was between naming substantive governors in this law and deferring to the organisation's; I took deferral, because the recorded failure is precisely r.15's failure mode — a pinned jurisdiction asked whether an agent could send an invoice answered with the act constituting the court, that act being the only law it had. Versioning is the condition that makes deferral compatible with r.2: a ruling binds as a citation, and a citation to a document that may since have changed is not checkable, so per r.6 an unversioned policy is no policy for this purpose. I decline the wider questions of who may edit a policy, how a version is adopted, and whether this is the last edit to VJS: a bench cannot bar its successors from amending (r.12), so that intention is recorded, not enacted.
