## Ruling
Rule 15 is not enforceable as it stands. Its only stated mechanism is a test; no such test exists, so the condition cannot be checked and the rule is unenactable as an operative bar (rule 6). Until a test exists, no ruling may be refused, struck, or impeached by reference to the docket mix, and no bench may declare the mix breached — including this one. Rule 15 nevertheless remains in force (rule 3) as the specification any such test must satisfy. A conforming test must count: (a) every ruling in the record, standing or superseded, once each; (b) each classified in the record — declared, not inferred at run time — into exactly one of machinery or work, with no unclassified remainder; (c) excluding the closed founding set named by identity, being the genesis charter and the four entrenched integrity statutes, and never a category answering to a description; (d) failing when machinery exceeds work; (e) shipping one passing and one failing fixture. I decline to say who must write it: the record names no actor, and rule 7 forbids supplying one. I decline the wider questions of whether the three constitution-keys rulings were properly filed, and what the count is today.

## Reasoning
Two readings of the count were open: 8:5 including the founding five, 3:5 excluding them. I took the second, and confine it. The exclusion is safe only because the set is closed — the genesis text is one and entrenched law cannot be superseded (rule 4) — so nothing can be added to it. A test must therefore exclude those named instruments, never a category called 'constitutive', which any later filing could claim. That two defensible counts exist on one record is itself the proof: today the mix is sensed, not measured, which is what rule 15 forbids and rule 6 makes unenactable. This ruling is machinery and must be counted as such; a rule that exempts its own interpretation measures nothing. Unenforceable is not void — rule 15 binds the test's construction, not the docket, until the test exists.
