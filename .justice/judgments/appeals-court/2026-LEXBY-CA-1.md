# [2026] LEXBY-CA 1

**COURT OF APPEAL OF THE VIBE JUSTICE SYSTEM**
**Before: Aldermere J (presiding), Blackmere J, Goffe J, Elden J**
**Appeal from: [2026] LEXBY-FI 1 (Bowan J)**
**5 June 2026**

---

## Ground of Appeal

Did the First Instance court err in law by applying s. 8 (first occurrence, forward duty) before completing the s. 5 Bolam inquiry, such that the ruling in [2026] LEXBY-FI 1 should be set aside?

---

## Outcome

**Appeal dismissed unanimously.**

---

## Ratio decidendi (binding)

The appeal is dismissed unanimously. The ground of appeal - that Bowan J applied s. 8 before completing the s. 5 Bolam inquiry - is not made out on a fair reading of the judgment below. Bowan J identified the s. 5 standard expressly, conducted the Bolam inquiry across the full artefact set, reached an affirmative finding, and only then invoked s. 8 to characterise the citation-numbering gap as generating a forward duty rather than a present breach. The sequencing complaint is further defeated by the structure of the statute itself: s. 8 cannot be applied without implicitly applying s. 5, because the reasonableness against which a novel gap is judged is the s. 5 standard. A procedural complaint that produces no different outcome on the merits does not found an appeal under s. 12. [2026] LEXBY-FI 1 stands as good law.

---

## Obiter dicta (persuasive)

Where a judgment invokes s. 8 in the wake of a completed s. 5 Bolam analysis, it is good practice - though not required - to include a brief explicit marker indicating that the s. 5 stage is closed before transitioning to the s. 8 characterisation. Such a marker would forestall sequencing complaints of the kind raised here. Courts are not obliged to narrate their logic in a formulaic checklist, but a transitional sentence costs little and reduces appellate attrition.

The forward duty recorded under s. 8 in [2026] LEXBY-FI 1 - deterministic citation numbering as a necessary condition for v1 - is confirmed as binding and should be discharged before any v1 release is considered.

---

## Remedy

None. No breach found. The forward duty identified at first instance stands.

---

## Panel opinions summary

**Blackmere J (textualist):** Dismissed. The statute's plain text at s. 8 contains the phrase "judged against reasonableness" which is a reference to the s. 5 standard. A court that applies s. 8 necessarily applies s. 5. The sequencing complaint describes a structural impossibility.

**Goffe J (pragmatist):** Dismissed. The appellant's real complaint is with the evaluative outcome of the Bolam inquiry, not with whether it was conducted. That is a merits challenge dressed as a procedural one and does not ground an appeal.

**Elden J (precedent-hawk):** Dismissed. No authority establishes that s. 5 and s. 8 must be applied under explicitly labelled sequential headings. The substance of the inquiry is what matters; the form of its narration is not reviewable.

---

## Lexby translation

The appellant argued that Bowan J had skipped a step - run the Bolam test - before invoking the forward-duty provision. The Court of Appeal read the judgment below carefully and found the step was there: Bowan J asked whether a responsible body of competent practice would endorse the release, said yes, and only then described the citation-numbering gap as a forward duty rather than a present breach. Beyond that, the three judges noted that the two provisions are structurally linked - you cannot apply one without the other - so the sequencing complaint describes something that is legally impossible. The earlier ruling stands. The citation-numbering forward duty is confirmed binding.

**What it means in practice:** [2026] LEXBY-FI 1 is good law and is not disturbed. The obligation to build deterministic citation numbering before v1 remains.

**Can it be appealed?** Permission to appeal to the Supreme Court may be sought on an arguable point of law or a binding-precedent conflict (VPR 3). Given the unanimous panel and the structural reasoning, any further appeal would face a high bar.
