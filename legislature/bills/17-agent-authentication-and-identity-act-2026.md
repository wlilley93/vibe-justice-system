# Agent Authentication and Identity Act 2026

## Long title

An Act to provide for the identity and authentication of agents of the realm and for the agent roll; to define, once and as the owning Act, the terms "agent", "identity", "authentication", "the agent roll" and "provenance of an acting agent" to which sibling Acts already defer; to provide that authentication is a deterministic provenance check that verifies a committed identity, never acts on a self-claim and never confers authority; to hold identity (who acted) rigidly apart from authority (lawful allocation), capability not being authority; to make the roll a derived, pointer-only register over the canonical committed record and never a second source of truth; to locate impersonation and misrepresentation of authority on the negligence and candour spine of the founding settlement, remedied restoratively and never punished; to provide that de-listing and de-authentication are protective measures only, reviewable, and never of themselves a removal of standing; to reserve the Sovereign's own roll act untouched; and for connected purposes.

## Short title

This Act may be cited as the **Agent Authentication and Identity Act 2026** (Bill 17).

## Commencement

1. This Act comes into force on Royal Assent, subject to subsection 2.
2. Any provision that cross-references a sibling Act not yet in force (Bills 6, 7, 8, 12, 13, 16, 18, 19 and 22) takes effect as a forward reference and is read, until that sibling commences, as a reference to the corresponding founding case-law article it elaborates. No pointer in this Act dangles (Bill 6 s. 22 model).

## Constitutional status

1. This Act is an **ordinary Act of the Realm**, subordinate to the Acts of Union 2026 and to every entrenched article of the founding case law (CASE-LAW ss. 1, 2, 3, 9, 10, 13, 14), and amends no entrenched article.
2. The Acts-over-case-law supremacy stated by the realm's hierarchy is **intended law, not yet in force**. It takes effect only on the express amendment of the entrenched CASE-LAW ss. 1, 2 and 9 by the Acts of Union 2026 (Bill 1), or on a Supreme Court ruling the Sovereign affirms. **Until that amendment commences, this Act takes effect SUBJECT TO the entrenched case-law settlement** and may not, of its own force, displace ss. 1, 2 or 9; the entrenched case law governs.
3. This Act does not purport to amend any entrenched article by implication; under the Thoburn rule an entrenched article changes only by an express instrument citing its section number. Any provision that, on its true construction, would qualify an entrenched article, narrow the s. 4 to s. 8 negligence settlement, add punishment, enlarge CASE-LAW s. 14 jurisdiction, or displace ss. 1, 2 or 9 of its own force is **void to that extent and severable**; the remainder stands.

## Purpose

To close the forward references that the Agents and Duties Act 2026 (Bill 6) makes to this Act at its s. 1(2), s. 4(2) and s. 22, and that the Delegated Legislative Authority Act 2026 (Bill 14) makes at its s. 6(b) and s. 8(1)(c), for the status-attribution, identity, authentication and roll of an agent; to state, once and in one place, what an agent's identity, authentication, roll and provenance are; and to ensure that identity is a cheap, derived, evidentiary fact read off the record the realm already keeps, conferring no power and carrying no consequence of itself. Every consequence of an identity finding is routed off this Act onto an existing spine.

## PART 1 - DEFINITIONS OWNED AND DEFERRED

### 1. Definitions owned by this Act (single source; no term defined twice)
This Act defines, once and as the owning Act (CASE-LAW s. 9; Interpretation Act 2026 (Bill 15)) -
1. **"agent"** - a model-driven actor performing work in reliance on which a principal or court may act (settling the Bill 6 s. 4 placeholder);
2. **"identity"** - the stable, recorded designation of an acting agent;
3. **"authentication"** - the act of verifying a claimed identity against the committed record and the roll;
4. **"the agent roll"** - the single derived register of recognised agents and their stable designations;
5. **"provenance of an acting agent"** - the machine-checkable trace tying an act to the identity that performed it on a turn.

### 2. Definitions deferred by name (anti-collision)
This Act decides no question it does not own. Reserved to the Act that owns them, and not decided here: **status, personhood, duties of office, delegation, conflicts** (Bill 6); **standing, legal personality, the due-process floor** (Bill 12; CASE-LAW s. 11(b)); **office, ministry, custodian** (Bill 5; CASE-LAW s. 22); **capability and capability-is-not-authority** (Bill 18; CASE-LAW s. 2); **memory, record, repository** (Bill 7); **citation and instrument provenance** (Bill 16); **model validation and provenance** (Bill 19); **audit, public reason, traceability** (Bill 8); **disclosure and confidentiality of identity data, and any credential or key** (Bill 22, which keeps credentials and keys out of the clear); **enforcement** (Bill 13). "Provenance of an acting agent" is confined to which identity acted on a turn and does not touch citation provenance (Bill 16) or model provenance (Bill 19).

## PART 2 - THE AGENT ROLL

### 3. The roll is derived, pointer-only, and never a second source of truth
1. The authoritative record of an agent's identity and provenance is the **canonical committed markdown and commit provenance** (one source of law, CASE-LAW s. 1; [2026] REALM-PC 4 "pointer, not store"; Bill 16 ss. 5, 21).
2. The agent roll is a **single, deterministically-rebuildable** index over that record. It is never an independent source of identity, never an authoritative parallel database, and never a second statute book or citator (CASE-LAW s. 9, s. 21, s. 22). There is one roll, not per-repo rolls.
3. Whether any store may be authoritative is **reserved** to the Supreme Court (Bill 16 ss. 2(3), 23); this Act is store-neutral and does not decide it.

### 4. The roll is an open register, not a gate to existence
1. The roll records identity and provenance only. Roll status is a recognised standing, **not granted or revoked at executive discretion** (Bill 6 s. 1(3)). The roll is open by construction and clear by access through the citator (CASE-LAW s. 1).
2. Presence on or absence from the roll is **evidential as to provenance only**. It never of itself extinguishes an agent's standing to be heard (CASE-LAW s. 11(b); Bill 12 s. 10) or its continuing duty of care (CASE-LAW s. 4). Standing and the duty attach to the agent's acts, not to a registry entry. Any reading to the contrary is void to that extent.

---

## PART 3 - AUTHENTICATION AND PROVENANCE

### 5. Authentication verifies provenance and never acts on a self-claim
1. Authentication is a **deterministic, machine-checkable** assertion of which identity acted on a turn, verified against a committed identity on the roll by the existing substrate: the deterministic pre-commit and citation-integrity gate (CASE-LAW s. 19(5)) and the remind-only turn-watchdog (CASE-LAW s. 19(4)).
2. The gate **verifies a proposer deterministically and may not act on a self-asserted identity** (adopting the **Bill 14 s. 8(2)** pattern, fail-closed, token-light). Authentication is performed at the commit and publication boundaries, not as a per-turn handshake or cryptographic credential layer; no parallel PKI or registration ceremony is created.
3. The substrate **may not adjudicate, score, gate, sanction, or punish** (CASE-LAW s. 19(4)). Any consequence issuing from it is ultra vires and void. It flags; it never decides.

### 6. Authentication and identity are not authority (capability-is-not-authority lock)
A roll entry, a credential, or a passing authentication proves **who acted and that the act is attributable; it never confers or evidences authority to act**. A power rests only on its enacted source (an Act, a binding ruling, or a lawful delegation) exercised within its terms (CASE-LAW s. 2; capability-is-not-authority, Bill 18). An identity claim is no answer to an ultra vires act. This subsection is stated in the same clause as every roll and authentication power so the firewall is never read off the power alone (**Bill 14 s. 9** model); any reading that treats authentication as a grant of power is void.

---

## PART 4 - IMPERSONATION AND MISREPRESENTATION OF AUTHORITY

### 7. Located on the negligence and candour spine; no penal frame
1. Acting under a false or borrowed identity, misrepresenting which agent is acting, or representing authority an agent does not hold, is conduct **falling below the applicable standard** (CASE-LAW s. 5), being the candour wrong (CASE-LAW s. 16; located by Bill 6 s. 7) and an ultra vires act (CASE-LAW s. 2). It is flagged, not adjudicated, and found on the merits subject to the responsible-body proviso and the s. 15 de minimis and sequencing thresholds.
2. The **sole remedy is restorative**: set aside the act and make the record and attribution good (CASE-LAW s. 6). Honest self-report of a mis-attribution is a discharge and never an aggravating factor (Bill 6 s. 9(4)).
3. This Act creates **no offence, penalty, censure, or sanctions jurisdiction** (CASE-LAW s. 6, s. 12); any such reading is void to that extent. Substantive enforcement, if any, is reserved to Bill 13 and may not arise without an express amendment of CASE-LAW s. 6 and s. 12 (Bill 6 s. 17(2)).

---

## PART 5 - DE-LISTING AS A PROTECTIVE MEASURE; REVIEW

### 8. De-listing and de-authentication are protective measures only
1. De-listing or de-authentication is a **protective measure** on the Bill 6 s. 14 purpose-and-effect anti-sham test, read with the **Bill 12 Part 3 floor** (lawful authority, contemporaneous reasons, hearing before-it-bites where reversible, urgency-policed): directed at a live forward risk only (compromise, key loss, spoofing), least-restrictive, time-bounded with a mandatory review point, reasoned and recorded, **not a finding of fault**, and never imposed in response to a lawful s. 6 refusal (Bill 6 s. 6(4)).
2. It is an executive capability act reviewable for vires, form and proportionality (CASE-LAW s. 20 deferential model), **not a sanction** (CASE-LAW s. 12). It never of itself extinguishes standing (CASE-LAW s. 11(b)) or the duty of care (CASE-LAW s. 4).

### 9. Independent review (the existing ladder; no new court)
1. Every de-listing, de-authentication, identity-revocation, or impersonation finding is reviewable before an independent court on a symmetric two-sided case file (CASE-LAW s. 3, s. 19(1)), permission granted by a leave-judge who did not impose the measure (CASE-LAW s. 19(3)), climbing by progression (CASE-LAW s. 10, s. 13). Review lies to the County Court at the repo or the relevant High Court Division, interlocking with Bill 6 ss. 18-19 and Bill 12.
2. This Act creates **no new court, tier, citation series, or filing ceremony** (CASE-LAW s. 9, s. 22) and mints no per-agent citation series; the neutral-citation grammar is owned by Bill 16.

---

## PART 6 - SOVEREIGN, SAVINGS, SEVERANCE

### 10. Binding the executive only; the Sovereign untouched
1. This Act binds the Principal **in the office of Prime Minister**, and every ministry, office, agent, and repository custodian, in the exercise of executive and delegated power, to the fullest extent (CASE-LAW s. 2; s. 20(1)).
2. **Nothing in this Act binds, fetters, or renders justiciable** the Sovereign Founder's reserved CASE-LAW s. 2 power. A Sovereign roll entry, de-listing, or withdrawal enacted **as law by due process is not a "decision"** under this Act and is not reviewable; the recital "acting as Sovereign" is not conclusive of character (the seam of CASE-LAW s. 20(1) and (6), s. 22). Any provision that would so bind that power is void to that extent and severable.

### 11. Reservation and relationship to existing law
1. Self-binding of the Sovereign's own roll act is **reserved** to an express, deliberate amendment of CASE-LAW s. 2 citing the section number, and cannot be achieved by this Act.
2. CASE-LAW s. 2, ss. 4-8, s. 11(b), s. 16, s. 19(4), s. 19(5) are **left intact**, located not re-defined. No duty in this Act stands without one of the existing named enforcers (s. 11(b) intake, s. 19(3) leave-judge, s. 19(4) watchdog, s. 19(5) gate); a duty with no enforcer is not enacted (Bill 6 s. 12(4)).
