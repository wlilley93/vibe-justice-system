## Declined or modified, with reasons (the honest ledger)

1. **Restraint's preference to drop first-draft s7 (decide-and-log)** — declined in favour of the
   cured permissive form, because Operability measured it enforceable with zero new code and the
   section is the Act's cheapest cost-reducer. The Restraint conditions on it (5, 6) are taken in
   full.
2. **Restraint's nine-section minimum** — not met numerically (structural change 6 explains the
   vehicle; Part 3 arrived by the Principal's instruction after round 1). Met in spirit: every
   surviving section names its gate or its court.
3. **Guardrail C4's "an allocation that omits a registered store is void"** — softened to
   refused-at-authoring / routed-on-assent, because voidness of an allocation on an assented record
   would itself cross FB-2. Guardrail's own floor analysis compelled the softening.
4. **Codification's physically separate amending schedules** — substance adopted via Sch 3;
   formatting offered at adoption. Reason: keeping each amendment beside its reasoning serves the
   second-round bench; a detached schedule serves the enacted text. Both can be true in sequence.
5. **The 20-record enumeration threshold (Restraint C13)** — the stricter unconditional rule
   (Guardrail C9) adopted instead. Where counsel conflicted, the stricter rule was taken in each
   case and this is the list of such conflicts: enumeration (Guardrail over Restraint), s7
   retention (Operability's measurement over Restraint's preference), allocation voidness
   (Guardrail's floor logic over Guardrail's own C4 wording).

**One correction to a counsel's own recital, recorded under the Act's own discipline:** Operability
C8's factual claim about `unreadable-orders.txt` (see the C8 row). The condition's substance was
right and is taken; its recital was stale; both are on the record.
