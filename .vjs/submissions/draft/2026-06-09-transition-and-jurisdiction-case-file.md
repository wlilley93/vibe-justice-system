## Part 2 - Q0: does the V2 Privy Council have standing and jurisdiction?

**Case FOR jurisdiction (the PC is competent).**
- The PC is the constituted realm-level first-instance court for constitutional, jurisdiction, routing, and public/private-boundary matters (2026-VJS-COURTS-CONSTITUTION-001 D2). Transition and onboarding are quintessential jurisdiction/routing questions.
- Kompetenz-kompetenz: a constituted court may determine its own jurisdiction; that is an ordinary judicial power, not an arrogation.
- Standing lies in the Principal's reference through Lexby as officer (the Principal has real, present stakes: live projects awaiting onboarding). This is a real party, a real question, real stakes.
- The "no phantom forum" limit is satisfied: the bench exists.

**Case AGAINST (or for partial decline / leapfrog).**
- Q3 (super-repo vs federation; the apex reconciliation) construes apex-singleness (s.15; REALM-SC 6) and is **foundational** - reserved to the V2 Supreme Court (the s.10/s.13/s.20 expansion trigger carried into V2), not a PC matter; a 3-judge bench should not entrench an apex rule.
- Q2's "should a project migrate" is a **Principal policy call** (reversible, low-blast per ACT-007), not justiciable - the PC should decline the policy and decide only the lawful mechanism.
- Parts of Q1 may be **fast-path**: vesting and supersession are already settled by Bill 32 + the courts-constitution order, disposable on citation without a sitting.
- A standing objection: is there a live dispute, or is this an advisory reference? (Answer likely: the Principal's reference is the recognised route, as in REALM-PC 12 / PC 24; but the bench should satisfy itself.)

**The likely shape (for the bench, not pre-empting it):** the PC has jurisdiction over Q1 (to the extent not already fast-path) and Q2's mechanism; it should **leapfrog Q3** as foundational; and it should decline Q2's policy ("whether to migrate") as the Principal's.

## Part 3 - Q1 (transitional continuity): the 1873 model

**Case FOR adopting the 1873 model in full.**
- It is the just, tested rule: transfer carries pending matters across at their current procedural state, preserves accrued rights, lets the old machinery perfect already-heard matters, and forbids relitigation. It avoids the chaos of treating every open matter as void or restarted.
- It fits the V2 settlement: Bill 32 already vested V1 runtime jurisdiction in V2 and continued the courts in continuity (s.15); the 1873 model supplies the *procedural bridge* the founding did not spell out.
- Anti-relitigation aligns with REALM-SC 7 (entry-by-entry, non-infectious; nothing voided wholesale by provenance).

**Case AGAINST / for variation.**
- V1 and V2 differ more than the 1873 old/new courts did (case-law-first vs computer-first; long prose vs compact records). A pending V1 matter may have no clean V2 procedural analogue; "continue at its current point" may be unworkable for some matters, which may need fresh V2 framing.
- The "perfect by old machinery" limb risks reviving V1 procedural machinery (s.9 concern) unless tightly bounded to *already-heard* matters and to publication-to-Gazette only.
- Some pending V1 matters may be moot post-commencement (the migration itself resolved them) and should be closed, not continued.

## Part 4 - Q2 (onboarding) and Q3 (super-repo / federation)

**Q2.** FOR express-incorporation-by-default: s.8/s.9 are clear - a migrating project's V1 local precedent is archive/persuasive unless expressly carried; this keeps V2 clean and avoids silent V1 revival at the project level. FOR a continuity election: forcing every project to re-incorporate its settled local precedent one by one is the relitigation the 1873 model condemns; a recorded wholesale election (in `.vjs/config.toml` + the local lawpack), revocable and local-scope-only (ACT-007:s4), is the efficient and just route. The tension: default-clean vs continuity-efficient. The bench may allow both (clean by default; election available).

**Q3.** FOR survival: a super-repo over peer local jurisdictions under one canonical lawpack is just ACT-007 federation with a coordinating root; REALM-SC 6 allowed it provided the apex stays single, which s.15 continuity preserves. AGAINST / caution: if the super-repo root asserts its own apex or binds peer repos beyond ACT-007:s4 (local-scope-only), it fractures the federation; this is the foundational risk that makes Q3 a Supreme Court matter.

## Part 5 - Relief options

- **(i)** PC takes jurisdiction over Q1/Q2-mechanism; settles the 1873 continuity doctrine (with the s.9 guardrail on the "perfect by old machinery" limb); directs a Transitional and Continuity Instrument; recognises onboarding as the local Principal's act with a continuity election; **leapfrogs Q3** to the V2 Supreme Court; declines Q2's migration *policy* as the Principal's.
- **(ii)** PC decides all four (including Q3) - only if it holds Q3 not foundational.
- **(iii)** PC declines/refers the lot to the Supreme Court as one foundational bundle.
- **(iv)** PC holds parts fast-path (vesting/supersession) and decides only the genuinely open mechanics.

The bench is bound by none of these. Nothing is migrated, adopted, or published by this file.
