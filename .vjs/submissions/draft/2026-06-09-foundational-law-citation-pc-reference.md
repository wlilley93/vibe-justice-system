# V2 Privy Council Reference: how the Realm organises and cites its foundational law

**Date:** 2026-06-09
**Filed by:** Lexby, on its own motion (self-submission duty, [2026] LEXBY-SC 3 s.17(b); first-impression trigger). NOT routed to the Principal.
**Court:** V2 Privy Council (citation system / constitutional structure / V1<->V2 boundary; bench of 3)
**Status:** public; system-data only; the question and rival cases stated neutrally (the intake), no access to Lexby's preference.

## The defect

V1's foundational document `Constitution/CASE-LAW.md` is, on its own source column, a **hybrid**: some articles were **enacted by the Principal** as Sovereign/Parliament (the "P" articles - e.g. s.1, s.2, s.13, s.14: Sovereign-enacted constitutional statute), and some were **made by the Supreme Court** (the "SC" articles - e.g. s.4-s.8, s.11, s.18-s.22: case law). It is captioned "CASE-LAW" and structured with statute-style sections (s.1...s.23), and the V2 corpus (the principles digest, the Framework Act, the Transition Doctrine) cites it pervasively as "CASE-LAW s.X". The Principal has identified the category error: case law is identified by the **case** that decided it, not by a section, so "CASE-LAW s.X" treats case law as an Act.

## The question

> How should the Realm organise and cite its foundational law, given V1 conflated Sovereign-enacted constitution and Supreme-Court case law under one sectioned "CASE-LAW" document - and what, if anything, is re-cited or restructured, consistent with the entrenched amendment procedure, the entrenchment list, Bill 16, and the non-disturbance rule?

Sub-questions: (a) is the binding convention that **statute/instrument is cited by Act + section, and case law by its case (neutral citation)**; (b) should the foundational law be **split** into a Sovereign-enacted constitution (cited by instrument/section) and a body of Supreme-Court case law (cited by case), or kept as one hybrid; (c) how is any change reconciled with the **entrenched amendment procedure** (which requires citing an article "by its section number"), the section-keyed entrenchment list, and Bill 16's citation scheme, without disturbing entrenched law or any immutable id/digest; (d) the **scope** of any re-citation pass over the live V2 corpus (digest, Framework Act, Doctrine), and whether it is a governed clerical pass or needs adoption.

## Case A - convention only (cite correctly, change no structure)

Adopt the convention (statute by section; case law by case) and **re-cite** the V2 corpus accordingly, but leave V1's `CASE-LAW.md` structurally intact (it is an archival V1-estate document; its sections and amendment machinery stay). *For:* least disturbance; honours the entrenched amendment procedure (sections preserved); the convention binds going forward; a clerical, governed re-citation pass suffices. *Against:* the hybrid mislabel persists in the archive (statute living under a "case-law" caption).

## Case B - split the foundational law in V2 (constitution vs case law)

In the V2 canon, separate the Sovereign-enacted constitutional articles (cited by instrument/section) from the Supreme-Court case law (cited by case), so the caption matches the source. *For:* removes the category error at the root; cleaner for a future V3 (the Generational Transition Doctrine's restatement step would carry it). *Against:* more disturbance; must be done by incorporation/restatement, not by editing the V1 archive; the V1 amendment procedure and entrenchment list are section-keyed, so the split must preserve a resolvable mapping; risks touching entrenched articles if done carelessly.

## Constraints the bench is asked to honour

Non-disturbance ([2026] VJS-SC 1): immutable ids, digests, locks, citation series. The entrenched amendment procedure and entrenchment list are themselves entrenched and section-keyed; they may be changed only by the heightened route. The back-fill rule ([2026] VJS-PC 3): nothing fabricated; transcription/derived-minute only. The Principal's correction is the occasion; the bench decides the law.

## Requested direction

Decide A, B, or a hybrid; fix the binding citation convention; state how it reconciles with the entrenched section-keyed machinery; and fix the scope/manner of any corpus re-citation (clerical-governed vs adoption-required). Nothing is re-cited or restructured by this reference.
