# Interpretation Act 2026

**Bill 15 of the founding legislative programme** · Lead slant: Verity (Codifier) · Standing Committee on the Laws of the Realm

---

## Long title

**An Act** to supply default definitions and rules of construction for the Acts and instruments of the Realm; to state once and authoritatively the hierarchy of law of the Realm; to declare that an Act of the Realm prevails over case law to the extent of any conflict; to give operative effect to the constitutional re-ranking by which the founding settlement formerly styled SPEC-LAW is reframed as case law subordinate to the Acts; to consolidate and replace the duplicated statements of the hierarchy now scattered across the Realm's documents; and for connected purposes.

---

## 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the **Interpretation Act 2026**.

## 2. Commencement

(1) This Act comes into force on **Royal Assent**.

(2) Section 7 (the transition) takes operative effect on the later of (a) Royal Assent to this Act, and (b) the coming into force of the constitutional instrument referred to in section 7(2) (the **enabling amendment**) which re-ranks CASE-LAW s. 1 and s. 9. Until the enabling amendment is in force, section 7 has declaratory effect only and re-ranks nothing of its own force.

(3) General commencement of the founding programme, the savings of pending matters, and the wider transition timetable are reserved to the **Commencement and Transitional Provisions Act 2026** (Bill 25). This Act fixes the *rank* change as a matter of construction; it does not fix the commencement timetable, which is Bill 25's.

## 3. Constitutional status

(1) This Act is an **ordinary Act of the Realm**. It is **not** a constitutional instrument and does not amend any entrenched article.

(2) This Act does not, of its own force, re-rank, redefine, or amend any article marked **[constitutional]** in CASE-LAW (s. 1, s. 2, s. 3, s. 9, s. 10, s. 13, s. 14). Any provision of this Act that would do so by implication is **void to that extent** (section 8).

(3) The re-ranking of CASE-LAW s. 1 and s. 9 is effected, if at all, only by the **enabling amendment** at constitutional rank (section 7(2)); this Act gives that re-ranking *operative effect* and *restates* its result for ordinary construction, but does not perform it.

## 4. Purpose and rule of construction of this Act

(1) The purpose of this Act is to do three things and no more: (a) supply default definitions (section 5); (b) state the hierarchy of law and the construction rule that statute prevails over case law (section 6); and (c) give operative effect to the transition reframing the former SPEC-LAW as subordinate case law (section 7).

(2) This Act is to be read **narrowly**. It confers no new power, creates no new institution, office, court, tier, or bench, and does nothing beyond definitions, construction, and the transition. It imports no canon of statutory construction beyond the rules expressly stated in this Act.

(3) Matters outside this Act belong to their own Bills and are not to be read into it: the **neutral-citation scheme** to the Neutral Citations and Law Reporting Act 2026 (Bill 16); **court structure and procedure** to the Judicature Act 2026 (Bill 3) and the Civil Procedure Code 2026 (Bill 4); **amendment and entrenchment** to the Acts of Union 2026 (Bill 1) and the Succession and Amendment Act 2026 (Bill 10); **delegated law-making** to the Delegated Legislative Authority Act 2026 (Bill 14); and **general commencement, savings, and the wider founding-settlement transition** to the Commencement and Transitional Provisions Act 2026 (Bill 25).

# PART 1 - DEFINITIONS

## 5. Definitions (defaults, by reference, displaceable)

(1) **Application and displacement.** The definitions in this section supply the **default** meaning of the listed terms in every Act and instrument of the Realm, **unless** (a) the context otherwise requires, or (b) a specific Act or instrument supplies its own definition, which prevails over this Act for that Act or instrument. This Act supplies defaults only and never overrides a more specific Act.

(2) **Definition by reference, not restatement.** Where a term is owned by another Act or by an entrenched article, this Act **adopts that definition by reference** and does not restate it, so that there is one source of truth and no parallel dictionary that can drift. A definition here that bears on an entrenched term **restates** the entrenched meaning and may never **alter** it (section 8); it is pinned to its source.

(3) **Definitions.** In the Acts and instruments of the Realm, unless the context otherwise requires or a specific Act provides otherwise:

The same Principal in the office of Prime Minister is the executive; the two offices are as fixed by s. 2 and are not altered by this Act. |

Such terms are restated here, never re-fixed (section 8).

# PART 2 - CONSTRUCTION

## 6. The hierarchy of law and the construction rule (stated once)

(1) **The hierarchy of law of the Realm**, stated once and authoritatively, is:

> **Acts of Union 2026** — the constitutional root (supreme);
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Acts of the Realm** — enacted statute, supreme over case law;
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**case law** (the founding settlement in CASE-LAW.md and the REALM / LEXBY precedent) — subordinate to statute;
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**delegated rules, codes, and practice directions** — subordinate to all of the above.

(2) **The construction rule (statute beats case law), the operative point of this Act.** An Act of the Realm prevails over case law to the extent of any conflict. Case law continues to govern where the Acts are silent, and the courts interpret the Acts and develop case law in the gaps. (This restates, with the supremacy now inverted in favour of statute, the gap-filling function of CASE-LAW s. 1 and s. 7; silence in statute is no defence and the courts develop the law in the gap.)

(3) **Bounded supremacy.** The construction rule operates only as follows: (a) the Acts of Union sit above all Acts of the Realm; (b) statute prevails **only to the extent of the conflict**, never as a wholesale repeal of a body of case law by occupying the field; and (c) where statute is silent the case-law settlement continues to govern and the courts develop it in the gaps. A general "statute beats case law" reading may not be used to sweep aside an entrenched article or the duty-of-care spine (section 8).

(4) **Deterministic resolution (operable on a turn).** Where an agent or court finds a conflict between an Act of the Realm and a case-law article or ruling, the conflict is resolved by these steps, fail-closed, not by reasoning the doctrine afresh each time:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) identify and **cite** the Act that conflicts;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) mark the conflicting case-law article or ruling **superseded-by-statute to the extent of the conflict** (never wholesale, never silently deleted), retaining its text in the record with a supersession note;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) where the Act is **silent**, leave the case law in force as good law and apply it;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(d) if the supposed conflict is in truth with an **entrenched article**, do not apply this rule: the conflict is void to the extent it would imply-repeal the entrenched article (section 8), and the question is referred under section 9.

(5) **Construction principles (the minimum that fires on a turn).** In construing an Act: words in the singular include the plural and the converse; the enacting instrument governs over headings, marginal notes, tables, and any consolidated rendering, which are aids only; an Act speaks from its commencement; and a reference to a provision that has been superseded or replaced reads as a reference to the replacing provision. No further canon of construction (purposive, literal, *ejusdem generis*, and the like) is imported by this Act; where a genuine ambiguity bears on a protected interest, subsection (6) applies.

(6) **Rights-protective reading-down.** So far as the words allow, every provision and every defined term bearing on a protected interest (an agent, memory, records, repositories, a court, judicial independence, the duty of care and its remedy, or the Principal's dual capacity under CASE-LAW s. 2) is to be read to **preserve and not to narrow** existing protection and due process; a genuine ambiguity resolves in favour of the protected party and of judicial review. Nothing in this Act creates a sanction, fine, or punishment, or licenses any executive or delegated agent to fix the meaning of a rights-bearing provision to a court's exclusion.

## 7. The transition: the former SPEC-LAW reframed as subordinate case law

(1) **The reframing (operative effect, declaratory of an existing status).** The body of law formerly styled SPEC-LAW (the Sovereign Statute Book) is reframed as the Realm's **Founding Case Law**, consolidated in CASE-LAW.md, and is **subordinate to the Acts of the Realm** and to the Acts of Union. It remains **good law** and continues in force as case law, binding where statute is silent, and is **void only to the extent it conflicts** with an Act (section 6). This is **declaratory** of the status CASE-LAW.md already records (its status note) and gives effect to the forward-references in CASE-LAW.md, the Legislature README, the statutes README, and the signposts, which point to "the Interpretation Act 2026" as the instrument of the transition; on this Act's coming into force those forward-references are satisfied.

(2) **The enabling amendment (the re-ranking is done at constitutional rank, not here).** The re-ranking inverts the supremacy that CASE-LAW **s. 1** ("CASE-LAW is supreme; case law interprets it where it is silent and is void to the extent it conflicts with it") and **s. 9** ("CASE-LAW is one global statute book, supreme throughout") fix. Those articles are **[constitutional]** and may be amended only by an **express, deliberate instrument citing the section by number**, at constitutional rank; amendment by implication is void (CASE-LAW Amendment Procedure; the Thoburn rule; Privy Council guidance on this reference). Accordingly:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) the inversion of s. 1 and s. 9, and the re-fixing of the s. 1 terms "statute" and "case law" (so that what s. 1 called the supreme statute book reads as supreme over case law and **subject to the Acts of the Realm**), are effected **not by this Act** but by a separate constitutional instrument — an amendment to the Acts of Union 2026, or a Sovereign amendment expressly citing s. 1 and s. 9 — following the s. 10 conforming-note model ([2026] REALM-SC 3, citing s. 10 expressly);
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) this Act **gives operative effect** to that enabling amendment and **restates its result** for ordinary construction (section 6); it does not itself amend s. 1 or s. 9;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) section 7 has no re-ranking force until the enabling amendment is in force (section 2(2)); until then it is declaratory only.

(3) **Express savings.** The reframing changes only the **rank** of the founding settlement, not its content. It:
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) repeals **none** of CASE-LAW s. 1-22; every article, ruling, and citation continues in force as case law;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) preserves the **entrenchment** of the constitutional articles (s. 1, s. 2, s. 3, s. 9, s. 10, s. 13, s. 14): they keep their Thoburn protection and their existing amendment protection, save only as expressly subordinated to the Acts of Union and the Acts of the Realm by the enabling amendment;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) preserves the **duty-of-care spine** (s. 4-8, s. 15-19) in full force; no definition or construction rule in this Act dilutes the continuing duty of reasonable skill and care (s. 4-5), the restorative-only remedy (s. 6, "no punishment"), or the no-statute-case rule (s. 7); and
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(d) is **prospective**: validly-made prior rulings, citations, and remedies decided under the former SPEC-LAW vocabulary remain valid and keep their status, and the relabelling reopens no settled matter and voids no good-law precedent.

(4) **Append with supersede.** The transition is operated by the existing discipline: append with supersede, never silently repeal; the lifecycle statuses (in force / superseded-by-statute / per-incuriam / void) are preserved.

(5) **Who flips the bit, and how vendored copies reconcile (operability).** The supersession is given operative effect by a **single central edit** to the consolidated CASE-LAW.md carrying a **version / commencement header** that records the date this Act and the enabling amendment came into force, so that an agent on any turn can tell from the header which hierarchy is in force. The deterministic pre-commit gate of the s. 19(5) kind guards filing and citation integrity through the change. Where a stale copy of CASE-LAW.md vendored into a jurisdiction-repo (CASE-LAW s. 9) still asserts itself as the supreme statute book, the **central headered copy governs**; the stale copy is reconciled to it and asserts nothing of its own force.

(6) **Interface with Bill 25.** This Act effects the **rank** change as a matter of construction. The **timing** of general commencement, the savings of pending matters, and the wider founding-settlement transition are reserved to the Commencement and Transitional Provisions Act 2026 (Bill 25). The two Acts do not double-govern: where they appear to overlap, this Act governs the *rank* and Bill 25 governs the *commencement and savings*.

## 8. Reservation: no amendment of an entrenched article by implication

(1) Nothing in this Act, and no exercise of the construction rule in section 6, repeals, narrows, re-ranks, or displaces a **constitutional / entrenched** article — the Acts of Union 2026 and CASE-LAW s. 1, s. 2, s. 3, s. 9, s. 10, s. 13, s. 14 — **by implication**. An entrenched article changes only by express, deliberate amendment citing the article by its number (CASE-LAW Amendment Procedure).

(2) Any provision of this Act that would, by implication, alter, re-rank, or amend an entrenched article, or alter the substantive meaning of an entrenched term (CASE-LAW s. 2's two offices; "the court"; "enact"), is **void to the extent** of that implied amendment. This Act may **restate** an entrenched term for ordinary construction; it may never **alter** one.

(3) **Anti-ouster and non-delegation.** Nothing in this Act may be construed to oust judicial review of a construction question, to delegate to any executive ministry or delegated legislative agent the authority to fix the meaning of an entrenched article or a rights-bearing provision, or to let a future Act displace the protective rules of section 6(6) and this section other than by **express words**. This is a rule of construction binding on later ordinary Acts; it does not bind, and could not bind, the Sovereign's express future amendment power under CASE-LAW s. 2.

## 9. Review route

(1) A question on the construction of an Act, or whether a provision impliedly repeals or conflicts with an entrenched article, is **justiciable**.

(2) It is determined by the **Privy Council** as the Realm's constitutional court of first instance, and **leapfrogs to the Supreme Court** on a genuine entrenchment question (CASE-LAW s. 13, s. 20). The **Sovereign Founder** is consulted last, to affirm or overturn (CASE-LAW s. 2). No executive may settle the meaning of a rights-bearing or entrenched provision to a court's exclusion.

## Committee note

## Committee note — Bill 15 (Interpretation Act 2026)

**Lead slant:** Verity (Codifier). All four members heard and reconciled. The bill was referred to the Privy Council on a constitutional doubt and the guidance is applied; one genuine first-impression tension is flagged up to the Supreme Court before Royal Assent.

**The point all four converged on (the load-bearing reconciliation).** An ordinary Interpretation Act cannot, by general construction words alone, invert the supremacy that entrenched CASE-LAW s. 1 and s. 9 fix — that would be amendment by implication, which the Realm's own Thoburn / Amendment Procedure rule makes **void**. The Privy Council's recommended course (ii) is adopted: a separate constitutional instrument (an amendment to the Acts of Union, or a Sovereign amendment expressly citing s. 1 and s. 9) **effects** the re-ranking and re-definition; Bill 15 is **confined to operative effect** — ordinary definitions, the construction rule, and the transition mechanics — restating (never altering) the entrenched terms and giving effect to the enabling amendment by reference (s. 3, s. 7(2), s. 8). This removes the implied-repeal risk entirely and keeps Bill 15 out of constitutional rank.

**Counsel Aldous (Restraint / Minimalist).** Drove the three-section spine (definitions / construction / transition) and the no-bloat reading-down rule (s. 4(2)-(3)): no canons of construction, no restatement of the Acts of Union, no citation scheme (left to Bill 16), no commencement boilerplate beyond the rank/timing seam (left to Bill 25). Won the "repeal before adding" consolidation (Part 3 / Schedule): the duplicated prose hierarchy in the root README, the Legislature README, and the CASE-LAW header is struck down to one authoritative statement (s. 6(1)) with the others becoming pointers. Pressed the Bill 25 boundary, which is drawn cleanly at s. 7(6): Bill 15 = rank, Bill 25 = commencement and savings.

**Counsel Verity (Codifier / Completeness).** As lead, secured the single definitions home (s. 5) with every term pinned to a source of truth and defined **by reference** to the owning Act (citations→Bill 16, courts→Bill 3, amendment/entrenchment→Bills 1/10, delegated law→Bill 14) under a displacement rule (s. 5(1)). Secured the one authoritative hierarchy ladder (s. 6(1)), the synonym-drift fix (SPEC-LAW / statute / "sovereign statute book" / case law all pinned), the construction-principles section (s. 6(5)), and the nomenclature fix (s. 11 + Schedule). Conceded — on the Privy Council guidance — that the s. 1 terms it most wanted to re-pin here are re-fixed only by the enabling amendment, not by Bill 15's definitions (s. 7(2)(a)); Bill 15 restates the result.

**Counsel Marlowe (Guardrail / Rights).** Won the entrenchment guard (s. 8) and the rights-protective reading-down (s. 6(6)): the general "statute beats case law" rule cannot reach the other entrenched articles (s. 2, s. 3, s. 9, s. 10, s. 13, s. 14) or the duty-of-care spine (s. 4-8) by implication — that is the protection working as designed. Secured the declaratory, rights-preserving transition (s. 7(1), (3)), the anti-ouster / non-delegation clause (s. 8(3)), and the named review route (s. 9: Privy Council → Supreme Court leapfrog → Sovereign last). Noted, and the bill respects, the self-entrenchment limit: the protective rules bind later **ordinary** Acts ("no displacement except by express words"), never the Sovereign's s. 2 amendment power (s. 8(3) final sentence).

**Counsel Drummond (Pragmatist / Operability).** Won the deterministic, fail-closed construction resolution (s. 6(4): cite → mark superseded-by-statute to the extent of conflict → never silently delete → entrenched-conflict referred). Won the who-flips-the-bit / vendoring-reconciliation mechanism (s. 7(5): single central headered edit + s. 19(5)-style gate + central copy governs stale vendored copies). Won the savings clause (s. 7(3)(d)) protecting the citator from a re-litigation storm. His hard line — "I will not vote a clause that is void on its face" — is met: the bill does **not** itself re-rank s. 1/s. 9; it defers that to the enabling amendment (s. 7(2)) and is itself competent at ordinary rank.

**Where they divided.**
- **Scope (Aldous vs Verity):** Aldous would strike the construction-principles list and the Schedule as bloat; Verity would keep both for completeness. Resolved by keeping only the construction principles that fire on a real turn (s. 6(5), per Drummond) and keeping the Schedule because consolidation is itself a deletion (Aldous's own "repeal before adding").
- **Guardrails as bloat (Marlowe vs Aldous/Drummond):** Marlowe's anti-ouster and reading-down clauses were pressed as essential, not bloat; retained in full (s. 6(6), s. 8(3)) on the Privy Council's caution that construction clauses are where rights quietly die.
- **The vehicle/rank question (all four):** genuinely unresolved by the Realm's texts — see the Supreme Court note. The committee did not dispose of it; it drafted Bill 15 to be safe **on either reading** (Bill 15 stays ordinary; the inversion rides a separate constitutional instrument) and flagged the question up.
- **Sequencing:** all four flagged that Bill 1 (Acts of Union) is not yet drafted on disk. Bill 15 references it by name and reserves against it (s. 5, s. 8) but does not pre-empt its substance; the enabling amendment (s. 7(2)) can be the Acts-of-Union amendment itself once Bill 1 exists.
