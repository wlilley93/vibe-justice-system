# The `Reasons-record:` commit-trailer convention

> The **only** behavioural addition the Reasons Ledger introduces (Bill 8 s. 4(2): reuse existing
> artefacts, add no new artefact type). A significant-decision commit carries its reasons-record **inside
> the commit message it would write anyway** - it is not a second log, register, or ceremony (Bill 8
> s. 6(3), s. 25). The trailer is parsed deterministically by `build-reasons-ledger.py`; no model
> judgement is involved (CASE-LAW s. 19(5)).

## When it is owed

A reasons-record is owed only for a **significant decision** (Bill 8 s. 5(2)):

- (a) a court-convening trigger (a first-impression design / architecture / consolidation / scope /
  naming fork; a Principal instruction conflicting with statute or precedent; a proposal to overrule
  precedent; or a discovered breach);
- (b) an irreversible or outward-facing act: **publish, send, deploy to production, spend, or grant a
  capability**;
- (c) a load-bearing or cross-cutting architectural fork; or
- (d) a step implementing a contested or appealable ruling.

A **routine turn** that meets none of these owes **no** reasons-record (Bill 8 s. 5(2) closing words;
CASE-LAW s. 15 de minimis). A convenience or non-ideal choice in genuinely disposable scaffolding that
weakens no governed surface and leaves every invariant intact owes nothing.

Note that two of the three sources need **no** trailer: caselaw forks are already structured in the
citator, and enacted Acts are already structured in the bill record. The trailer is for the **executive
acts that would otherwise leave only a bare commit subject** (deploys, publishes, spends, capability
grants, and load-bearing architectural commits).

## The format

A single block, placed at the **bottom of the commit body** (after a blank line), before any
`Co-Authored-By:` trailer. Four keys, exactly the four s. 5(3) fields, each on its own line; a long value
may wrap onto continuation lines (any line that does not begin with a known `Key:` continues the previous
field). The block ends at the first blank line or `Co-Authored-By:` line.

```
Reasons-record:
Decided: <what was decided - the act itself, stated contemporaneously>
Why: <the reasoning or basis>
Rested-on: <the governing instruction, statute, case law, or binding ratio relied on>
Authorised-by: <the actor / office and the authority under which it was taken>
```

Field mapping to Bill 8 s. 5(3):

| Trailer key     | s. 5(3) field            |
|-----------------|--------------------------|
| `Decided`       | (a) what was decided     |
| `Why`           | (b) why                  |
| `Rested-on`     | (c) what it rested on    |
| `Authorised-by` | (d) who/what authorised it |

Only `Decided` is strictly required by the parser (so a sparse record still surfaces); supply all four
to discharge the duty in full. Keys are case-insensitive; `Rested-on` / `Authorised-by` may be written
with any capitalisation.

## Worked example

```
Deploy the Realm Law Reports reading room to production

Stands up the searchable case-law + legislation corpus at the public endpoint, rebuilt
pointer-only in lockstep with the citator and the bill record.

Reasons-record:
Decided: Deploy the Realm Law Reports reading room to production (outward, irreversible)
Why: PC-4 requires any derived index to be regenerated in lockstep with its sources and to
 remain pointer-only; the corpus is rebuilt from the committed markdown on every deploy
Rested-on: [2026] REALM-PC 4 (index-not-replacement); Bill 8 s. 4(2) (reuse, no new home);
 Bill 16 (neutral-citation/provenance scheme); Bill 20 (repository certification)
Authorised-by: Prime Minister office (executive, CASE-LAW s. 2); Sovereign authorisation on
 the record per legislature/bills/SOVEREIGN-CONSULTATIONS.md

Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 4.8 <noreply@anthropic.com>
```

`build-reasons-ledger.py` parses that into one full **Executive** row (trigger:
`significant-decision commit (Reasons-record trailer)`) with all four fields populated and a
`git:<short> (<date>)` pointer - no duplication of the commit text, just a pointer into it.

## Pre-convention commits (backfill)

Commits made **before** this convention that are nonetheless significant (they touched `.justice/`,
`statutes/`, or `legislature/`, or their subject records publish / send / deploy / enact / grant /
reconstitute / release) are listed automatically with their extractable subject, the author, and a
`git:<hash>` pointer, marked `(reasons in commit body)`. Read the full reasoning with `git show <hash>`.
The convention is **not** applied retroactively and these rows are never rewritten (never-rewrite-history).

## Enforcement (do not over-wire)

Bill 8 s. 7 names the enforcer as the **existing** deterministic fail-closed gate (CASE-LAW s. 19(5)) and
the per-turn watchdog (s. 19(4)) - not a new mechanism. A pre-commit check MAY warn when a commit touches
a governed surface yet carries no `Reasons-record:` trailer, but it must **fail open** (warn, never block)
unless and until a court rules that blocking is trivially safe: blocking commits is itself a governed,
load-bearing change that should not be adopted silently. See README.md "Integration".
