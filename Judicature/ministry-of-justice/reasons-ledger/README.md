# Reasons Ledger

The **executive analogue** of the rulings ledger ([`../ledger/`](../ledger/)). It is the realm's
pointer-only index of every **significant decision** and its **reasons-record**, discharging the
**Public Reasons and Audit Act 2026 (Bill 8)**.

Where the rulings ledger ([`../ledger/INDEX.md`](../ledger/INDEX.md)) indexes the realm's *judicial*
holdings, the Reasons Ledger indexes the *executive* decisions and the reasoning trail behind them: the
four fields Bill 8 s. 5(3) requires for any significant decision -

1. **what was decided** (the act itself, stated contemporaneously);
2. **why** (the reasoning or basis);
3. **what it rested on** (the governing instruction, statute, case law, or binding ratio); and
4. **who or what authorised it** (the actor / office and its authority).

## What makes a decision "significant"

Only an enumerated **significant decision** owes a reasons-record (Bill 8 s. 5(2)):

- a court-convening fork (first-impression design / architecture / consolidation / scope / naming; a
  Principal instruction conflicting with law; a proposal to overrule; or a discovered breach);
- an irreversible or outward-facing act (publish, send, deploy to production, spend, grant a capability);
- a load-bearing or cross-cutting architectural fork; or
- a step implementing a contested or appealable ruling.

A **routine turn owes nothing** beyond the automated trace the substrate already emits (s. 5(2) closing
words; CASE-LAW s. 15 de minimis). The ledger does not invent significance; it reads it off the existing
traces.

## The legal basis: reuse, derive, do not duplicate

This ledger is built to two binding constraints.

**Bill 8 s. 4(2) (reuse, no new home).** The reasoning trail *reuses existing artefacts* - the engineer's
duty to record why (CASE-LAW s. 3), the symmetric researched case file (s. 19(1)), the universal ledger
and the one citator (s. 1, s. 11(d), s. 22), the deterministic citation-integrity gate (s. 19(5)), and the
per-turn watchdog (s. 19(4)). It creates **no new artefact type, no new register, no new log, and no
second governance home** (s. 4(2), s. 24, s. 25). Accordingly this ledger stores **no decision text of its
own**: it points into the citator rows, the bill files, and the git commits that already hold it.

**[2026] REALM-PC 4 (index-not-replacement).** A derived index over the committed record is permitted only
if it is *wholly derived, deterministically rebuildable, never a store of any ratio / status / authority, a
pointer only, and regenerated in lockstep with its sources* so it never drifts into a competing record.
This ledger satisfies all of those: it is regenerated by re-running the clerk, holds only pointers, and
re-running it on the same repo state produces byte-for-byte identical output.

> **This is NOT a source of law or truth.** It is a projection. If it disagrees with the citator, a bill
> file, or a commit, the **source** governs and the projection is stale - rebuild it. It never adjudicates,
> scores, gates, or referees; it is read-only and surfacing-only (Bill 8 s. 10, s. 25).

## The three sources it projects from

| # | Source (existing trace)                              | Significance trigger        | How the four fields are derived |
|---|------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------|---------------------------------|
| 1 | The **citator(s)** - `.justice/INDEX.md` (central) and any sub-repo `.justice/INDEX.md` (CC-* hearing-centres) | court-convening fork (s. 5(2)(a)) | what = the ratio; rested_on = the Cites column; authorised_by = the court + its citation; pointer = the ruling path the citator links |
| 2 | The **bill record** - `legislature/bills/NN-*.md` (header + long title), `ORDER-PAPER.md`, `SOVEREIGN-CONSULTATIONS.md` | statute / Royal Assent (s. 5(2)(b)) | what = enact the Act (its long title); authorised_by = Sovereign Founder on Royal Assent + the committee vote |
| 3 | **Git history** - `git log` over the repo | significant-decision commit | full record from a `Reasons-record:` trailer (going forward); pre-convention significant commits listed with subject + `git:<hash>` pointer + "(reasons in commit body)" |

Sources 1 and 2 are already fully structured, so they need no new convention. Source 3 is the only one
that adds anything: a documented **commit-trailer convention** for the executive acts that would otherwise
leave only a bare commit subject (see below and [`.reasons-convention.md`](.reasons-convention.md)).

## How to rebuild

From the repo root:

```sh
python3 ministry-of-justice/reasons-ledger/build-reasons-ledger.py
```

It writes [`INDEX.md`](INDEX.md). No third-party dependencies (Python 3 standard library only). It reads
git via `git log` / `git show`; the output is a pure function of the committed repo state, so re-running it
without changing the sources is a no-op on the file (byte-for-byte identical). Regenerate it whenever a
ruling, an Act, or a significant commit lands - in lockstep with the source, exactly as
[`../ledger/build-ledger.py`](../ledger/build-ledger.py) is regenerated after a new ruling.

## The commit-trailer convention (the one behavioural addition)

A significant-decision commit carries its reasons-record inside its own commit message, at the bottom of
the body:

```
Reasons-record:
Decided: <what was decided - the act itself>
Why: <the reasoning or basis>
Rested-on: <the governing instruction, statute, case law, or binding ratio>
Authorised-by: <the actor / office and its authority>
```

This is parsed deterministically (no model judgement). It is **not** a new log or register - it is text the
commit message would carry anyway (Bill 8 s. 4(2), s. 6(3), s. 25). The full spec, field mapping, and a
worked deploy-to-production example are in [`.reasons-convention.md`](.reasons-convention.md).

### Worked example (the short form)

```
Deploy the Realm Law Reports reading room to production

Reasons-record:
Decided: Deploy the Realm Law Reports reading room to production (outward, irreversible)
Why: PC-4 requires the derived index to be rebuilt in lockstep and pointer-only
Rested-on: [2026] REALM-PC 4; Bill 8 s. 4(2); Bill 16 (citation scheme)
Authorised-by: Prime Minister office (CASE-LAW s. 2); Sovereign on the record

Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 4.8 <noreply@anthropic.com>
```

Becomes one full **Executive** row in `INDEX.md` with all four s. 5(3) fields and a `git:<short>` pointer.

## Integration (do not over-wire)

- The Reasons Ledger is a **sibling** of the rulings ledger under `ministry-of-justice/`. The two are
  parallel projections of the same transparency goal: one indexes holdings, one indexes decisions and their
  reasons.
- Bill 8 s. 7 names the enforcer as the **existing** deterministic fail-closed gate (CASE-LAW s. 19(5)) and
  per-turn watchdog (s. 19(4)) - not a new mechanism. A pre-commit hook MAY warn when a commit touches a
  governed surface (`.justice/`, `statutes/`, `legislature/`) yet carries no `Reasons-record:` trailer, but
  it must **fail open** (warn, never block): blocking commits is itself a governed, load-bearing change and
  must not be adopted silently. A binding decision to make it blocking would itself be a significant
  decision under s. 5(2)(c) - put it to the court first.
- Files in this directory:
  - [`build-reasons-ledger.py`](build-reasons-ledger.py) - the clerk (the only executable; mirrors
    `../ledger/build-ledger.py`).
  - [`INDEX.md`](INDEX.md) - the generated projection (rebuilt on every run; do not hand-edit).
  - [`.reasons-convention.md`](.reasons-convention.md) - the commit-trailer spec.
  - `README.md` - this file.
