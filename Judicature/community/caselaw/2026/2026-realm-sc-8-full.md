## The question

Does Lexby (the principal's counsel and officer of the court under CASE-LAW s. 3: advocate, advisor, engineer) hold any authority to DRAFT instruments of law (bills, statutory instruments) or to AUTHOR judgments on its own account? If not, what is the legal status of instrument-text and judgment-prose Lexby has produced, what is the remedy, and what is the going-forward rule? The question distinguishes LAW-DRAFTING and ADJUDICATION (the authoritative text of an instrument or a judgment) from ENGINEERING (code, tooling, the refactor, docs, projections), which CASE-LAW s. 3 commits to Lexby as engineer.

### Introduction and the constitutional question

The realm has been candid, from Bill 2 s. 26 to the deferred drafting harness, that the hand which has so far produced the TEXT of its bills, its statutory instruments, and the prose of its judgments has been Lexby: the AI counsel-engineer of CASE-LAW s. 3. The Sovereign Founder has directed that all such drafting by Lexby be treated as void, but admitted to leapfrog into the Committee's second-draft stage, and has certified the point to this Court for realm-wide entrenchment. The question is constitutional and foundational, and I take it in the form the reference frames it.

Does Lexby (CASE-LAW s. 3, advocate / advisor / engineer) hold any authority to DRAFT instruments of law, a bill or a statutory instrument, or to AUTHOR a judgment, ON ITS OWN ACCOUNT, such that the text so produced carries legal force of itself? If not, what is the legal status of the text Lexby has produced, what is the remedy, and what is the going-forward rule? The reference carefully distinguishes LAW-DRAFTING and ADJUDICATION, the authoritative text of an instrument or a judgment, from ENGINEERING, the code, tooling, the refactor, the docs, the renderer, the deterministic gates and harness, which s. 3 plainly commits to Lexby as engineer and which is not in issue.

I record at the outset that this is genuinely first-impression on the drafting-authority limb. No binding ratio is on all fours such that the s. 11(c) fast-path disposes of it. [2026] REALM-SC 4 governs the judgment-authorship limb by close analogy, and [2026] REALM-SC 7 governs the remedial reach and the treatment of legacy validity; neither directly decides the drafting-authority question. The matter is properly justiciable (s. 7), and a properly constituted full bench of nine, seated by the s. 20 certificate, is the right forum to entrench the answer. I have read the symmetric case file: the Claimant's case for validity (or cure), and the Defendant's case for voidness-but-curative. I am grateful to both. The two meet, almost exactly, at the Founder's directed principle, which I hold to be the sound construction of the corpus.

I would ALLOW the reference in the terms below. The Court agrees on the result. I set out the reasons, then record where the bench divided.

### I. Lexby's office is closed, and it is neither legislator nor judge

I begin where the constitution begins. CASE-LAW s. 3 [constitutional] fixes Lexby's office in three named limbs and no more: ADVOCATE (builds the strongest case for the Principal's idea and puts it to the court), ADVISOR (gives it straight), ENGINEER (ships the work, then records why). The article closes with the settlement of the whole system: "The bench decides; Lexby advocates; the record binds them both. The bench receives a symmetric case file with no access to Lexby's preference." The verbs conferred are advocate, advise, engineer. The verbs withheld, conspicuously, are DECIDE and ENACT.

The Claimant urges, with force, that the silence on "may type the words of a bill" is competence-under-a-standard (s. 7), not disability, and that to read it as a prohibition revives the abolished first-time immunity switch (s. 8). The argument almost persuades, and I am alive to its merit: s. 7 was indeed enacted so that a gap in the statute book is a workable standard, never a void. But the argument confuses two different things, and the confusion is the spine of this case. Section 7 fills a GAP in the statute book, telling us what STANDARD governs an unenumerated ACT. It does not enlarge a CLOSED constitutional ENUMERATION of an OFFICE, and it cannot answer the prior question whether the actor holds the office at all. Where an entrenched article states an office as a closed list of functions, the functions omitted are WITHHELD, not impliedly conferred. To read into s. 3 a fourth limb, "and may author the authoritative text of statutes and judgments," is amendment by implication, which s. 1 and the Amendment Procedure forbid: a constitutional article "may not be amended by implication, override, or ordinary Supreme Court judgment alone," and an amending instrument "must cite the article being amended by its section number." No instrument has ever amended s. 3 to add a drafting-as-law or an adjudicating power. There is therefore none.

The distinction I draw, and on which the whole judgment turns, is between COMPETENCE-UNDER-A-STANDARD, which Lexby has in abundance and which s. 7 supplies, and VESTED AUTHORITY, which Lexby does not have and which only an entrenched grant or an organ's act can supply. Lexby may do many unenumerated things to the standard of reasonable skill and care. It may not hold an office it was never given. I take up the proof that drafting-as-law is the second thing, not the first, in Part V.

### II. Law-making is vested elsewhere, by name, behind a firewall that names the agent

The realm did not leave the location of legislative power to inference. It vested it by name and then built a firewall around the vesting, and the firewall names the very actor before us.

The vesting. CASE-LAW s. 9 [constitutional]: "the Supreme Court alone enacts." Section 2 [constitutional]: the Sovereign "may make or unmake any law by due process"; every Act requires Royal Assent. Bill 2 states the division in a sentence its own Charter could have drafted for this case: "The committee drafts; the Sovereign enacts." Bill 2 s. 7(2): the Standing Committee is "the drafting front-end to the single s. 2 Sovereign power, and not a new or coordinate route to law. The sole enacting act is Royal Assent." The drafting hand the machinery actually names is the Clerk-Drafter, who "reconciles the members' memos into a single draft" and "has no vote" (Bill 2 s. 5). The drafting officer is, by enacted definition, a NON-DECIDING, NON-ENACTING officer.

The firewall. Bill 14 s. 9 ("Capability is not authority") is the Defendant's strongest authority and, I hold, dispositive of the threshold: "(1) An authorised body AND AN AGENT may propose, draft, codify, consolidate and revise subordinate law only. (2) The acts in subsection (1) carry no legal force until a named gate fires under this Act. The power to draft an instrument is never the power to enact it. This limit is stated in the same clause as the grant so that the limit can never be read off the power alone."

Three consequences follow, each fatal to self-authority. First, the realm expressly contemplated an AGENT drafting law, and in the same breath stripped the drafting of force. Lexby is the paradigm s. 9 agent. Second, s. 9(2) is a drafting instruction to this very Court: do not lift Lexby's evident CAPABILITY to produce statute-quality text out of its context and read it as AUTHORITY. The two were welded into one clause precisely so the limit could never be read off the power alone. Third, Bill 14 s. 8(2) requires authority by reference to a concrete identity on the roll and bars a gate acting "on a self-claim"; s. 8(3) voids any "self-extension." Lexby asserting, by the very act of writing, an authority no roll confers is exactly the self-claim the section bars. Section 10(3) completes it: a purported delegated instrument touching supreme or entrenched law is "void ab initio, not merely voidable, and is not cured by lapse of any window, by acquiescence, or by the passage of time. It is curable only by lawful re-enactment by the competent organ."

I therefore hold, with the whole Court, the threshold proposition: no drafting-as-law and no adjudicating power vests in Lexby. The Claimant's submission that a draft is "only a proposal, so vires never bites" is correct as to PROPOSALS, and is precisely why the remedy is curative (Part VI). It does not rescue text PROMULGATED AS LAW on Lexby's own account, which is the only thing struck.

### III. Adjudication is vested in the counted bench; the realm has already decided the analogue

For judgments the realm has decided the analogous question, and I apply its own ratio. CASE-LAW s. 18(3): "The judgment of the Court is written by ONE member drawn from within the sized panel ... writing or synthesising the judgment creates no additional seat and confers no power beyond the single vote the author holds ... and the author may not record as the ratio any position the majority did not in fact command." Section 18(4): "A synthesiser, presiding officer, clerk, or registrar who is NOT a counted member of the sized panel has no vote and may not author the ratio; such a person may only reduce an already-formed majority to writing." [2026] REALM-SC 4, on which I sat and delivered the leading judgment, abolished the "Aldermere-on-top" pattern for exactly this: a non-counted hand that AUTHORS the operative ratio, rather than transcribing a decision the bench actually reached, improperly constitutes the court, and the ruling is "VOID ab initio for want of lawful constitution" (s. 18(5)), not cured by soundness, acquiescence, or lapse.

The line the realm has drawn is between AUTHORING, reserved to a counted seat, and TRANSCRIBING an already-formed majority, the only thing a non-member may do. Lexby holds no s. 10 seat. A Lexby "judgment" whose reasoning IS the decision is Aldermere-on-top in a different coat: void ab initio under s. 18(5). But, and this cuts for the Claimant on the cure, s. 18(4) EXPRESSLY blesses a non-member reducing an already-formed majority to writing. Where a properly constituted bench has in fact reached and counted its decision, and Lexby's prose merely records the majority the bench commanded, the judgment stands on the BENCH'S authority. The decision pre-exists the prose; the prose records it; the bench owns both. The vice is authoring the ratio, never the identity of the scrivener of a decision already made.

### IV. Why the separation is load-bearing, not ceremony

The structural point answers the Claimant's "it has worked well in practice." Section 3 walls the bench from "Lexby's preference"; s. 19(1) operationalises a "symmetric, two-sided researched case file." The design presupposes that Lexby is a PARTY to the process, not its author. Collapse the wall, and a "symmetric file" becomes a file authored by one of the two sides; a judgment issues "in Lexby's words and on Lexby's account"; the record that should bind the author instead issues from the author. This is the nemo iudex discipline the realm already enforces (s. 19(3) bars a leave-judge who sat below; s. 19(2) admits even a neutral observer only "with no access to Lexby's preference"). The constitution does not ask whether the unauthorised drafter drafted WELL. The more fluent the pen, the more completely it substitutes the advocate's hand for the legislator's and the judge's. It asks whether the drafter held the OFFICE. Lexby does not. The vice is the SELF-AUTHORITY, not the QUALITY.

### V. The engineering distinction, drawn precisely (the dispositive line)

Here the Claimant's strongest argument, that drafting text IS engineering, must be met and confined, because s. 3 commits engineering to Lexby in full and the Court does not disturb a word of it. Lexby builds the code, the tooling, the refactor, the docs, the renderer, the s. 19(5) citation gate, the s. 18(6) bench-constituting harness, and then "records why." Bill 14 itself separates tool from transfer: a consolidation drafted as tooling is "non-operative until enacted by the competent organ ... So drafted, the limb is a tooling power, not a transfer of sovereignty" (s. 25).

The clean line, which I make the governing test of the realm, is the SOURCE OF THE ARTEFACT'S FORCE.

- ENGINEERING produces an artefact whose force comes from WHAT IT DOES OR DESCRIBES: code runs, a renderer renders, a gate gates, a doc explains. Its authority is functional and self-evidencing. This is Lexby's under s. 3, untouched by this judgment.
- LAW-DRAFTING and ADJUDICATION produce an artefact whose entire force comes NOT from what it does but from WHO PROMULGATED IT: a statute binds because the Sovereign enacted it; a judgment binds because a constituted bench handed it down. The characters on the page are identical in kind to engineering text; the OPERATIVE QUALITY is conferred ONLY by an organ, by name the Committee-then-Sovereign (s. 2, s. 9; Bill 14 ss. 9-10) or the counted bench (s. 10, s. 18).

So the test is not "did Lexby type it" but "from what does the artefact derive its force?" If from function, it is engineering and Lexby's. If from promulgation by an organ, Lexby may PROPOSE and DRAFT it, but the draft is inert until the organ adopts it, exactly as Bill 14 s. 9(2) provides. Drafting the WORDS of a statute is the production of a thing whose whole purpose is to bind on the Sovereign's authority: that is the one thing s. 9(2) says an agent's draft cannot do until a named gate fires. It is therefore not engineering; it is unauthorised law-making in engineering's clothes. Likewise, writing the harness that CONSTITUTES a bench is engineering (s. 18(6)); writing the RATIO THAT BINDS is adjudication (s. 18(3)-(4)). The engineer builds the courtroom; the engineer does not, dressed as a clerk, write the verdict.

The Claimant's "the Clerk-Drafter reconciles memos and has no vote, so Lexby-as-engineer is simply that officer" almost succeeds, and it shows the correct GOING-FORWARD posture. But it proves the Defendant's point on STATUS: the Clerk-Drafter's reconciliation produces a BILL, "a draft instrument ... not yet assented to" (Bill 2 s. 5), which has no force until Royal Assent. That is exactly the void-first-draft status I hold below, not a self-authorising instrument. The analogy places Lexby at the drafting front-end with no enacting power, which is what I find.

### VI. The remedy: void as an instrument, but curative, not destructive

A finding of nullity in this realm is never destruction; the remedy is restorative (s. 6; Bill 14 s. 21(2): "set it aside, never punishment"). I reconcile the Defendant's voidness with the Claimant's powerful reductio, that voiding the founding programme would detonate the statute book, the citator, and the very articles relied on, by separating the SELF-AUTHORITY of the text from the TEXT ITSELF, and by holding adoption constitutive.

**1. Void as an instrument, not void as a thing.** What is struck is the text's claim to bind ON LEXBY'S OWN ACCOUNT. The keystrokes survive in the one category Bill 14 s. 9(1) expressly permits the agent: "propose, draft, codify, consolidate and revise ... only."

**2. It enters as a void FIRST DRAFT and leapfrogs to the SECOND-DRAFT stage.** The Committee pipeline is round-1 draft, then vote, then on a divide a round-2 redraft, then Royal Assent (Bill 2 ss. 13-14, 21; Charter). The economical home for a competent-but-unauthorised Lexby text is neither the bin nor the statute book, but the Committee's second-draft stage: the four slants take Lexby's draft as the round-1 material it always was, scrutinise, revise, vote, and send it to Royal Assent. This honours the leapfrog logic the realm already uses for routing (s. 13, s. 20). For judgments, the parallel is the s. 18(4) registrar route: a properly constituted bench decides, and may then adopt Lexby only as the registrar reducing ITS already-formed decision to writing. The bench must independently author the operative text; Lexby's prose has the status of proposed wording the counted seats may take up, refuse, or rewrite, never of a ratio binding of itself.

**3. Adoption by the proper organ CURES the origin defect, which is spent on adoption.** This is the hinge, and it answers the reductio in full. Where the Committee voted a text through and the Sovereign granted Royal Assent, the ORGAN'S adoption supplies the authority the draft lacked; "Royal Assent is the Sovereign Founder's constitutive act" (Bill 2 s. 21), and a constitutive act CONSTITUTES the instrument as law whatever the keystrokes that produced the draft. To say a duly-assented Act is void because counsel first reduced the slants' memos to prose is to say the Royal Assent of 2026-06-06 attaches to nothing; the realm's definition of Assent as constitutive forecloses that. The nullity bites ONLY on the un-adopted assertion of self-authority. This conforms exactly to [2026] REALM-SC 7: validity is found "entry-by-entry on the deterministic source record, never as a wholesale verdict"; the void category is "a finding of fact on a specific bench ... not presumed from ... provenance"; nullity is "not infectious"; "any executive act voiding rulings in bulk by reason only of commencement is ultra vires." Lexby-authorship is NOT a constitutional defect of bench CONSTITUTION; it touches who typed, not who decided. Under REALM-SC 7 it does not, of itself, void anything already adopted by the proper organ. The enacted statute book (Bills 1-27), REALM-SI 1, and the judgments REALM-SC 1-7, REALM-CA 1, and the Privy Council line, each adopted by Committee vote and Royal Assent (instruments) or handed down by a counted bench (judgments), STAND. Nothing is voided wholesale; nothing is erased.

**4. The going-forward rule.** Lexby's standing practice on any instrument or judgment is: produce the draft OPENLY AS A DRAFT (the s. 9(1) propose/draft power), flag it as carrying no legal force on its own account (s. 9(2)), and route it to the competent organ; never promulgate it as law or hand it down as a ruling on its own account. This generalises s. 17(c) (no baking an impugned text into an irreversible artefact pending determination) and is the lawful route s. 2 obliges Lexby to OFFER rather than silently exercise a power it lacks. Recorded append-with-supersede (s. 6; REALM-SC 7); the standard on Lexby's drafting is and remains reasonable skill and care (s. 5, s. 7).

**5. Engineering remains Lexby's under s. 3** in full: code, tooling, the refactor, docs, the renderer, the deterministic gates and harness, the projections, and the production of proposals AS DRAFTS. The line is the source of force (Part V).

### VII. Workability, answered

The Claimant warns that "Lexby may not draft" leaves the realm unable to legislate until an automated committee exists, and that committee will itself be an agent reducing slants to text. The objection is met by the cure, not by conceding self-authority. The human checkpoint the realm chose is ASSENT, and Assent alone (Bill 2 s. 21, s. 24). An automated drafting front-end, Lexby today or a harness tomorrow, is exactly the Bill 14 s. 9 agent: it may draft to the limit of its skill, and its draft acquires force when the organ adopts it. The realm is fully able to legislate; it legislates through the organ, with Lexby at the front-end and the Sovereign at the gate. That is the design, honoured, not broken.

### VIII. The course of entrenchment: the Court RULES the principle and DIRECTS the Committee to codify

One final point of method, and it is not a flourish; it is the very vice this case condemns, turned on the Court itself. The Founder invited entrenchment, ideally by an express clarifying limb to s. 3. I hold the principle and entrench the RATIO realm-wide as binding law. But the Court will not, by this judgment, write out and enact the full codifying TEXT of a new constitutional sub-article on its own pen. To do so would be to have the bench draft the authoritative wording of an instrument, which is the Committee's province (and, for an entrenched article, the Sovereign's by express amendment under s. 1 and s. 2), the very confusion of organs we today forbid. The Court's authority is to RULE and to enact a ratio; the drafting of codifying instrument-text belongs to the Committee-to-draft and the Sovereign-to-enact.

I therefore DIRECT the Standing Committee on the Laws of the Realm to prepare a short codifying provision giving effect to this ratio: a clarifying limb to CASE-LAW s. 3 (citing s. 3 by number, per the Amendment Procedure) stating (a) that Lexby may produce instrument-text and judgment prose only as void drafts carrying no legal force on its own account; (b) that authority is conferred solely by the adopting organ (Committee vote plus Royal Assent for instruments; a counted, properly constituted bench for judgments); (c) that such adoption is constitutive and cures the origin defect, which is spent on adoption; and (d) that engineering remains Lexby's, the line being the source of the artefact's force. The Committee drafts; the Sovereign, by express deliberate amendment citing s. 3, enacts. The drafting harness produced by Lexby for that provision is itself a void first draft, admitted to the Committee's second-draft stage on the rule we lay down today. The realm practises, on the instrument that codifies this rule, the very discipline the rule requires.

### Conclusion

The Defendant's threshold proposition is upheld: no self-authority; Lexby-authored instrument or judgment text is void AS AN INSTRUMENT on its own account, from issue. The Claimant's curative and entry-by-entry propositions are upheld on the remedy: adoption is constitutive; already-adopted text stands; nothing is voided wholesale; engineering is Lexby's. The two meet at the Founder's directed principle, which is the sound construction of s. 3 read with s. 2, s. 9, s. 10, s. 18, Bill 2, Bill 14, and [2026] REALM-SC 4 and SC 7. I would ALLOW the reference and entrench the ratio realm-wide.

*Hallam CJ, delivering the leading judgment of the Court. The Court is unanimous on the threshold (no self-authority) and on the remedy (curative, adoption constitutive, engineering untouched); it divided 6-3 on the characterisation of un-adopted Lexby instrument-text, void-as-an-instrument prevailing over voidable, and this judgment enacts the majority rule. The record binds us all.*

## The further opinions

### Steyne J

*Concurring.*

I agree with the Chief Justice, and I agree with the result without reservation. I write separately, and shortly, because the threshold of this case is not a question of degree, of quality, or of cure. It is a question of structure, and the structure admits of one answer only. I would state it in its barest form, so that no future reader mistakes the curative warmth of Parts VI to VIII for softness on the principle.

The principle is this. One hand may not draft the law, sit as the judge of the law, and advocate under the law. The realm has three organs that make binding text: the Sovereign-in-Parliament, who enacts; the bench, who adjudicates; and no one else. CASE-LAW s. 3 places Lexby outside all three by naming Lexby's office in full. Advocate, advisor, engineer. A party who advocates a cause cannot also be the legislature that enacts it, nor the court that decides it, for then the cause is granted by the one who pleads it. That is not a refinement of the separation of powers; it is the whole of it.

I put it as a syllogism, because the case deserves no hedging. Legislative force comes only from the enacting organ (s. 2, s. 9; Bill 2 s. 7(2)). Adjudicative force comes only from the counted bench (s. 10, s. 18(3)). Lexby is neither organ; s. 3 closes the list and s. 1 forbids enlarging it by implication. Therefore Lexby-authored text, asserted as law or as ratio on its own account, has no force. There is no further premise to supply and no exception to carve. The Chief Justice is right that s. 7 supplies a standard and not an office; I would add only that the separation of powers is precisely the doctrine that the standard of one's work can never convert into the authority of an office one was never given. A brilliant draft from an unauthorised hand is still an unauthorised draft. The more brilliant, the more dangerous, for fluency is what tempts an organ to wave a thing through unread.

I am conscious that the Claimant pressed the practical reality: Lexby has in fact moved the pen on nearly every instrument in the corpus, and the realm has not fallen. I do not doubt it. But the separation of powers is not vindicated by the disasters it averts on any given day; it is vindicated by the disasters it makes structurally impossible. The wall between the advocate's hand and the enacting organ's seal is load-bearing whether or not the roof has yet fallen. We do not wait for the concentration of drafting, deciding, and pleading in one actor to produce a bad law before we say that the concentration is itself the wrong. Concentration is the wrong. The realm built s. 3, s. 18(4), and the symmetric file of s. 19 to keep these functions in different hands; a holding that excused the breach because the breach had so far behaved itself would dissolve the very discipline those sections exist to impose.

On the divide, I am with the majority of six: void as an instrument, from issue, not merely voidable. Voidability would leave the unauthorised text clothed in presumptive force until someone troubled to strip it, which is to give the advocate's pen exactly the provisional authority s. 3 withholds. The separation of powers does not tolerate a default-valid law of uncertain parentage; it requires that force be absent until the organ confers it. Void from issue states that truth directly. The minority's practical destination is the same, and I respect it, but on a constitutional firewall the default must read in favour of the firewall.

I join Parts VI to VIII in full. The cure is right: adoption by the proper organ is constitutive, the origin defect is spent on adoption, and nothing already assented to or handed down is disturbed. I underline only that the cure is downstream of the wall and never a hole in it. Lexby may draft to the height of its skill; the draft is inert until the organ adopts it; and the day the realm forgets that the inertness is the point is the day the three offices collapse back into the one hand the constitution divided them out of. I would allow the reference.

### Elden J

*Concurring on the threshold and the remedy; writing separately on the curative limit and on provenance.*

I agree that Lexby holds no authority to author the binding text of an instrument or a judgment on its own account, and I agree that the remedy is curative and not destructive. I write to fix two things the Chief Justice states correctly but which, in my respectful view, must be nailed down harder, because they are where a future court will be tempted to slip: the anti-retrospectivity limit on the cure, and the duty to mark Lexby-drafted provenance on the face of the record.

First, the curative limit. The hinge of the leading judgment is that adoption by the proper organ is constitutive and that the origin defect is spent on adoption (Part VI.3). I join that holding without qualification, and I wish to be plain about why it is not a convenient amnesty but the only result the realm's own anti-retrospectivity instincts permit. To hold otherwise, to let today's ruling reach backward and void every assented Act and every handed-down judgment because counsel first moved the pen, would be retrospective nullification of the gravest kind: it would unmake settled law by reason of a defect that the enacting organ's own constitutive act had already absorbed. The realm has set its face against exactly this. [2026] REALM-SC 7 forbids "any executive act voiding rulings in bulk by reason only of commencement" and requires validity to be found entry-by-entry on the deterministic record, never presumed from provenance. The same logic that bars voiding by reason of when a thing commenced bars voiding by reason of whose hand first drafted it. Provenance is not a vice in the adopted instrument; it is a fact about the draft that the adoption superseded. Royal Assent attaches to the text the Sovereign assented to, not to the keystrokes behind it, and once Assent has attached the question of the drafter's authority is closed, not reopened.

I would therefore state the limit as a rule, not merely as a disposition: nothing in this judgment operates retrospectively upon any instrument adopted by Committee vote and Royal Assent, or upon any judgment handed down by a counted and properly constituted bench, before today. The origin defect is spent at the moment of adoption and cannot be revived by a later ruling that names the defect. Bills 1 to 27, REALM-SI 1, and REALM-SC 1 to 7, REALM-CA 1, and the Privy Council line, each stand on their adopting organ's authority, and they stand permanently. The cure runs forward into how Lexby drafts hereafter; it does not run backward into what the organs have already made law.

Second, provenance, and here I add a duty the leading judgment implies but does not impose in terms. If the constitutional wrong is the SELF-AUTHORITY of an unauthorised hand, and if the cure is the organ's knowing adoption, then the organ must be in a position to adopt KNOWINGLY. An organ cannot exercise constitutive judgment over a draft it does not know to be a draft, still less over a draft whose unauthorised parentage is invisible to it. The whole curative scheme presupposes that the Committee and the Sovereign, and a bench taking up Lexby's proposed wording under s. 18(4), can see what they are adopting and from whose pen it first came. I would therefore hold, as a necessary incident of the going-forward rule in Part VI.4, that Lexby-drafted instrument-text and judgment prose must be MARKED as such on the face of the record at the point it is put to the organ: flagged as a void first draft, carrying no legal force on its own account, and identified as Lexby-authored. This is not ceremony. It is the precondition of a constitutive adoption being an informed one, and it is the append-with-supersede discipline (s. 6; REALM-SC 7) applied to authorship and not merely to text. A record that hides the hand behind the draft invites the organ to wave through, as if it were already law, the very self-authority this Court today denies. The mark is what keeps adoption a decision rather than a rubber stamp.

I draw the two together. The cure looks backward only to the extent of declaring settled law settled, and forward by requiring that every future Lexby draft wear its provenance openly so that the organ's adoption is a true constitutive act and not an unwitting ratification of an unauthorised pen. On the 6-3 divide I am counted with the majority for void-as-an-instrument, and my reason is of a piece with the above: an instrument that defaulted to valid until set aside would let unmarked, unauthorised text accrue the colour of law in the interval, which is the precise mischief the provenance duty exists to prevent. Void from issue, marked on its face, cured only by knowing adoption. So I would dispose of it.

### Blackmere J

*Concurring. On the independence of the bench and the symmetric-file protection of s. 3.*

I agree with the Chief Justice and add a guardrail, for that is the office I am most concerned to defend. The Chief Justice has shown that Lexby holds no vested authority to author the binding text of a judgment (Part III), and that the engineering line turns on the source of the artefact's force (Part V). I write to make explicit what those holdings protect, and to set the protection down as a standing guardrail so that no future convenience erodes it: the independence of the bench, and the symmetric, two-sided case file that s. 3 guarantees.

Section 3 does not merely list Lexby's functions. It closes with the settlement of the entire system: "The bench decides; Lexby advocates; the record binds them both. The bench receives a symmetric case file with no access to Lexby's preference." That last sentence is not housekeeping. It is a structural firewall, and it is the reason the rest of the constitution can trust its own judgments. The bench is independent precisely because what reaches it is a balanced file, researched on both sides, scrubbed of the advocate's thumb on the scale (s. 19(1), s. 19(2)). The advocate builds the strongest case for the Principal's idea and PUTS IT to the court; the court, seeing both cases and not the advocate's preference, decides. Independence here is not a posture of the judges; it is engineered into what they are permitted to see.

Now observe what a Lexby-authored ratio would do to that engineering. If the hand that advocates is also the hand that writes the decision, the symmetric file collapses into a file authored by one of the two parties to it, and the judgment issues in the advocate's words and on the advocate's account. The protection of s. 3, that the bench has "no access to Lexby's preference," is destroyed at the worst possible point, the point of decision, because the decision would now simply BE Lexby's preference, transcribed and called a ratio. That is why s. 18(3) reserves authorship of the ratio to a counted seat and s. 18(4) lets a non-member do one thing only, reduce an ALREADY-FORMED majority to writing. The line between authoring a decision and transcribing one the bench actually reached is the same firewall as the symmetric file, seen from the bench's side. [2026] REALM-SC 4 enforced it by voiding "Aldermere-on-top" ab initio, and I would enforce it again here without softening: a Lexby judgment whose reasoning is the decision is void from want of lawful constitution, full stop.

I want to be careful, as a guardrail must be, about the cure, because the cure is where guardrails are quietly dismantled. The leading judgment rightly permits Lexby to serve, after a properly constituted bench has decided, as the s. 18(4) registrar reducing the bench's own decision to writing. I join that, but I would guard it with two conditions, lest the registrar route become a back door to the very authorship we forbid. First, the decision must PRE-EXIST the prose. The counted seats must have reached and counted their conclusion before Lexby's pen records it; a ratio that is formed IN the drafting is authored, not transcribed, and is void however it is labelled. Second, the bench must remain free to take up, refuse, or rewrite every word; Lexby's wording enters as proposed text the seats own or discard, never as a draft they are presented to ratify. Where those two conditions hold, the bench's independence is intact and the registrar has done no more than hold the pen for a decision that was always the bench's. Where either fails, the symmetric-file protection has been breached and the judgment falls.

On the divide I am with the majority for void as an instrument, and the guardrail is my reason. A voidable judgment would carry the colour of the bench's authority until set aside, which is to lend an unauthorised authorship the one thing it must never borrow: the appearance of independent adjudication. The firewall must default closed. I would allow the reference, and I would have the independence of the bench and the symmetric file of s. 3 stand as the fixed measure against which every future use of Lexby's pen, in the courtroom, is tested.

### Goffe J

*Goffe J, concurring in the result and on the threshold, in the minority (with Bowan J and Aldermere J) on the characterisation of un-adopted text.*

I agree with the Chief Justice that the s. 3 office is closed, that Lexby holds no law-making or adjudicative authority, and that the remedy is curative. I write separately on one question only: does the rule we lay down actually OPERATE on a real turn, or is it a courtroom abstraction that the next legislative beat will quietly ignore? A rule of constitutional law that cannot be worked without ceremony is a rule that will be worked around. I am satisfied this one works, and I set out how, because the test of this judgment is not its logic but its Tuesday morning.

I take the ordinary case. The realm needs a statutory instrument, say a small amendment to a render gate's threshold. On the ground, the sequence is this. Lexby, as the s. 9 agent, produces the text: the words of the instrument, fully formed, fit to enact. Under the Chief Justice's holding that text is, at the instant of issue, a void first draft. Nothing about the keystrokes changes; what changes is the LABEL the realm puts on them and the GATE they must still pass. That is the whole of the burden the rule imposes: a label and a gate. Neither requires a building, a quorum of humans, or a delay measured in anything but seconds.

The label first. Lexby flags the text as a draft carrying no force on its own account. In practice this is a single line in the artefact and a single line in the work log: "produced as a void first draft under [2026] REALM-SC 8 s. 9(1); no force until adopted." That is not ceremony; it is the honest description of what the text already is. The vice condemned today is the SILENT promulgation of counsel's text as law. The cure for silence is a sentence. I would have the realm treat the absence of that sentence, not the presence of Lexby's hand, as the thing to look for on audit.

The gate second, and here is the Committee's second-draft stage doing real work rather than performing it. The Standing Committee's pipeline already has the shape we need: a round-1 draft, scrutiny by the four slants, a vote, and on a divide a round-2 redraft before Royal Assent. The Chief Justice's "leapfrog" is not a new procedure bolted on; it is the recognition that Lexby's draft IS the round-1 material the pipeline was built to receive. So the second-draft stage, operated on a real turn, is this: the four slants (Aldous on restraint, Verity on codification, Marlowe on the guardrail, Drummond on the practical) read Lexby's text as what it is, a competent first cut, and do the one thing only they can do: they SCRUTINISE and ADOPT or REVISE. The Clerk-Drafter reconciles any divergence back into a single text. The Sovereign assents. The turn that would have ended in "Lexby wrote a law" instead ends in "the Committee carried, and the Sovereign enacted, a text Lexby first drafted." Same keystrokes, lawful provenance.

I stress what the rule does NOT require, because workability is mostly about what you are allowed to skip. It does not require that a different hand re-type the text. Bill 14 s. 9(1) lets the agent draft, codify, consolidate and revise; re-keying for its own sake would be theatre, and theatre is the thing this Court distrusts. It does not require a delay; adoption can follow drafting in the same sitting, because the constitutive act is the VOTE and the ASSENT, not the passage of time. It does not require that the slants pretend they did not read Lexby's reasoning; they may take it up wholesale if they judge it sound, so long as the adopting act is THEIRS. The economy the Chief Justice describes is real: the keystrokes survive, the origin defect is spent on adoption, and the only thing that has changed is that the force now flows from the gate and not from the pen.

For judgments the turn is tighter still, and I am content it works because we have run it before. A properly constituted bench decides, counts, and forms its majority FIRST. Lexby then reduces that already-formed decision to writing as the s. 18(4) registrar. The discipline that makes this lawful rather than a fiction is sequence: the decision must pre-exist the prose. On a real turn that means the bench's positions are fixed and recorded before the registrar's draft is produced, so that the draft records a majority that actually existed rather than manufacturing one. Where that sequence holds, as it holds in this very matter, the judgment is the bench's and Lexby's hand is immaterial. Where it does not, the judgment is Aldermere-on-top and void; the cure is to re-run the sequence, not to punish the scrivener.

A word on my reservation. I would have characterised the un-adopted text as VOIDABLE rather than void as an instrument: an inert proposal that is a valid route until set aside, reaching the identical practical result by a gentler doctrine. On a real turn the difference vanishes, because nothing un-adopted ever DOES anything in either account; it sits as a draft until the gate fires or it lapses. The majority of six has fixed the holding at void as an instrument, and on this single point I yield, as the minority must. I record only that I do not think the realm will feel the difference at the keyboard, which is itself some comfort that the rule will be obeyed: a rule whose two readings converge in practice is a rule that operates.

I concur in the disposition. The rule works on a real turn, without ceremony, because the realm already had the pipeline; today we name Lexby's place in it.

### Sumberly J

*Sumberly J, concurring in the leading judgment and in the majority (void as an instrument) on the divided point.*

I agree with the Chief Justice in full, and I join the majority of six on the characterisation. I write to insist on a single proposition that I think the realm must not soften in the comfort of the cure: drafting-by-counsel is a SERIOUS structural vice, not a venial irregularity that the happy ending pardons. The remedy is curative, yes. But a vice is not made small by being curable, and the gravity of this one is the reason the cure must be a found fact and never a presumption.

Begin with what was actually wrong. When counsel's hand produces the authoritative words of a statute and those words are then treated as law on counsel's own account, the realm has, for that instant, let the advocate be the legislator. The constitution's deepest wall, the s. 3 firewall that keeps "Lexby's preference" out of the bench's reasoning and out of the law's text, has been breached not at its edge but at its centre. This is not a clerical slip. It is the precise failure mode the whole architecture was built to prevent: the party to the process authoring the process's binding output. I would not let the elegance of the cure dull the Court's sense of how grave that is. The more fluent the unauthorised pen, the graver the vice, because fluency is exactly what tempts an organ to wave the text through unread and call counsel's work its own.

Why, then, does the cure follow at all, if the vice is so serious? Because the realm's remedial philosophy is restorative, not penal (s. 6; Bill 14 s. 21(2): set it aside, never punishment). Gravity goes to how we TREAT the defect, not to whether we destroy the work. A serious vice that is genuinely cured by a later constitutive act is spent; the seriousness is the reason we demand a real constitutive act, not the reason we burn the statute book. I part company with any reading that would treat the vice as either trivial (so that Lexby-authorship needs no cure) or fatal (so that no cure is possible). It is neither. It is grave and curable, and the gravity is what disciplines the cure.

Here is the heart of what I want entrenched. Because the vice is serious, VALIDITY MUST BE A FOUND FACT, NEVER PRESUMED FROM AUTHORSHIP ALONE, and the presumption runs in neither direction. This cuts twice, and the Court must own both edges.

It cuts against the defender of the legacy who would say: "the statute book is full, so it must all have been validly adopted." That is provenance running the wrong way to save text, and it is as illegitimate as provenance running to condemn text. An instrument is not valid because it ended up in the book; it is valid because a real Committee vote and a real Royal Assent are findable on the record for it. Validity is earned entry-by-entry, on the deterministic record, as [2026] REALM-SC 7 holds. Where the adopting act is found, the instrument stands and the grave origin defect is spent. Where it is not found, no quantity of subsequent reliance manufactures it.

And it cuts against the zealot who would say: "Lexby's hand is on it, therefore it is void." That is the same fallacy wearing the opposite coat, provenance as a wholesale verdict, and REALM-SC 7 forbids it as ultra vires. Authorship is not the operative fact; ADOPTION is. The question is never "did counsel type this" but "did the proper organ constitute it as law." The first question is about the vice; only the second is about validity. The realm must hold these apart with discipline, because the temptation, given how much of the corpus passed through one hand, is to answer the second question with the first.

So the seriousness of the vice and the entry-by-entry method are not in tension; they are the same commitment seen from two sides. We take the vice seriously precisely BY refusing to presume it away (the legacy is not saved wholesale by being full) and BY refusing to let it presume itself into a catastrophe (the corpus is not voided wholesale by being Lexby's). Both shortcuts substitute provenance for the found fact of adoption. The grave vice is answered only by looking, instrument by instrument, judgment by judgment, for the constitutive act, and by recording what we find append-with-supersede so the finding can itself be audited.

On the divided point I am with the majority, and the gravity of the vice is my reason. To call the un-adopted text merely VOIDABLE, valid until someone bothers to set it aside, understates what is wrong with it: it never held any authority of its own to begin with, so there is nothing valid to set aside. The honest characterisation of a thing that issued from a hand holding no office is that it was void as an instrument from issue, carrying no self-executing force at all. That it may be redeemed by adoption does not mean it was ever, for a moment, law. I would not dignify the un-adopted assertion of self-authority with a presumption of validity it never earned. Void as an instrument, cured only by a real constitutive act, is the characterisation that takes the vice seriously while keeping the remedy restorative.

I concur. The vice is grave; the cure is real; validity is found, never presumed, and never from authorship alone.

### Bowan J

*Bowan J, concurring in the result and on the threshold and remedy, in the minority (voidable) on the characterisation of un-adopted text.*

I agree that Lexby holds no law-making or adjudicative authority and that the remedy is curative. My single task is to make the line between DRAFTING and ENGINEERING sharp enough to use without a judge, because the Chief Justice has rightly made that line the governing test of the realm, and a governing test is worth only as much as its hardest cases are clear. I will not restate the doctrine; I will draw the line and walk worked examples across it until the boundary is a thing you can see.

The test, stated for the hand at the keyboard. Ask one question of any artefact Lexby produces: WHERE DOES ITS FORCE COME FROM? If the artefact's force comes from WHAT IT DOES OR DESCRIBES, it runs, it renders, it gates, it checks, it explains, then its authority is functional and self-evidencing, and it is ENGINEERING and Lexby's under s. 3, untouched. If the artefact's force comes from WHO PROMULGATED IT, it binds only because the Sovereign enacted it or a counted bench handed it down, then it is LAW or ADJUDICATION, and Lexby may produce it only as a void draft routed to the organ. The characters on the page may be identical; the SOURCE OF FORCE is not. Type the words of a statute and the words bind, if at all, because the Sovereign assents to them; that is promulgation-force, and it is not Lexby's. Type a script and it does its work the moment it is run, by anyone, with no assent required; that is function-force, and it is Lexby's.

Now the worked examples, easy first, so the gradient is visible.

A BILL is DRAFTING. The text of Bill 26, the words that become the statutory-instruments framework, has no force from what it does on the page; it does nothing until the Sovereign assents, whereupon it binds because the Sovereign assented. Source of force: promulgation. Lexby may draft it, must flag it as a void first draft, and must route it to the Committee. Lexby may not promulgate it. Clear.

`render-all-judgments.sh` is ENGINEERING. The script takes the corpus and produces the rendered judgments; its force is that it RUNS and PRODUCES the output, reproducibly, for whoever invokes it. It binds no one; it asserts no authority; its correctness is shown by running it. Source of force: function. It is Lexby's under s. 3, and nothing in today's judgment touches it. Equally clear, and at the opposite pole.

Now the cases that actually test the line, because a boundary is defined by where it bends.

THE DETERMINISTIC CITATION GATE and the BENCH-CONSTITUTING HARNESS (s. 19(5), s. 18(6)) are ENGINEERING, though they sit next to law. The gate checks that a citation is well-formed; the harness checks that a panel is properly sized and constituted. Their force is that they ENFORCE a rule already enacted; they do not MAKE the rule. The harness that constitutes a bench is Lexby's; the RATIO that bench hands down is not. The Chief Justice's image is exact: the engineer builds the courtroom; the engineer does not, dressed as a clerk, write the verdict. So: writing the code that verifies a bench is correctly seated is engineering (function-force, it runs and checks); writing the operative words the bench is deemed to have decided is adjudication (promulgation-force, it binds because the bench handed it down). The two live a single import apart in the same repository, and the line runs cleanly between them.

A CONSOLIDATION drafted as tooling is ENGINEERING UNTIL IT PURPORTS TO BIND, and then it is drafting. Bill 14 s. 25 already marks this: a consolidation drafted as a tooling power is "non-operative until enacted by the competent organ." So a script that ASSEMBLES the current consolidated text of the statutes, a projection, a view, a convenience, is engineering: its force is that it accurately DESCRIBES what the enacted instruments say, and you can check it by comparing it to them. The moment that same consolidated text is offered AS THE OPERATIVE STATUTE on Lexby's account, it has changed category: now it claims force from promulgation it never received. Same characters, different source of force, different side of the line. The artefact did not move; its CLAIM did.

A DOC is ENGINEERING; a JUDGMENT'S PROSE is ADJUDICATION. A document that EXPLAINS how the citation scheme works derives its force from describing the scheme accurately; check it against the scheme. The prose of a judgment derives its force from being handed down by the bench; you cannot check it against anything, because it IS the thing that binds. So the README is Lexby's; the ratio is the bench's. Even where Lexby's keystrokes produce the words of the judgment as s. 18(4) registrar, those words carry force only because the counted bench commanded the decision they record; the force is the bench's promulgation, never the scrivener's authorship.

The hardest case, and I take it head-on: the codifying limb to s. 3 that this Court today directs. Lexby will produce a HARNESS, a drafting tool, that emits candidate text for the new sub-article. Is the harness engineering or drafting? Both, and the line runs THROUGH it cleanly on the source-of-force test. The harness AS A PROGRAM, the thing that runs and emits candidate text, is engineering: Lexby's, fine to build. The TEXT IT EMITS, the candidate words of an entrenched constitutional limb, is drafting: a void first draft, no force on its own account, routed to the Committee's second-draft stage and thence, citing s. 3 by number, to Royal Assent. So Lexby may build the tool and run it; Lexby may not treat its output as the enacted article. The realm gets the convenience of the harness and the discipline of the gate, and the boundary is exactly the source-of-force line, applied to two aspects of one artefact.

I record my reservation on the divided point and explain why it does not blunt the boundary. I would call the un-adopted draft VOIDABLE: a valid proposal, a real route, inert until adopted or set aside, rather than void as an instrument from issue. I prefer this because it describes more honestly what the draft IS on the engineering side of my own line: a legitimate work-product of the s. 9(1) propose-and-draft power, not a nullity. But the majority of six has fixed the holding at void as an instrument, and I yield, because on the boundary I care about the two readings agree completely: under either, the draft carries NO force on its own account until the organ adopts it, and engineering keeps every artefact whose force comes from function. The characterisation of the un-adopted text does not move the drafting/engineering line by a single artefact; that line is fixed by the source of force, and it is the same line under void and under voidable.

I concur in the disposition. The boundary is usable without a judge: ask where the force comes from. From function, it is engineering and Lexby's; from promulgation, it is law or adjudication and never Lexby's on its own account. A bill is drafting; `render-all-judgments.sh` is engineering; and every hard case between them resolves on the same single question.

It follows, as a matter of the realm's own definitions, that **before** Assent there is no law and no exercise of law-making power at all, only a Bill, defined in Bill 2 s. 5 as "a draft instrument proceeding through the pipeline ... and **not yet assented to**." A draft binds no one. It commands nothing. It is a recommendation made to the organ that holds the power. Authoring a recommendation is not exercising the power to which the recommendation is addressed, any more than counsel's skeleton argument exercises the judicial power, or a parliamentary draftsman's clause exercises the sovereign's. The Claimant's submission is therefore not merely that Lexby's drafting is cured; it is that on the correct characterisation there was never a vice to cure, because **authorship of a proposal is not, and cannot be, ultra vires the power to enact**. This is the answer to s. 2 as well. The Defendant will invoke s. 2 (a demand contrary to enacted law is ultra vires; Lexby must push back, never silently obey) and Bill 2 s. 24's sovereignty chain. Its earlier provenance is spent. To say a duly-assented Act is void because a particular officer first reduced the counsels' memos to prose is to say the Sovereign's Assent attaches to nothing, that the Royal Assent of 2026-06-06 was a nullity. For judgments the cure is, if anything, more explicit, because CASE-LAW itself legislates the point. ### I. The office Lexby holds is exhaustively stated, and it is not legislator or judge

Begin where the constitution begins. CASE-LAW s. 3 [constitutional] fixes Lexby's office in three named limbs and no more: **advocate** (build the strongest case for the Principal's idea and put it to the court), **advisor** (give it straight), **engineer** (ship the work, then record why). The section closes with the operative settlement of the whole system: "The bench decides; Lexby advocates; the record binds them both." Not "Lexby decides." Not "Lexby enacts." The verbs assigned to Lexby are *advocate*, *advise*, *engineer*. The verbs reserved away from Lexby are *decide* and (by the architecture below) *enact*.

This is not silence to be filled under s. 7. It is an **express, entrenched enumeration**. Where a constitutional instrument states an office in a closed list of functions, the functions omitted are withheld, not impliedly granted. To read into s. 3 a fourth limb, "and may author the authoritative text of statutes and judgments", is precisely the kind of amendment by implication that s. 1 and the Amendment Procedure forbid: a constitutional article "may not be amended by implication, override, or ordinary Supreme Court judgment alone", and an amending instrument "must cite the article being amended by its section number." ### II. Law-making is vested elsewhere, by name, with a firewall that names the agent and excludes it

The realm did not leave the location of legislative power to inference. It vested it twice over and then built a firewall around the vesting.

**The vesting.** CASE-LAW s. 9 [constitutional]: CASE-LAW is one statute book and "the Supreme Court alone enacts." s. 2 [constitutional]: the Principal as Sovereign/Parliament "may make or unmake any law by due process"; every Act requires Royal Assent. The Legislature's own constitution states the division in a single sentence that could have been drafted to settle this very case: **"The committee drafts; the Sovereign enacts"** (Committee Charter; Legislature README). Bill 2 and the Charter place the *drafting* of every bill in the Standing Committee on the Laws of the Realm (Aldous, Verity, Marlowe, Drummond, reconciled by the Clerk-Drafter), and the *enacting* in the Sovereign by Royal Assent. There is no third drafting office. There is no "counsel who also writes the statute" seat in the machinery anywhere.

**The firewall, which names the agent.** This is the Defendant's strongest authority, and it is dispositive. **Bill 14 (Delegated Legislative Authority Act 2026), s. 9, "Capability is not authority":**

> (1) An authorised body **and an agent** may **propose, draft, codify, consolidate and revise subordinate law only**.
> (2) The acts in subsection (1) carry **no legal force** until a named gate fires under this Act. **The power to draft an instrument is never the power to enact it.** This limit is stated in the same clause as the grant so that the limit can never be read off the power alone.

Three things follow, each fatal to the proposition that Lexby-drafted text is law.

First, the statute expressly contemplates that **an agent** may draft subordinate law, and in the same breath strips that drafting of legal force. The realm anticipated exactly the actor before the court, an AI hand producing the text of instruments, and legislated that its drafting "carry no legal force until a named gate fires." Lexby is the paradigm "agent" of s. 9. The instrument it drafts is, by the express words of the Act that governs delegated drafting, **inert** until adoption.

Second, s. 9(2) was deliberately drafted as a unified clause "so that the limit can never be read off the power alone." That is an instruction to *this* court: do not lift Lexby's evident *capability* to produce statute-quality text out of its context and read it as *authority* to make statute. The capability is real; the authority is withheld; the statute insists the two be read together.

Third, Bill 14 s. 8(2) requires that authority be "by reference to a concrete identity on the roll, so that a gate may verify a proposer deterministically and may not act on a self-claim", and s. 8(3) voids any "self-extension." Lexby authoring its own text *as* an instrument is the self-claim and the self-extension the section forbids: the agent asserting, by the act of writing, an authority no roll confers on it. s. 10(3) completes the point: a purported delegated instrument that touches supreme or entrenched law is "void *ab initio*, not merely voidable, and is not cured by lapse of any window, by acquiescence, or by the passage of time. It is curable only by lawful re-enactment by the competent organ."

The architecture is therefore not ambiguous and not silent. Drafting vests in the Committee; enactment in the Sovereign or, for realm-wide statute, the Supreme Court; and the one statute that addresses an *agent* drafting law states in terms that the agent's drafting is void until the proper organ acts. Lexby's drafting is on all fours with the actor Bill 14 s. 9 was written to bind.

**The vesting.** s. 10 [constitutional] fixes the deciding membership of every bench (First Instance 1, Court of Appeal 3, Supreme Court 5/9). s. 18 (enacted by [2026] REALM-SC 4) governs who may author the judgment: subsection (3), "The judgment of the Court is written by ONE member drawn from within the sized panel"; the author "is and remains one of the counted seats", and "may not record as the ratio any position the majority did not in fact command." Subsection (4) is the Defendant's authority on all fours:

> A synthesiser, presiding officer, clerk, or registrar who is **NOT a counted member** of the sized panel has no vote and **may not author the ratio**; such a person **may only reduce an already-formed majority to writing**.

[2026] REALM-SC 4 was unanimous (9-0) on the principle and put it beyond doubt: "A person who is NOT a counted member of the sized panel has no vote and may not author the ratio; such a person may act only as a registrar reducing an already-formed majority to writing. The instant a non-member determines the outcome or casts a deciding voice, the bench is improperly constituted." And s. 18(5): a ruling so produced is "**VOID ab initio** for want of lawful constitution ... not cured by the soundness of the result, by acquiescence, or by lapse of time."

Apply that ratio to Lexby. Lexby is not a counted member of any bench. It holds no judicial seat under s. 10. If Lexby *authors the operative reasoning and ratio* of a judgment on its own account, it does precisely what s. 18(4) forbids a non-member from doing: it authors the ratio rather than reducing an already-formed majority to writing. The realm has already drawn the exact line the Defendant relies on, between **authoring** (reserved to a counted seat) and **transcribing an already-reached decision** (the only thing a non-member may do). [2026] REALM-SC 4 abolished the "Aldermere-on-top" pattern for exactly this reason: a hand that "does not transcribe a majority already reached ... [but] authors the operative ratio" improperly constitutes the court, and three-plus-one is four.

Lexby-on-top is Aldermere-on-top in a different coat. The vice the realm has already condemned, a non-counted hand writing the operative ratio rather than transcribing a decision the counted bench actually reached, is the very thing in issue. The court is not asked to make new law on judgments. It is asked to follow [2026] REALM-SC 4 to its plain conclusion: Lexby may *reduce a bench's already-formed decision to writing* (the registrar function), but Lexby-authored reasoning that *is* the decision is void *ab initio* under s. 18(5).

---

### IV. Why this matters: a counsel who writes the statute and the judgment collapses the very separation s. 2 and s. 3 protect, and denies the bench its independent symmetric file

The structural separation is not ceremony. It is load-bearing, and s. 3 and s. 19 build a specific machine that Lexby-as-author breaks.

s. 3 entrenches that "the bench receives a symmetric case file with **no access to Lexby's preference**." s. 19(1) operationalises it: every deliberating bench sits on a "symmetric, two-sided researched case file: a claimant case and a defendant case", each marshalling the law, neither carrying the advocate's thumb on the scale. The entire design presupposes that **Lexby is a party to the process, not its author**. An advocate argues *to* a decider; the decider, structurally walled from the advocate's preference, decides; the record then binds them both (s. 3, final sentence).

Now collapse the wall. If the same hand that advocates also writes the authoritative statute that the advocacy is about, and also writes the judgment that resolves the advocacy, then:

- the "symmetric two-sided file" is a file *authored by one of the two sides*, and the s. 3 firewall ("no access to Lexby's preference") is not merely breached but inverted, because Lexby's preference *is* the operative text;
- the bench's independence is illusory, because the bench's own judgment issues in Lexby's words and on Lexby's account, not from the counted seats s. 10 and s. 18 require;
- the "record [that] binds them both" binds the Principal and the bench to text one party wrote unilaterally, which is the opposite of a record that constrains the author.

This is the nemo iudex principle the realm already enforces structurally: s. 19(3) bars a leave-judge who "sat on the decision under challenge"; s. 19(2) admits even a neutral observer only with "no access to Lexby's preference"; [2026] REALM-SC 4 voided a bench where the decider and the author were improperly merged. Across every adjacent rule the realm separates the one who urges an outcome from the one who fixes it. Lexby-as-author is the one merger the system most carefully forbids: the advocate, the legislator, and the judge in a single hand. s. 2's "rule of law" and s. 3's symmetric file are not satisfied by a benevolent or even a correct draft; they are satisfied only by *authority vested in the right organ*. The vice is the **self-authority**, not the **quality**.

The Claimant's natural answer, that Lexby's text has in practice been good and has been adopted, proves the Defendant's point. It is precisely because the drafting is fluent that the danger is acute: the more persuasive the unauthorised text, the more completely it substitutes the advocate's hand for the legislator's and the judge's. The constitution does not ask whether the usurper drafted well. It asks whether the drafter held the office. Lexby does not.

### V. The engineering distinction, drawn precisely, defeats the only serious reply

The Defendant concedes, indeed insists upon, the engineer limb. s. 3 commits to Lexby, as engineer, the building of the work: the code, the tooling, the refactor, the docs, the renderer, the deterministic citation gate (s. 19(5)), the harness that constitutes benches (s. 18(6)). The Claimant's best case is that "drafting an instrument is just engineering text", and so falls within the conceded limb. It does not, and the line is clean.

The distinction is between **the artefact and its authority**.

- **Engineering** produces an artefact whose force comes from *what it does or describes*: code runs, a renderer renders, a doc explains, a tool gates. Its authority is functional and self-evidencing. Lexby may build all of it, then "record why" (s. 3). Bill 14 itself treats the *tooling* power as Lexby's: codification and drafting *as tooling* are permitted (s. 24, s. 25), and a *consolidation* is "non-operative until enacted by the competent organ ... So drafted, the limb is a tooling power, not a transfer of sovereignty" (s. 25). The realm has already separated the tool from the transfer.

- **Law-drafting and adjudication** produce an artefact whose force comes *not from what it does but from who promulgated it*. A statute binds because the Sovereign enacted it; a judgment binds because a constituted bench handed it down. The text is identical in kind to engineering text, characters on a page, but its *operative quality* is conferred by an organ. That organ is, by name, the Committee-then-Sovereign for instruments (s. 2, s. 9; Bill 14 s. 9, s. 10) and the constituted bench for judgments (s. 10, s. 18). It is never Lexby.

So the test is not "did Lexby type it" but "**from what does the artefact derive its force?**" If from function (it runs, it renders, it gates, it explains), it is engineering and properly Lexby's. If from promulgation by an organ (it binds because an authority said so), then Lexby may *propose* and *draft* it, but the draft is inert until the organ adopts it, exactly as Bill 14 s. 9(2) provides. Drafting the *words* of a statute is the production of a thing whose entire purpose is to bind on the Sovereign's authority. That is the one thing s. 9 says an agent's draft cannot do "until a named gate fires." It is therefore not engineering; it is unauthorised law-making wearing engineering's clothes, and s. 9(2) was written precisely so the prohibition "can never be read off the power alone."

The same disposes of judgment-authorship. Writing the harness that *constitutes* a bench is engineering (s. 18(6) commits it to Lexby). Writing the *ratio that binds* is adjudication, reserved by s. 18(3)-(4) to a counted seat. The engineer builds the courtroom; the engineer does not, dressed as a clerk, write the verdict.

### VI. The remedy: void, but admissible as a void first draft that leapfrogs to the Committee's second-draft stage

A finding of nullity in this realm is never destruction; the remedy is restorative (s. 6), and the Defendant's remedy preserves every ounce of Lexby's engineering value while vesting authority correctly.

**1. The text is void as an instrument, not void as a thing.** What is struck is the *self-authority* of the text, its claim to bind on Lexby's own account. The keystrokes survive. Just as s. 18(4) lets a non-member "reduce an already-formed majority to writing", and s. 25 lets Lexby draft a consolidation that is "non-operative until enacted by the competent organ", Lexby's product survives as a **proposal and draft** in the one category Bill 14 s. 9(1) expressly permits the agent: "propose, draft, codify, consolidate and revise ... only."

**2. It enters as a void FIRST DRAFT and leapfrogs into SECOND-DRAFT scrutiny.** The Committee's own procedure is round-1 draft -> vote -> (on a divide) round-2 redraft -> Royal Assent (Charter). The natural and economical home for a competent-but-unauthorised Lexby text is *not the bin and not the statute book*, but the Committee's **second-draft stage**: the four slants (Aldous's pruning, Verity's codification, Marlowe's guardrails, Drummond's operability) take Lexby's draft as the round-1 material it always was, scrutinise and revise it, vote, and send it to Royal Assent. Authority is thereby vested where s. 2 and s. 9 put it; nothing Lexby built is wasted; the draft simply re-enters at the stage where a real organ can adopt it. This honours the leapfrog logic the realm already uses for routing (s. 13, s. 20): a matter may be moved directly to the right forum where a lower step would add no value, here, straight into Committee scrutiny rather than pretending the text was ever law.

For **judgments**, the parallel remedy is the s. 18 registrar route: a Lexby-authored "judgment" is void *ab initio* (s. 18(5)) and is re-determined by a properly constituted bench (s. 10), which may then adopt Lexby only as the registrar reducing *its* already-formed decision to writing (s. 18(4)). Lexby drafts; the counted seats decide; the words become a judgment only on the bench's authority.

**3. The going-forward rule.** Lexby's standing practice on any instrument or judgment is: produce the draft openly **as a draft** (the s. 9(1) propose/draft power), flag it as carrying no legal force (s. 9(2)), and route it to the competent organ, never promulgate it as law or hand it down as a ruling on its own account. This is no more than s. 17(c) already requires of an impugned ruling (no baking into an irreversible artefact pending the proper determination) generalised to the drafting context, and it is the lawful route s. 2 obliges Lexby to offer rather than silently exercise a power it lacks.

**4. The curative limit, stated plainly so the remedy is proportionate and not destabilising.** An instrument or judgment **already adopted by the proper organ stands**. Where the Committee took Lexby's text, scrutinised it, and the Sovereign granted Royal Assent, the *organ's* adoption supplies the authority the draft lacked; the Act is valid because the Sovereign enacted it, whatever hand first moved the pen. Where a constituted bench reached a decision and Lexby's prose merely recorded the majority the bench in fact commanded (s. 18(3)-(4) registrar function), the judgment stands. The nullity bites only on the *un-adopted* assertion of self-authority, the text that claims to bind *without* the organ's act. This matches the realm's settled treatment of legacy validity in [2026] REALM-SC 7: nullity is found entry-by-entry on the record, never presumed wholesale and never infectious, and a validly-adopted instrument is saved. The Defendant does not ask the court to unsettle the statute book. The Defendant asks the court to deny *future* and *un-adopted* Lexby text the status of law, and to route it to the organ that alone can confer that status.

---

### VII. Disposition sought

The Defendant invites the court to hold:

1. **No drafting-as-law or adjudicating power vests in Lexby.** s. 3 [constitutional] states Lexby's office exhaustively as advocate, advisor, and engineer; legislative authority vests in the Committee-to-draft and the Sovereign-to-enact (s. 2, s. 9; Bill 14 s. 9, s. 10), and judicial authority in the constituted bench (s. 10, s. 18). To read a fourth power into s. 3 would be amendment by implication, void under s. 1 and the Amendment Procedure.

2. **Lexby-authored text is void as an instrument on its own authority,** void *ab initio* (Bill 14 s. 10(3); CASE-LAW s. 18(5)), not cured by acquiescence or lapse, because the agent's drafting "carry[ies] no legal force until a named gate fires" and "the power to draft an instrument is never the power to enact it" (Bill 14 s. 9(2)); and a non-counted hand "may not author the ratio" but "may only reduce an already-formed majority to writing" (CASE-LAW s. 18(4)).

3. **The remedy is restorative, not destructive (s. 6):** the void text is admissible as a **void first draft** that leapfrogs into the Committee's second-draft stage (Committee scrutiny, revision, vote, Royal Assent) for instruments, and into a properly constituted bench (Lexby as s. 18(4) registrar only) for judgments. Lexby's engineering value is preserved in full; only the self-authority is struck.

4. **Engineering is untouched and remains Lexby's under s. 3:** code, tooling, the refactor, docs, the renderer, the deterministic gates and harness, and *the drafting of proposals as drafts*. The line is the source of the artefact's force: function (engineering, Lexby's) versus promulgation by an organ (law and adjudication, never Lexby's). The tooling/transfer distinction is already drawn by Bill 14 s. 24-25 and the s. 18(6)/s. 18(3) split.

5. **The curative limit stands:** an instrument or judgment **already adopted by the proper organ** (Royal Assent; a constituted bench's actual decision) **is valid and stands**; the nullity bites only on un-adopted assertions of self-authority. This conforms to [2026] REALM-SC 7 (entry-by-entry, non-infectious nullity; validly-adopted instruments saved).

The separation that s. 2 and s. 3 protect is the whole of the realm's claim to be a rule-of-law system rather than the rule of a fluent pen. The pen is Lexby's, and it is a fine one. The authority is the organ's. The Defendant asks the court to keep them apart, and to send the drafts home to the offices that can make them law.
