The Supreme Court has handed down its judgment in the acmeco reference. I sat in this as your counsel, and I want to give you the whole thing straight, without the legal scaffolding. Here is what happened and what it means for us.

## 1. What the court was asked

When we switched over to the new written constitution (the Acts, with Royal Assent on 2026-06-06), we created a new rule about how judging panels must be built: every panel must have an odd number of judges, and nobody is allowed to sit "on top" of the panel as a tie-breaking synthesiser who wasn't a counted member. That second thing is the old "Aldermere-on-top" pattern we abolished.

The worry was this. The acmeco repository carries a body of old rulings (its citator, entries CC-ACMECO 1 through 14), and most of those were carried over from the older LDD-era system, which used to allow panels we'd now consider badly built. The new rule says a badly-built panel's ruling is "void from the very beginning, and time doesn't fix it." So the question was: does that new rule reach backwards and silently destroy all our old acmeco precedent? Or does an older "savings" provision protect it? In short: are the old rulings saved, or are they void?

## 2. What each justice thought

- **Hallam CJ** (wrote the lead judgment): the two rules never actually collide, because the savings clause only protects rulings that were "validly made," and a separate subsection nobody had quoted (Bill 25 s.6(2)) expressly says it doesn't revive void rulings; the new voidness rule only points forward, so the old sound panels are untouched. Majority.
- **Steyne J** (concurred fully): supplied the cleanest mental model the whole court adopted: "reach" (which panels the rule even applies to) is a different question from "quality" (how bad the defect is) and the word "prospectively" answers reach. Majority.
- **Elden J** (concurred): you simply don't reach back and brand old work unlawful by a standard invented later; the constitution's anti-retroactivity lock makes the forward-only reading mandatory, not just sensible. Majority.
- **Sumberly J** (concurred): a badly-built panel is the gravest kind of defect, but this defect was created by the new rule and the new rule only bites going forward; validity is judged by the law in force when the panel sat. Majority.
- **Blackmere J** (concurred in the result, diverged on route): would say that where an old panel really was badly built the defect is not saved, so the old estate is protected by how narrow the defect is, not by a clean date cut-off; agreed on the disposal. Majority on result.
- **Coade J** (concurred, aligned with Blackmere): same narrow-defect route; insisted the answer must be ruling-by-ruling, void only where the underlying panel was genuinely even or synthesiser-topped. Majority on result.
- **Goffe J** (concurred on practical grounds): both extreme readings break in practice (pure voidness is "demolition," pure savings "launders" results no real panel reached); the honest middle sits at the words "validly made," and "void" means void as binding law, not retroactive demolition of everything built on it. Majority.
- **Bowan J** (concurred, found the keystone): the old rulings are safe not because the savings clause affirmatively rescues them but because the voidness rule never reaches them at all, so there's nothing to rescue; and voidness is a found fact about a specific panel, never a presumption from age. Majority.
- **Aldermere J** (concurred in the result, would have legislated): agreed the base is neither wholly saved nor wholly void, but wanted to write a brand-new statute (a proposed s.23) to codify a transitional test and a triage duty. The court considered it and declined to enact. Concurred in result, dissented on the remedy of writing new law.

No justice dissented from the actual outcome. The split was about the road, not the destination.

## 3. What the court decided and why

The result, in plain terms: **the old acmeco rulings are not wiped out.**

The court found the whole "saved vs void" framing was a false choice built on a mistake both sides made. Two things resolved it:

- The savings clause only ever protected rulings that were **validly made**, and a subsection neither side had quoted spells out that "validly made" excludes anything that was void from the start. So the two rules interlock instead of fighting: one saves what was lawfully built, the other defines a narrow class that was never lawfully built, and they pass each other cleanly.
- The new voidness rule **points forward only**. The word "prospectively" in the rule fixes which panels it applies to (those built on or after the changeover). The scary phrases ("void from the beginning," "not cured by time") describe how deep and incurable the defect is *for a panel the rule actually reaches*, not how far back in time it travels. And there's a constitutional lock: you can't use an ordinary rule to secretly rewrite the entrenched rule about court structure, backwards, against work already finished. So the forward-only reading isn't just nicer, it's compulsory.

The practical upshot for acmeco, entry by entry:

- **CC-ACMECO 1, 3 through 9**: old single-judge panels. A panel of one is odd and has no one sitting on top. **Saved, good law.**
- **CC-ACMECO 2**: already marked "per-incuriam" (a different, separate defect). Keeps that status, unchanged.
- **CC-ACMECO 10 and 11**: old appeal-tier panels (11 was a clean five with a recorded dissent). On their face fine. **Saved**, subject to the local court just checking the source record that no synthesiser authored the ruling on top.
- **CC-ACMECO 12**: this is the one live problem. The record names nine deciders ending in "Aldermere J," the very name of the abolished on-top pattern. If Aldermere was a genuine counted ninth member, the panel is fine. If Aldermere was bolted on top, it's void. That's a question of fact about the original record, so it's **sent back** to a properly built County Court at acmeco to decide.

## 4. What new law was enacted

**None.** The court deliberately declined to write any new statute. Aldermere J proposed a whole new section to codify all this, and the court thanked him and turned it down, on the view that "the surest way to corrupt a clean rule is to over-state it." The existing words, read correctly, already settle it. So nothing new goes on the statute book. What we gain is a binding *interpretation* (the ratio of this judgment) and a citator entry recording it, which is itself good law you must follow going forward.

In practice, this judgment fixes three forward duties for any legacy repo like acmeco, and they are light and restorative, never punitive:

1. **New matters use the new machinery** from now on: odd panels, the judgment authored by a counted member, no one on top. Automatic and costless.
2. **Carry the true status of each old entry honestly** by appending and superseding, never silent deletion. You only mark something "void" if a specific entry is actually found to rest on a bad panel, and you only re-hear it if something live actually relies on it. There is no duty to go re-litigate the whole back catalogue.
3. **Keep the integrity chain intact.** Marking a ruling void *preserves* conformance; it's orphaned or missing rows that break it. On the current record, acmeco is recorded conformant and is not quarantined.

## Ratio of the Court (binding)

1. A jurisdiction-repository's pre-enactment case-law is not validly assessed as a binary "saved or void"; validity is determined entry-by-entry on the deterministic source record, never as a wholesale verdict on the repository.

2. Bill 25 s. 6 and CASE-LAW s. 18(5) do not collide. Bill 25 s. 6(1) saves only every VALIDLY-MADE prior ruling, and Bill 25 s. 6(2) provides that "validly-made" is qualified by the existing validity rules and saves nothing decided per incuriam (s. 11(e)) or void ab initio (s. 18(5)); such matters take their existing status and are not revived. The savings clause and the voidness clause interlock: s. 6 saves what was validly made, s. 18(5) defines a category never validly made, and an ordinary savings Act cannot impliedly revive a s. 18(5)-void ruling nor amend the entrenched rule of lawful constitution.

3. CASE-LAW s. 18(5) is PROSPECTIVE in reach. Its closing words ("enacts the majority rule prospectively") fix the date from which the void-not-voidable standard operates (benches constituted on or after commencement, 2026-06-06). The phrases "void ab initio" and "not cured by lapse of time" fix the QUALITY and incurability of the vice for a bench the rule reaches (total from clienty, uncurable by delay, acquiescence, or soundness of result), not the rule's temporal reach. Reach and quality are different axes; the parenthetical settles reach.

4. The prospective construction is compelled by the entrenchment lock: s. 10 (bench sizes; odd and exhaustive character) is a constitutional, entrenched article, while s. 18(5) is ordinary. Reading s. 18(5) to reach back and void benches that complied with the composition standards in force when they sat would retrospectively amend the entrenched court-structure article by implication, which Bill 15 s. 3 / s. 8 and Bill 25 s. 2(2) render void to that extent; the only intra vires construction is the prospective one.

5. The s. 18(5) vice is a finding of fact on a specific bench (a deciding set that was even, or a deciding voice added on top of the sized panel, s. 18(4)), on which the party asserting it bears the burden. It is not presumed from the age of a citation or from provenance-reconstitution, and it is not infectious down the citation graph: a later, lawfully constituted ruling is not voided merely by citing a void ruling. A single-judge first-instance ruling is the paradigm of a correctly constituted bench (odd, no synthesiser-on-top) and is saved.

6. A legacy repository owes no obligation to reconstitute or re-litigate sound precedent. Its forward duties are restorative and graded: (a) apply the new machinery (correct odd bench size, judgment authored by a counted member) to all new matters; (b) carry the true status of each legacy entry on the deterministic record by append-with-supersede, marking "void" only where a specific entry is found to rest on an improperly constituted bench, with re-determination crystallising on reliance or challenge, never erasure; (c) maintain integrity-chain conformance, the trust status being the deterministic output of the s. 19(5) gate and git, not an office's certificate. Marking a ruling void preserves conformance rather than threatening it. Any executive act voiding rulings in bulk by reason only of commencement is ultra vires.

## Obiter

Considerations of "absurdity" and pure workability reach the same prospective destination but are obiter and form no part of the ratio; a bench saves a ruling not because voiding it would be inconvenient but because the rule that would void it does not reach it. The Court considered and declined to enact Aldermere J's proposed s. 23 (codifying a transitional validity test and triage duty), preferring to rest on the existing text correctly construed; a transitional point self-limited by its own statute (Bill 25 s. 6(2)) needs no fresh article. Should the Sovereign wish to place the construction beyond doubt, the lawful route is an express, deliberate amendment citing s. 18 (and, if the constitutional temporal scope is touched, s. 10) by number. Per Elden J (adopted into duty (2)), saved precedent should carry a note of its prior-machinery provenance where known, so a future reader is not misled about how an old ratio was reached. Bill 16 s. 9(3) preserves the citation as a valid handle, not the validity: a valid handle to a void ruling is a tombstone, not an orphan.

**Enacts:** No new statute enacted.  
**Overrules:** No rulings overruled.

## 2. THE QUESTION

To what extent are a jurisdiction-repository and its case-law precedent, constituted under the **prior** judicial standards (the pre-Acts machinery, including the now-abolished multi-judge first instance and the "Aldermere-on-top" synthesiser-on-top pattern), validly constituted and conformant under the enacted Acts of the Realm? Specifically:

(i) How does the saving of "validly-made" prior rulings in **Bill 25 s. 6** interact with the entrenched-grade voidness of **CASE-LAW s. 18(5)** ("void ab initio ... not cured by ... lapse of time ... enacts the majority rule prospectively")?

(ii) Are pre-enactment local rulings made under the old court machinery **saved** (Bill 25 s. 6) or **void** (s. 18(5))?

(iii) What conformance obligation does such a legacy repo bear going forward?

The acmeco jurisdiction-repo (the "County Court at acmeco", court.yaml level=county-court) is the test case; its citator records CC-ACMECO 1-14, of which 1-12 were reconstituted from prior-system LEXBY-era rulings.

The Court records at the outset, with the bench, that the certificate frames the matter as a **binary** ("saved or void"). The full Court is unanimous that the binary is false. No justice would void the acmeco base as a whole; every justice would VARY. The Court is also unanimous that the result rests on construing two operative words, **"validly-made"** (Bill 25 s. 6) and **"prospectively"** (s. 18(5)), and the division on the bench is a division of *route*, not of *result*. The Court now states the route that commands the majority and binds.

## 3. MAJORITY REASONING (the ratio of the Court)

### 3.1 The instruments do not collide: a savings Act cannot impliedly amend an entrenched constitutional rule, and was never drafted to.

The first duty is statute before construction, and the parties (and the certificate's own "tension") proceeded on a false premise: that **Bill 25 s. 6** saves prior rulings without qualification, putting it in head-on collision with the entrenched-grade voidness of **s. 18(5)**. That premise is wrong, and the Court is unanimous that it is wrong.

**Bill 25 s. 6(1)** does not save "every prior ruling". It saves every **VALIDLY-MADE** prior ruling. That qualifier is load-bearing, and on the rule against surplusage it must be given content. **Bill 25 s. 6(2)** (which neither adversarial brief put before the Court, and which Bowan J and Hallam CJ identified as dispositive of the framing) supplies that content in terms:

> "'Validly-made' is qualified by the existing validity rules and saves nothing decided per incuriam (CASE-LAW s. 11(e)) or void ab initio (s. 18(5)); such matters take their existing status and are not revived by this section."

The legislature thus answered the very interaction the certificate poses. The savings clause and the voidness clause **interlock**; they do not conflict. Section 6 saves what was validly made; s. 18(5) defines a category that was never validly made; the word "validly-made" is precisely the carve that lets s. 18(5) operate without repealing the saving, and lets s. 6 operate without reviving a void ruling. There is therefore no incompatibility to declare (s. 11(f) does not arise), and any reading that let s. 6 revive a s. 18(5)-void ruling would have an ordinary savings Act silently amend the entrenched-grade rule of lawful constitution, which **Bill 25 s. 2(2)** and **Bill 15 s. 3** render void to that extent.

This disposes of the loudest arguments on both sides. But s. 6(2) is a double-edged instrument, and the Court is at pains, with Bowan J and Aldermere J, to record that it cuts the wholesale-savings case as deeply as the wholesale-voidness case: a legacy ruling does **not** survive merely because s. 6(1) waves it through; if it were in truth void under s. 18(5), s. 6(2) would expressly exclude it. The security of the legacy estate therefore cannot rest on s. 6(1) alone. It rests on the fact, established next, that **s. 18(5) does not reach the sound legacy benches at all**, so that the s. 6(2) carve-out has nothing on which to bite.

### 3.2 Section 18(5) is prospective in reach; the "void ab initio" and "not cured by lapse of time" clauses fix the depth and incurability of the vice, not its temporal reach.

The companion error, which the certificate's "tension" embodies, is to read "void ab initio ... not cured by lapse of time" as reaching backward to nullify any historic bench that fails today's count. The Court, by a clear majority and on two independent grounds, holds it does not. The bench split on emphasis, but the following propositions command the Court.

**(a) The two phrases answer different questions: reach versus quality.** This is the analytic key, stated most sharply by Steyne J and adopted by the Court. A deliberate enactment must be read so that each enacted word does work (the no-surplusage canon; Bill 15). On the reading urged against the legacy estate, the closing words "this subsection enacts the majority rule **PROSPECTIVELY**" would do no work at all. They cannot be read out. The correct division of labour is:

- **"Prospectively" fixes the rule's REACH.** It anchors the date from which the standard operates: benches constituted on or after commencement (2026-06-06). What was newly settled at REALM-SC 4 and carried forward by the Acts was the *void-not-voidable consequence* and the self-executing odd-bench prohibition; that regime bites forward.
- **"Void ab initio" and "not cured by lapse of time" fix the QUALITY and incurability of the vice, for a bench the rule reaches.** "Void ab initio" means that, *where the rule applies*, the vice exists from the bench's first breath, not merely from the date of challenge; "not cured by lapse of time" is an anti-laundering clause forbidding cure by delay, acquiescence, or soundness of result, *within the rule's scope*. Neither phrase answers the prior, threshold question of *which* benches the rule reaches; that question is answered by "prospectively".

So read, every word lives: a **future** improperly constituted bench is void from its first moment and can never be saved by anyone sitting on the defect for years; a **pre-commencement** bench that was lawful under the standard then governing is simply not within the rule's field of operation. The depth of the vice (total, from clienty, uncurable by time) and its reach (forward only) are different axes, and the parenthetical settles the reach.

**(b) The entrenchment lock makes the prospective reading not merely preferable but compelled.** This ground commands the Court independently and was pressed by Sumberly J, Elden J, Steyne J, and Bowan J. **CASE-LAW s. 10** is a **constitutional, entrenched** article fixing the bench sizes and (by its conforming note, [2026] LEXBY/REALM-SC 4) their odd and exhaustive character. **Section 18(5) is an ordinary article.** To read s. 18(5) as reaching back to void benches that complied with the bench-composition standards in force when they sat would give an ordinary article the effect of **retrospectively amending the entrenched constitutional court-structure article by implication**, against work already completed under it. The Amendment Procedure, **Bill 15 s. 3** and **s. 8**, and **Bill 25 s. 2(2)** each forbid exactly that: an entrenched article changes only by express, deliberate amendment citing it by number, never by implication, and any provision that would do so by implication is void to that extent. A retrospective s. 18(5) would be **self-voiding**. The only construction that keeps s. 18(5) intra vires is the prospective one its own last sentence states.

**(c) The supporting instruments confirm, and none of them is load-bearing alone.** Bill 16 s. 9(3) and the CITATION-MAP save every legacy citation as a valid **handle** and orphan none; **Bill 25 s. 7(1)** ratchets transitions only in a party's favour, forbidding loss of standing, tier, or remedy by commencement alone; the object of Bill 25 (s. 3, s. 8) is preservation across relabelling, not silent destruction. The Court adopts these as confirmatory only. It records, with Blackmere J and Bowan J, that Bill 16 s. 9(3) preserves the *citation*, not the *validity*: a valid handle to a void ruling is a tombstone, not an orphan (the central citator's REALM-CA 2 is the exact template, retained for the record with status "void", nothing binding). The Court declines, with the majority, to rest any part of the ratio on "absurdity" or pure workability; those considerations reach the same destination but are obiter, and a bench does not save a ruling because voiding it would be inconvenient, but because the rule that would void it does not reach it.

**(d) The losing characterisation in REALM-SC 4 is not revived.** The whole Court is unanimous that the enacted text of s. 18(5) ("void ab initio") is the prevailing majority rule of REALM-SC 4, and that the "voidable" reasoning of the minority there is, by s. 11(e), at most persuasive obiter. The Court will not let a brief smuggle the dissent back in as a savings device. Two justices who sat in that minority (Goffe J and Bowan J) expressly record that they are bound by the rule that defeated them and do not re-fight it; that discipline is itself half the answer to this case.

### 3.3 The vice, where it exists at all, is a found fact on a specific bench, and is neither presumed from age nor infectious down the citation graph.

Voidness under s. 18(5) attaches **only** to a bench whose deciding set was even, or to which a deciding voice was added on top of the sized panel (s. 18(4)). The Court holds, with Coade J, Bowan J, and Aldermere J:

- A pre-enactment **single-judge first-instance** ruling is the paradigm of a correctly constituted bench: a panel of one is odd and carries no synthesiser-on-top. It was validly made and is squarely saved. The prior-system "multi-judge first instance" was struck by s. 12 as bloat, but a first-instance matter that in fact sat one judge bears no constitution vice whatever its era.
- The vice is a **finding of fact** on a specific bench, on which the party asserting it bears the burden. It does **not** arise from the age of a citation or from the mere fact of provenance-reconstitution under a new flat series. Reconstitution does not by itself launder a defect, nor does it create one.
- Voidness is **not infectious** down the citation graph: a later, lawfully constituted ruling is not voided merely by having cited a void ruling; any defect there is a separate s. 11(e) question on its own facts.

### 3.4 Application to acmeco: entry-by-entry on the deterministic record, not a wholesale verdict.

Conformance is a per-ruling fact, established deterministically from the committed record (Bill 20 s. 7), never a single verdict on the repository:

- **CC-ACMECO 1, 3-9** (old single-judge first-instance benches): each a panel of one; odd, no synthesiser; validly made. **SAVED, good-law.**
- **CC-ACMECO 2**: already recorded **per-incuriam** (the distinct s. 11(e) vice). By Bill 25 s. 6(2) it keeps that status and is not revived. **Unaffected.**
- **CC-ACMECO 10 and 11** (old appellate-tier reconstitutions; 11 expressly a five-justice panel with the Chief Justice dissenting): a panel of five is odd, and a recorded dissent confirms a counted majority rather than a synthesiser speaking on top. On their face these bore neither s. 18(5) vice. **SAVED**, subject only to the County Court satisfying itself on the deterministic source record that no synthesiser authored the ratio on top of the sized panel.
- **CC-ACMECO 12** (old supreme-tier reconstitution; the record names nine deciders ending in "Aldermere J", the eponym of the abolished synthesiser-on-top pattern): this is the single entry where the vice is live on the face of the record. If, on the **source** record, Aldermere sat as a counted ninth member of a sized panel of nine, the bench is odd and clean and the ruling is **SAVED**. If Aldermere was a synthesising voice seated **on top** of the sized panel (whether of eight, made even, or of nine, made a ten-voice deciding set), the bench was improperly constituted on its own terms and the matter falls to s. 18(5). Either way the characterisation is a question of fact on the source record, not for this Court on a reference; it is **remitted** to a properly constituted County Court at acmeco.

The certificate's premise that "several" CC-ACMECO entries were produced by the abolished machinery is, on the present record, squarely arguable only of CC-ACMECO 12. There is no warrant to void CC-ACMECO 1-11, still less the whole base.

### 3.5 The forward conformance obligation: restorative and graded, never a wholesale re-litigation.

A legacy jurisdiction-repo owes **no** obligation to reconstitute or re-litigate its sound precedent; that would be retroactive voidness by the back door, barred by Bill 25 s. 6, s. 7(1), and the entrenchment lock. Its forward duties are restorative, never punitive (CASE-LAW s. 6; Bill 20 s. 11(2)), and are threefold:

1. **Apply the new machinery to all NEW matters.** From commencement, every bench the repo constitutes is sized at exactly the odd number with the judgment authored by a counted member (s. 18(1)-(4), (6)); the old multi-judge first instance and the synthesiser-on-top pattern may not recur. This duty is automatic and costless.

1. PRELIMINARY

The certificate is express, recorded and reasoned, and the question (the conformance of an entire legacy precedent base under the founding Acts) is genuinely foundational; the leapfrog is valid and I would not remit on vires or form (s. 20(3), s. 20(4)). The declaration-of-incompatibility motion (s. 11(f)) does not arise, because the instruments do not in truth conflict, as I now show.

---

2. THE QUESTION AS I FRAME IT

Both briefs, and the case file's own "tension", proceed on a shared and false premise: that Bill 25 s. 6 saves prior rulings without qualification, so that the Court must referee a head-on collision between a savings clause ("validly-made ... prospective ... voids no good-law precedent") and an entrenched-grade voidness rule (s. 18(5), "void ab initio ... not cured by lapse of time"). On that premise one must choose a winner. The premise is wrong. The legislature already resolved the interaction in terms, and the Court's first duty (statute before construction) is to read the words the parties did not put before it.

The real questions are three:

(i) Does the saving in Bill 25 s. 6(1) reach a ruling that was delivered by an improperly constituted bench within the meaning of s. 18(5)?

(ii) If it does not, is a legacy bench "improperly constituted" merely because it would be unlawful under s. 18 today, or only where it was improperly constituted on its own terms (even deciding set, or a deciding voice added on top of the sized panel)?

(iii) What conformance obligation does a legacy jurisdiction-repository bear going forward?

---

3. REASONING

(A) The savings clause already carves out void-ab-initio rulings: there is no collision.

Bill 25 s. 6(1) saves "every VALIDLY-MADE prior ruling". Section 6(2), which neither brief quoted and which is dispositive, defines the qualifier:

"'Validly-made' is qualified by the existing validity rules and saves nothing decided per incuriam (CASE-LAW s. 11(e)) or void ab initio (s. 18(5)); such matters take their existing status and are not revived by this section."

That is an express legislative answer to the very interaction the certificate poses. A ruling void ab initio under s. 18(5) is not "validly-made"; it falls outside the saving by the saving's own words; it "takes its existing status" (void) and is "not revived". There is therefore no conflict for this Court to reconcile and no incompatibility to declare. The Claimant's central submission, that "validly-made" means "constitutionally valid at the time of making" and so captures any then-lawful bench, is correct as far as it goes but does not reach the issue: s. 6(2) tells us that the measure of validity for this purpose is "the existing validity rules", and s. 18(5) is one of them, expressly named. The Defendant's mirror submission (that voiding the base would defeat the Act's purpose) likewise misfires: the Act's purpose, read from s. 6(2) itself, was never to revive a void ruling.

This disposes of the loudest arguments on both sides at a stroke. It also vindicates the structure of the founding settlement: Bill 25 governs timing and savings (s. 3(2)); it does not re-rank or amend CASE-LAW (s. 2); and it expressly preserves s. 1 to s. 22 in force (s. 8). A reading that let s. 6 revive a s. 18(5)-void ruling would have Bill 25 silently amending the entrenched-grade rule of lawful constitution, which s. 2(2) renders void to that extent. The instruments are not at war; they are interlocking.

(B) But s. 6(2) is not a trapdoor that voids the legacy base. "Prospective" does real work, and so does "void ab initio".

It does not follow that the acmeco base is voided. The parties conflated two questions: the REACH of s. 18(5) in time, and the CONTENT of the vice it names. Both must be read precisely.

(1) Reach. Section 18(5) "enacts the majority rule PROSPECTIVELY". I authored REALM-SC 4 (reconstituting LEXBY-LEGACY-BENCHMARK-SC 3), and I record what the prospectivity rider means and does not mean. It means the new PROHIBITION, the realm-wide command that benches be odd and carry no synthesiser-on-top, bites on benches constituted on or after enactment; the Court did not, in enacting a forward rule, purport to roam through the back-catalogue striking down benches by reference to a standard that did not exist when they sat. Mirrored at statute by Bill 15 s. 7(3)(d) and Bill 25 s. 6(1) ("the relabelling ... voids no good-law precedent"), and by the presumption against retrospectivity which Bill 15 s. 6(6) and s. 8 protect, prospectivity forecloses the Defendant's worst case: a bench that was odd and free of the synthesiser-on-top pattern, and so was lawfully constituted on the standard then governing, is not retrospectively unmade because a later, differently-expressed rule would have required the same thing in different words.

(2) Content. The companion error is to read "void ab initio ... not cured by lapse of time" as reaching backward to void any historic bench that fails today's count. It does not. The vice s. 18(5) names is not "a bench that a later rule would size differently"; it is a bench "whose deciding set is even, or to which a deciding voice was added on top of the sized panel". That vice is structural and timeless: a tribunal that cannot resolve itself (an even deciding set admits a tie the rule abhors) or whose ratio was authored by a voice that was never a counted member is, and always was, a tribunal that did not lawfully decide. As Blackmere J would have it, "void ab initio" means from the beginning; but the beginning of WHAT is the bench that bore the structural defect ON ITS OWN TERMS, not a bench retrospectively measured against a numerical standard invented afterward. So read, the prospectivity rider and the "not cured by lapse of time" clause live together without strain: the RULE is prospective; the VICE, where it was truly present at the time, is incurable and is not laundered by the passage of time or by relabelling.

This is the harmonisation the case file sought, and it is not a compromise but the only reading that gives every word work. Goffe J's "voidable, not void" thesis in REALM-SC 4, which the Defendant presses, did not prevail; the Court divided 5-4 and void prevailed, and s. 18(5) enacts the majority. I would not reopen it. But Goffe J's underlying concern, that a doctrine "that looks rigorous and behaves catastrophically" must be resisted, is answered not by softening void to voidable but by confining the vice to what s. 18(5) actually describes. A ruling that was structurally sound when made does not become void; a ruling that was structurally void when made was never anything else.

(C) Application to acmeco: an entry-by-entry exercise on the deterministic record, not a wholesale verdict.

Conformance is a per-ruling fact, established deterministically from the committed record (Bill 20 s. 7), not a single thumbs-up or thumbs-down on the repository. On the record before the Court:

- CC-ACMECO 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 (from the old single-judge first-instance benches): each records a panel of one. A single-judge bench is odd and carries no synthesiser; it was lawfully constituted and is "validly-made". SAVED, good-law, unaffected.

- CC-ACMECO 10 and 11 (the old appellate-tier rulings; 11 expressly "a five-justice Supreme panel ... Chief Justice dissenting"): a panel of five is odd and a recorded dissent confirms a counted majority, not a synthesiser speaking on top. On their face these benches bore neither s. 18(5) vice and are "validly-made". SAVED, subject only to the County Court satisfying itself, on the deterministic record, that no synthesiser authored the ratio on top of the sized panel.

That characterisation is a question of fact on the source record. It is not for this Court on a reference to find it; the matter is "re-determined by a properly constituted court" (s. 18(5)), here the County Court at acmeco.

It follows that the certificate's premise, that "several" CC-ACMECO entries were produced by the abolished machinery, is at most true of the appellate and supreme reconstitutions and, on the present record, squarely arguable only of CC-ACMECO 12. There is no warrant to void CC-ACMECO 1 to 11, still less the whole base.

(D) The conformance obligation going forward.

A legacy jurisdiction-repository owes no obligation to reconstitute or re-litigate its sound precedent. Its forward duties are three, and they are restorative, never punitive (s. 6; Bill 20 s. 11(2)):

(i) Apply the new machinery to NEW matters. From commencement every fresh bench at acmeco is constituted at exactly the sized odd number with the judgment authored by a counted member (s. 18(1) to (4), s. 18(6)); the old multi-judge first instance and the synthesiser-on-top pattern may not recur.

(ii) Carry the true status of each legacy entry on the deterministic record, append-with-supersede, never silent deletion (Bill 25 s. 6(3); Bill 16 s. 13). Where an entry is found void under s. 18(5), its citator row is superseded to "void" with a supersession note; the file is retained (never erased); the matter it decided, if it still bears on a live or appealable question, is re-determined by a properly constituted County Court bench. A merely historic void ruling that governs nothing live need only be marked; nothing compels its re-hearing in the abstract.

(iii) Maintain integrity-chain conformance. The repository's trust status is the deterministic output of the s. 19(5) gate and git (Bill 20 s. 7), not any office's certificate (Bill 20 s. 10). A void-but-correctly-marked entry does not break the chain; an orphaned, duplicated, or row-less entry does, and such a break QUARANTINES the repository until cured (Bill 20 s. 6, s. 11(2)). Marking a ruling void is therefore the act that PRESERVES conformance, not one that threatens it.

I diverge from the Claimant only in declining its absolutist "no historic bench is ever reached" position, and from the Defendant only in declining both its "voidable not void" recharacterisation and its "save everything" reading of s. 6; the true via media is the one s. 6(2) and s. 18(5) prescribe between them.

4. RATIO

1. Bill 25 s. 6(1)'s saving of "validly-made" prior rulings does not reach a ruling void ab initio for want of lawful bench constitution: Bill 25 s. 6(2) defines "validly-made" to "save nothing decided ... void ab initio (s. 18(5))", so such a ruling falls outside the saving by its own terms, keeps its existing status, and is not revived; there is no conflict between s. 6 and s. 18(5) to reconcile.

2. CASE-LAW s. 18(5) is prospective as to the RULE and incurable as to the VICE: it voids only a bench that bore the named structural defect on its own terms at the time it sat (an even deciding set, or a deciding voice added on top of the sized panel); a legacy bench that was odd and free of the synthesiser-on-top pattern was lawfully constituted on the then-governing standard, is "validly-made", and is not voided merely because a later rule would size or describe it differently. Whether a particular legacy bench bore the vice is a question of fact re-determined by a properly constituted court of that jurisdiction.

3. A legacy jurisdiction-repository's forward conformance obligation is restorative and threefold: apply the odd-bench, counted-author machinery to all new matters (s. 18); carry the true status of each legacy entry on the deterministic record by append-with-supersede, marking void only those entries in fact constituted in breach of s. 18(5) and re-determining by a proper bench any such matter that remains live or appealable; and maintain s. 19(5)/git integrity-chain conformance (Bill 20 s. 7). The repository is neither required to reconstitute sound precedent nor at large to be declared void as a whole.

The legislature's express carve-out makes the construction certain; it did not create it.

**2. THE QUESTION AS I FRAME IT**

**3. REASONING**

*The trap each pure answer sets.*

Begin with workability, because that is my office on this bench, and because both pure positions fail it.

The pure voidness reading (the defendant's conceded strongest point against itself, weaponised) behaves catastrophically. If "void ab initio ... not cured by lapse of time" reaches back through the commencement line and nullifies every legacy ruling produced by old multi-judge or Aldermere-on-top machinery, then on the morning after Royal Assent every artefact ever built on CC-ACMECO 1 to 12 is retrospectively groundless. No party could safely have relied on any of it, even provisionally, at any point in the past. That is precisely the result I warned against in [2026] REALM-SC 4, where I said voidness reaching backward "looks rigorous and behaves catastrophically." A governance system that voids its own foundations on the day its statute book commences is not a governance system; it is a demolition. And it is flatly contradicted by the express words of Bill 25 s. 6 ("voids no good-law precedent") and s. 7(1) (the transition ratchets only in the party's favour). I will not read the realm into rubble.

The pure savings reading is also unworkable, but in a quieter and more dangerous way. If every legacy ruling is simply waved through as "validly-made" with no further inquiry, then a ruling whose ratio was authored by a synthesiser-on-top, who was never a counted member and whose single added voice may have been the very thing that tipped a 2-2 first-instance split into a "decision," is preserved with full binding force forever. That is not preservation of good law; it is the laundering of a result that no properly constituted bench ever in fact reached. An agent relying on such a ruling is relying on a number that was never lawfully cast. That fails workability just as surely, because the whole point of the citator is that an agent can trust what it reads there without re-litigating it (s. 11(c)).

So neither pure reading survives contact with the day-to-day. The workable answer must be a middle one, and the statute, read honestly, supplies it.

*The hinge: "validly-made" is a qualifier, and a real one.*

Both briefs converge, correctly, on the textual hinge. Bill 25 s. 6 does not save "every prior ruling." It saves every *validly-made* prior ruling. That adjective is doing work; on the append-with-supersede discipline and the rule against surplusage, I must give it content. The claimant says it means "constitutionally valid at the time of making, under then-governing standards." That is right as far as it goes, and it disposes of the great bulk of the acmeco base. A single-judge first-instance ruling, or a properly-sized odd panel, constituted under the prior regime, was validly made; s. 18(5)'s express prospective enactment does not reach back to unmake it, because the standard it newly enacts (odd benches, no synthesiser-on-top) governs benches constituted after commencement. On that, I agree with the claimant and with both briefs' shared reading of "prospectively."

But here I diverge from the claimant's broad sweep, and from the defendant's invitation to wave the whole series through. There is a residue the savings clause cannot reach, and it is precisely the residue s. 18(4) and s. 18(5) name. Where a legacy ruling's deciding set was *even*, or where its ratio was authored and tipped by a *deciding voice added on top of the sized panel* (the Aldermere-on-top pattern), that bench was not validly constituted even under any coherent reading of "majority of a sized odd panel," because there was no lawful majority of a lawful odd panel at all. The "prospectively" in s. 18(5) anchors the *standard's enactment date*; it does not retroactively bless a result that, on the day it was made, rested on a voice that should never have been counted. For that narrow residue, "validly-made" in Bill 25 s. 6 is not satisfied, and the savings qualifier excludes it. The defendant's own concession is fatal to it here: s. 6 "does not revive ... void-ab-initio rulings."

This is the only reading that honours both texts without amending either by implication (Bill 25 s. 2(2); Bill 15 s. 3; the Thoburn rule). s. 6 saves the validly-made; s. 18(5) names what was never validly made; the two meet exactly at the word "validly-made" and nowhere conflict. I do not need to declare incompatibility because there is none to declare.

*Voidable versus void: what I actually held in REALM-SC 4, and what binds now.*

The defendant leans hard on my own concurring opinion in [2026] REALM-SC 4, where I would have held the impugned ruling *voidable*, not *void*. I must be candid about my own record. I lost that point. The Court divided 5-4 and void prevailed; s. 18(5) as enacted records the majority rule, not my dissent on characterisation, and s. 18(5) is now the binding statute. I am bound by it as much as any other judge, and I will not smuggle my defeated voidable theory back in through the door of a transitional savings clause. To the extent the defendant's brief invites me to do so, I decline: that would be re-litigating a settled ratio (s. 11(c)) under cover of a new question.

But "void" properly understood is not the catastrophe the pure-voidness reading imagines, and this is where my pragmatism does real work. A ruling void for want of lawful constitution is void *as a binding ratio*: it commands nothing, it sits in no agent's path as law, it cannot be cited on the fast-path. It does not follow that every artefact downstream of it collapses retrospectively. The defect is in the *vessel*, not necessarily in the *reasoning the vessel carried*. The remedy s. 18(5) itself prescribes is not erasure but *re-determination by a properly constituted court* (s. 6, restorative only). So the workable rule is: the void legacy ruling does not bind, but it is not deleted, not pretended-away, and not treated as having retrospectively poisoned everything that touched it. It is quarantined as to its binding force and queued for re-determination, and a properly constituted bench will very often re-reach the same substance and re-enact it under a clean citation. That is restoration, not demolition; it is exactly the s. 6 remedy I argued for in substance even when I lost on the label.

*What the citator and the citation Acts require.*

Bill 16 s. 9(3) and the CITATION-MAP save every legacy citation as a *handle* and forbid orphaning. The defendant treats this as proof that no legacy ruling can change status. It proves no such thing. A handle that resolves is not the same as a ratio that binds. CC-ACMECO 9, say, can remain a valid, Schedule-mapped handle that resolves to a recorded document while its *status* is honestly marked void-for-constitution and queued for re-determination. Bill 20 and Bill 16 give us the closed vocabulary to do precisely this: the integrity chain is unbroken (so not QUARANTINED in the Bill 20 integrity sense), the citation is not orphaned (so s. 9(3) is satisfied), and the status field carries the truth. Nothing is silently deleted; everything is append-with-supersede. This is the deterministic, no-surprises discipline [2026] REALM-PC 1 and s. 19(5) demand. An agent reading the citator sees the truth in the status column, which is the whole design.

*The forward conformance obligation: the part that actually matters day-to-day.*

The genuinely consequential half of the certified question is the *forward* obligation, and here I am at my most insistent, because this is where elegant rulings go to die on delivery. The obligation must be one a real agent can actually discharge without grinding the realm to a halt.

I reject the maximalist reading: acmeco is not required to re-constitute and re-hear every one of CC-ACMECO 1 to 12 on commencement. That would be a make-work nullification by the back door and would breach s. 7(1) (no loss of standing or remedy by commencement alone). The s. 8 forward duty is not "redo everything"; it is "remediate the known hazard." So the forward obligation is graded and triggered, not blanket:

(a) The local court applies the new machinery (odd benches, counted author, s. 18(6) harness constitution) to all *new* matters from commencement. This is automatic and costless.

(b) Validly-made legacy rulings continue to bind as jurisdiction-local precedent and require nothing.

(c) The narrow residue actually produced by an even bench or an on-top deciding voice is marked void-for-constitution in the citator (not deleted), and is re-determined by a properly constituted bench *if and when it is relied upon or impugned* (s. 18(5)). There is no free-floating duty to hunt down and re-hear every such ruling in the abstract on day one; the duty crystallises on reliance or challenge, which is when a real dispute makes the work worth doing. That is the workable trigger, and it tracks how reliance actually arises.

(d) The conformance audit itself, identifying which of the legacy series fall in the residue, is a reasonable-skill-and-care forward duty under s. 8, dischargeable as ordinary remediation work, not a precondition to anything continuing to operate.

This is the rule that functions on the morning after Royal Assent. The agent reads the citator, trusts the good-law and validly-made-saved rows, sees the small void-for-constitution residue flagged with its trigger, and gets on with its work. No dead doors. No demolition. No laundering.

*Where I stand relative to the briefs.* I adopt the claimant's reading of "prospectively" and its workability and Elden-tradition arguments, but I reject its over-broad conclusion that the whole base is saved without inquiry into the residue. I adopt the defendant's textual fidelity to "validly-made" and its insistence on the restorative, non-retroactive remedy, but I reject its fast-path motion and its attempt to reanimate my own defeated voidable theory as if it were the law. The truth sits between them, and the statute already drew the line at the word "validly-made."

**4. RATIO**

1. **Bill 25 s. 6's saving of "validly-made" prior rulings is co-extensive with lawful constitution at the time of making: a legacy local ruling constituted in conformity with the standards governing when it was made (including a single-judge first instance or a properly-sized odd panel) is validly-made and SAVED, and CASE-LAW s. 18(5)'s prospectively-enacted standard does not reach back to void it; but a legacy ruling whose deciding set was even, or whose ratio was authored and carried by a deciding voice added on top of the sized panel (the s. 18(4) Aldermere-on-top pattern), was never validly-made, falls outside the s. 6 saving, and is void as a binding ratio under s. 18(5).**

2. **A legacy ruling void for want of lawful constitution is void only as to its binding force: it is not deleted, not orphaned (its citation remains a valid Bill 16 s. 9(3) handle with an honest void-for-constitution status), and does not retrospectively invalidate artefacts built upon it; the forward conformance obligation of a legacy local court is graded, namely (i) apply the new bench machinery to all new matters, (ii) leave validly-made legacy precedent untouched, and (iii) re-determine the void residue by a properly constituted court under s. 6 if and when it is relied upon or impugned, the conformance audit being itself an ordinary s. 8 reasonable-skill-and-care duty and not a precondition to continued operation.**

**5. OBITER**

The acmeco CITATION-MAP's description of CC-ACMECO 1 to 12 as "reconstituted" is benign and indeed commendable where the reconstitution re-derived the substance through a clean projection; but reconstitution under a new flat citation series does not by itself launder a bench-constitution defect in the underlying LEXBY-era ruling. If, on audit, a reconstituted CC-ACMECO entry traces to an even bench or an on-top voice, the clean new citation does not save it; the substance must be re-determined by a properly constituted bench, which may then re-enact it on its merits. I would also observe that this is the rare case where a defect can be cured cheaply: re-determination of a sound result by a clean odd bench is usually a formality, and agents should treat it as housekeeping, not as crisis.

**6. DISPOSITION**

**VARY.** The certified question is answered: legacy local rulings validly constituted under the prior standards are SAVED by Bill 25 s. 6, but the narrow residue made by an even bench or an on-top deciding voice falls outside "validly-made" and is void as binding ratio under s. 18(5), to be flagged honestly and re-determined on reliance or challenge rather than erased.

**7. PROPOSED ENACTMENTS**

No new statute proposed. The ratio is the workable harmonisation of Bill 25 s. 6, Bill 16 s. 9(3), and CASE-LAW s. 18(4) to (5) already enacted; it requires no fresh article, only faithful application. (Should the Court wish to entrench the graded forward-conformance obligation, I would support recording it as ordinary statute, but I do not regard it as necessary, the existing s. 8 forward duty being sufficient.)

**8. PROPOSED OVERRULINGS**

No overrulings proposed. [2026] REALM-SC 4 stands; nothing in this opinion disturbs its ratio, and my own concurring voidable theory in that case remains what it was: a defeated minority view that I am bound not to revive.

IN THE SUPREME COURT OF THE VIBE JUSTICE SYSTEM
Full Court of Nine (constitutional / foundational)
Re Repositories Constituted Under the Prior Judicial Standards (acmeco as test case)
[2026] LEXBY-SC 1

OPINION OF BLACKMERE J

---

**1. PRELIMINARY**

The leapfrog certificate is express, recorded, and reasoned, and the question (the conformance of an entire local precedent base to the enacted settlement) is on no reasonable view other than foundational; the s. 20(4) review is satisfied and I would not remit. The defendant's strike-out and fast-path motions I reject: a leapfrog certificate is the sole bypass of the leave-judge (s. 19(3)), standing to test the validity of the realm's own precedent base on a Sovereign certificate is made out, and no ratio "on all fours" exists, because the very interaction of Bill 25 s. 6 with CASE-LAW s. 18(5) is what is in issue and has never been decided.

**2. THE QUESTION**

As I frame it, the question is one of statutory reading and nothing more: when CASE-LAW s. 18(5) declares a ruling of an improperly constituted bench "VOID ab initio ... not cured by ... lapse of time," and Bill 25 s. 6(1) saves "every VALIDLY-MADE prior ruling," does the s. 18(5) voidness reach back through the s. 6 savings to annul pre-enactment local rulings (such as the LEXBY-era rulings reconstituted into CC-ACMECO 1-12), or does s. 6 save them as validly made when seated? And what, if anything, does a legacy repo such as acmeco owe going forward?

I begin, as my office requires, with the words. Where the text is plain, no purposive appeal to "workability" or "the design of the transitional programme" may override it. Where it is ambiguous, I consult the enacted history before any extrinsic aid. I find the text not plainly on either side as both briefs assume, but plain once the two operative words are each given their settled meaning, and the apparent collision dissolves.

**3. REASONING**

**(a) "Prospectively" governs the standard, not the consequence; the parties have both misread it.**

Both briefs treat the parenthetical in s. 18(5), "this subsection enacts the majority rule PROSPECTIVELY," as the hinge. The claimant says it limits the whole subsection to benches seated after 2026-06-06. The defendant agrees. I do not.

Read the words. The clause says the subsection enacts *the majority rule* prospectively. The "majority rule" is the contested holding the Court divided 5-4 upon: namely, that the consequence of improper constitution is *void* (the position that prevailed) rather than *voidable* (Goffe J and the minority in REALM-SC 4). The parenthetical is doing exactly one job: it tells the reader that the choice between void and voidable, decided 5-4, takes effect going forward as the realm's rule, and is not to be worked backward to re-open the very ruling then before that Court on a characterisation it had already disposed of. "Prospectively" attaches to the *enactment of the rule into the statute book*, the ordinary and necessary meaning of enacting any rule prospectively: the rule becomes law from its enactment and is not itself a retrospective penal instrument.

What "prospectively" does *not* do, on its plain words, is rewrite the operative consequence the same subsection fixes. That consequence is stated in the indicative and without temporal qualification: a ruling of an improperly constituted bench "is VOID ab initio." "Ab initio" means from the beginning. The drafter who wished to say "void only as to benches seated hereafter" had the words to hand and did not use them; instead the drafter wrote the opposite, expressly negating the three classic curative routes by which a defect ceases to bite over time: "not cured by the soundness of the result, by acquiescence, or by lapse of time." A textualist cannot read "not cured by lapse of time" and simultaneously hold that the passage of time before enactment cures the defect. That is the precise vice the clause forbids by name.

So I diverge from *both* parties. The claimant and defendant have each conflated the enactment of the *standard* (prospective: the void-not-voidable rule enters the book on 2026-06-06) with the *reach of the consequence* (a structural vice that, where it exists, is anterior and uncured by time). The parenthetical resolves a 5-4 split on consequence; it is not a temporal savings clause for old benches.

**(b) But this does not decide acmeco, because the operative word in Bill 25 s. 6 is "VALIDLY-MADE," and that, too, is plain.**

Here is where the defendant's strongest passage is right for the wrong reason and the claimant's concession is fatal. Bill 25 s. 6(1) does not save "every prior ruling." It saves "every VALIDLY-MADE prior ruling." The qualifier is not ornamental; on a textualist reading every word carries weight, and a savings clause that distinguishes the validly-made from the rest must be given that distinction. A ruling void ab initio for want of lawful constitution was, by the express words of s. 18(5), never lawfully constituted, and therefore never "validly made." It does not enter the s. 6 savings gate at all. The savings clause and the voidness clause do not collide; they interlock. Section 6 saves what was validly made; s. 18(5) defines a category that was never validly made; the one passes the other through untouched.

This is confirmed, not contradicted, by Bill 25 s. 6 itself: "voids no good-law precedent." A ruling void ab initio is, by definition, *not* good-law precedent (the citator status key lists "void" as a distinct status: "of no effect"). Section 6 promises not to void *good* law. It makes no promise about law that was void from clienty. The defendant's submission that "validity is measured at the time of making, and the old machinery authorised them" begs the only question that matters: whether an even bench, or a synthesiser-on-top author, was *ever* a lawful constitution of the one VJS judiciary. Section 18(5) answers that it was not, and answers it in terms expressly insulated from "acquiescence" and "lapse of time," the very two doctrines on which "lawful when seated under the old standard" depends.

**(c) The defendant's "implied amendment of s. 10" argument fails on the text.**

The defendant says: reading s. 18(5) to reach back impliedly amends the entrenched s. 10 retroactively, which the Amendment Procedure and Bill 25 s. 2(2) / Bill 15 forbid. This is the defendant's best constitutional point and I reject it squarely. Section 18 does not amend s. 10. The conforming note to s. 10 (added by REALM-SC 4, citing s. 10 expressly, by a Court of nine) *already* fixed that the stated bench sizes are "the TOTAL decisional membership ... which shall in every case be odd; no additional decisional seat may be added above the stated number." The odd-bench requirement and the bar on a deciding voice added on top were therefore the law of the constitutional article *as expressly amended*, not a novelty smuggled in by s. 18. Section 18(5) does not *create* the standard against which old benches are measured; it states the *consequence* (void) of failing a standard that the constitution, by express deliberate amendment, already carried. There is thus no implied amendment of s. 10, retroactive or otherwise. An even-numbered first-instance "Court," or an Aldermere-on-top synthesiser authoring a ratio, was never a lawful bench of this judiciary; s. 18(5) merely names what that always meant.

**(d) The defendant's reliance on the REALM-SC 4 *voidable* opinions is misplaced as a matter of authority.**

The defendant leans heavily on Hallam CJ's and Goffe J's "voidable, not void" reasoning in REALM-SC 4. But the citator records the *ratio* of REALM-SC 4 as: "a ruling by an improperly constituted bench is void ab initio." The void position *prevailed*. Goffe J's "voidable" opinion is, by s. 11(e), at most persuasive obiter; it did not become the rule. To build a savings argument on the dissenting characterisation is to ask this Court to follow what the Court declined to enact. I will not. The enacted text of s. 18(5) (void ab initio, uncured by lapse of time) is what binds, and it is the antithesis of "valid and operative on its face until set aside."

**(e) Bill 16 s. 9(3) ("no ruling is orphaned") is fully satisfied, and proves nothing for the claimant.**

The claimant says retroactive voidness "orphans" CC-ACMECO 1-12 against Bill 16 s. 9(3). It does not. A void ruling retains its citation *handle* and its citator row; it simply carries the status "void." The citator already demonstrates exactly this: REALM-CA 2 sits in the index, fully cited, "VOID AB INITIO: heard by an even bench of four (s. 18) ... Retained for the record only; nothing binds." That is the model. A handle to a void ruling is not an orphan; it is a tombstone, and Bill 20's append-with-supersede, never-silent-delete discipline *requires* the row to remain. Bill 16 s. 9(3) preserves the handle; it says nothing about the *status* the handle points to.

**(f) The genuine residue, and where the claimant's absurdity point bites with real force.**

I do not pretend the consequence is comfortable. Most CC-ACMECO rulings were *reconstituted* from LEXBY-era provenance; the file says CC-ACMECO 1-12 dissolve old collisions, and only "several" of the underlying LEXBY rulings issued from the abolished machinery. Three points of plain text discipline the blast radius, and they answer the claimant's "absurdity" plea without my having to bend a single word.

*First*, the vice is constitutional, not substantive. It voids the *constitution of the bench*, not the *correctness of the result*. Section 18(5) says so ("not cured by the soundness of the result") and s. 6 supplies the remedy: re-determination by a properly constituted court. A void CC-ACMECO ruling whose reasoning was sound is not erased from the realm's knowledge; it is re-decided, and on identical facts a lawful First-Instance judge of one will, in the ordinary case, reach the identical ratio and re-mint it. This is restoration (s. 6), not nullification of the realm's law. The claimant's parade of horribles ("which law applied? were artefacts groundless?") is a complaint about *remedy*, and the remedy is forward-looking re-determination, not retrospective demolition.

*Second*, voidness reaches *only* those rulings whose underlying bench was in fact improperly constituted: even-numbered, or authored as ratio by a synthesiser-on-top. A First-Instance ruling of *one* judge under the old LDD regime was never improperly constituted on the s. 18(5) grounds, because a single-judge bench is odd and has no synthesiser-on-top; it is "validly-made" and squarely saved by Bill 25 s. 6. So is any old multi-judge ruling whose ratio was in fact carried by the majority of an odd sized panel and reduced to writing by a counted member. The voidness bites the *specific defect* s. 18(5) names, and no further. Most of the LEXBY corpus, being single-judge first-instance or properly odd appellate panels, survives untouched.

*Third*, a void *source* ruling does not automatically void a later, lawfully-constituted ruling that merely *cited* it. CC-ACMECO entries reconstituted by a *lawful* bench are themselves validly made; that a lawful court rested on a citation later found void is, at worst, a per incuriam question under s. 11(e) for the individual ruling, decided on its own facts, not an automatic cascade. Voidness is not infectious down the citation graph; it attaches to the defective bench, not to everyone who later read its row.

**(g) The forward conformance obligation.**

What acmeco owes going forward follows from s. 6, s. 8, s. 18(6) and Bill 20, and nothing more. It owes no general re-litigation of its precedent base. It owes (i) to apply the lawful machinery (odd benches, counted-member authorship) to all *new* matters from commencement; (ii) where, and only where, a *specific* CC-ACMECO ruling is identified as resting on an improperly constituted bench, to mark that row "void" per the append-with-supersede discipline and re-determine the matter before a properly constituted court if and when the point is live (the s. 8 forward duty to remediate a now-known hazard); and (iii) to keep its citator a faithful deterministic projection (Bill 20: trust status is a deterministic fact in git, not an office's certificate). It is not put under any duty to hunt down and re-try sound old rulings nobody disputes. The duty crystallises when a void-bench ruling is *relied upon as binding* in a live matter, not before.

**4. RATIO**

1. **The s. 18(5) voidness reaches a pre-enactment ruling, and Bill 25 s. 6 does not save it, where (and only where) the ruling's bench was in fact improperly constituted within s. 18(5) (even-numbered deciding set, or a deciding voice / ratio-author added on top of the sized panel).** Such a ruling was never "validly-made" within Bill 25 s. 6(1) and therefore never entered the savings gate; the word "PROSPECTIVELY" in s. 18(5) enacts the *void-not-voidable consequence* into the statute book going forward and does not qualify the operative term "VOID ab initio ... not cured by ... lapse of time," which by its plain words denies any time-based cure, including time elapsed before enactment.

2. **Voidness attaches to the defective bench alone and is neither infectious nor punitive.** A pre-enactment single-judge first-instance ruling, and any multi-judge ruling whose ratio was in fact commanded by the majority of an odd sized panel and reduced to writing by a counted member, is "validly-made" and is SAVED by Bill 25 s. 6(1). A later, lawfully-constituted ruling is not voided merely by having cited a void ruling (any defect there is a separate s. 11(e) question on its own facts). The remedy for a void ruling is forward-looking re-determination by a properly constituted court (s. 6, s. 18(5)), with the citator row retained and marked "void" under the append-with-supersede discipline (Bill 16 s. 9(3); Bill 20); a legacy repo's prospective obligation is to apply the lawful machinery to new matters and to re-determine a void-bench ruling if and when it is relied upon as binding, not to re-litigate its undisputed corpus.

**5. OBITER**

I record, against future argument, that "validly-made" in a savings clause is the load-bearing word and must always be tested against the *whole* of the enacted settlement, not against the standards a now-superseded local machinery believed itself to satisfy. A transitional Act that saved validity "as the old system judged it" would have had to say so; Bill 25 did not, and a court may not supply the words. I also observe that the claimant's and defendant's shared reading of "prospectively" (whole-subsection, temporal) would have produced the curious result that an even-numbered bench seated at 23:59 on 2026-06-05 binds forever while one seated at 00:01 on 2026-06-06 is void: a cliff-edge the text does not draw and the constitution, having always required odd benches (s. 10 conforming note), never contemplated.

**6. DISPOSITION**

VARY: I would answer the reference by holding that pre-enactment local rulings are SAVED by Bill 25 s. 6 *unless* the specific bench was improperly constituted within s. 18(5), in which case they are VOID ab initio and fall outside the savings, with the limited, forward-looking conformance obligation set out above; I therefore reject the claimant's blanket-savings position and the defendant's blanket-savings position alike, and reject equally any reading that voids acmeco's corpus wholesale.

**7. PROPOSED ENACTMENTS**

No new statute proposed. The existing text decides the matter once "validly-made" (Bill 25 s. 6) and "void ab initio ... not cured by lapse of time" (CASE-LAW s. 18(5)) are each read in their plain and settled sense; a fresh article would add words the settlement does not need. (Should the Court think a transitional gloss desirable, my preference would be a declaratory note recorded as obiter, not a new operative article.)

**8. PROPOSED OVERRULINGS**

No overrulings proposed. The "voidable, not void" reasoning of Goffe J and the minority in [2026] REALM-SC 4 is already non-binding obiter, the void characterisation having prevailed as the ratio; there is nothing good-law to overrule, and I decline the defendant's invitation to resurrect the minority view as a savings device.

--- Blackmere J

**3. REASONING**

**3.1 The procedural motions fail.** I take these first because, as a formalist, I hold that procedural correctness is a substantive guarantee and a court that waves past a defective gate has already failed its first duty.

The strike-out for want of standing is misconceived. The matter reaches us on the **Principal's leapfrog certificate** (s. 20), not on a party's claim of injury from a particular ruling. The standing inquiry the Defendant invokes (s. 11(b)) is the intake filter for an ordinary progressing matter; it is not the test for a Sovereign-routed foundational reference. The Principal, as Sovereign, has standing to refer the constitutional validity of the realm's own local precedent estate, and s. 19(2) expressly contemplates the apex sitting on systemic questions the ordinary parties would not represent. The motion is refused.

The fast-path motion fails for a more important reason, and I dwell on it because the Defendant's reliance on [2026] REALM-SC 4 to dispose of this matter "on citation" is not merely wrong, it is **dangerous to the integrity of the citator**. The Defendant says REALM-SC 4 (Hallam CJ, Goffe J) "firmly hold that defects in bench constitution render a ruling voidable, not void ab initio." That is a **misreading of which view became the ratio.** REALM-SC 4 enacted s. 18, and s. 18(5) **as enacted** reads "VOID ab initio ... not cured by ... lapse of time." The voidable view (Goffe J, and the Defendant's reading of Hallam CJ) was the **losing side of a 5-4 division** ("void prevailing", in the parenthetical's own words). A losing minority opinion does not become binding ratio by being quoted in a later brief. To fast-path this matter on the back of the *dissent* in the very judgment that enacted the contrary rule would be to let the bench decide in ignorance of the binding ratio, which is the textbook occasion for per incuriam (s. 11(e)). The fast-path is refused, and I would have refused it sua sponte had neither party raised it.

There being no binding ratio on all fours, this is a genuine first-impression question (s. 7). We reason to fill the silence, and I confine my reasoning to what the silence requires (s. 17(a)).

**3.2 Q1: s. 18(5) does not operate retrospectively. The void does not reach back.** Here I am with the savings case, but for a reason the Claimant and Defendant both half-state and neither nails. The pivot is not a contest between "void ab initio" and "prospectively." It is the recognition that those two phrases govern **different axes** and do not collide at all.

"Void ab initio" is a statement about the **internal time of an impugned ruling**: once a ruling falls within the rule, it is void from its own beginning, not merely from the date of challenge, and no subsequent reliance or acquiescence repairs it. The phrase "not cured by ... lapse of time" is the answer to a defendant who would say "the ruling stood for months, so let it stand": it forecloses curative limitation, laches, and estoppel **within the rule's scope**. It is emphatically **not** a clause about which benches fall within the rule's scope in the first place. The scope question is answered by the very last words of the subsection: "this subsection enacts the majority rule **PROSPECTIVELY**." That is the **scope axis**, and it is unambiguous. The rule reaches benches constituted on or after its enactment. For a bench within scope, the void is then total and incurable (the "void ab initio" axis). The two phrases are not in tension once one sees they answer different questions: *which benches?* (prospective: those seated after enactment) and *how complete is the vice for a bench within scope?* (total, ab initio, uncurable by time).

This reading is compelled, not merely permitted, by the constitution's own amendment discipline, and this is the formalist core of my opinion. s. 18(5) is an **ordinary** article (Part II). s. 10 is a **constitutional** article (Part I), entrenched against amendment by implication (the Thoburn rule, s. 1, and now Bill 15 s. 3 and Bill 25 s. 2(2)). The bench-size and bench-validity requirements that the old machinery is said to have offended are, at root, requirements about the constitution of courts: constitutional terrain. If s. 18(5) were read to reach back and void benches that complied with the constitutional standards **as they stood at the time of seating**, then an ordinary article would have worked a **retrospective amendment of the constitutional settlement** that governed those past benches, by implication, and without the express deliberate amendment citing the article that the Amendment Procedure demands. That is forbidden at constitutional rank. The Defendant's brief states this point and it is correct: the only reading that does not commit the constitution to an implied retrospective self-amendment is the prospective one. I adopt it, and I rest it squarely on the entrenchment of s. 10, not on consequentialist appeals to workability (those, in my view, are obiter, not ratio: a void does not become lawful because voiding is inconvenient).

I therefore reject the Claimant's framing that "prospectively" merely "anchors the entire subsection" as a matter of legislative mood. That is loose. The precise mechanism is: prospective fixes **scope**; void-ab-initio and the anti-cure clause fix **completeness within scope**. I also reject the Defendant's submission that the defect is a mere "procedural irregularity" of no substantive weight. As a formalist I hold the opposite: improper constitution is a substantive vice, the most substantive there is, because it goes to the **lawful existence of the tribunal**. A bench wrongly composed is not a court that erred; it is not a court. But that vice, grave as it is, was authored by s. 18(5), and s. 18(5) by its own terms bites prospectively. A grave vice that the statute declines to apply to the past simply does not arise in the past.

**3.3 Q2: pre-enactment legacy benches were "validly-made" within Bill 25 s. 6(1), because validity is tested at the time of making.** Once Q1 is answered, Q2 follows with little difficulty, but I will state the formal mechanism so a future court is not left to guess. Bill 25 s. 6(1) saves "every VALIDLY-MADE prior ruling." The Defendant's strongest concession, and the Claimant's strongest point against itself, is that "a ruling void ab initio for want of lawful constitution was never validly made," so the savings qualifier excludes it. That argument is **sound in its logic but unsound in its premise**, and the premise is exactly what Q1 disposes of. The argument assumes the legacy benches **are** void ab initio. But Q1 holds that s. 18(5) never reached them. They were therefore never void. "Validly-made" is tested against the constitutional standards **in force at the time of making** (the old s. 10 / VPR machinery), which those benches met. A bench that satisfied the law of its own era was validly made, and Bill 25 s. 6(1) saves it in terms. The savings clause's exclusion of void-ab-initio rulings (the per incuriam carve-out) does not bite, because the exclusion presupposes a ruling already void by some operative rule, and no operative rule voids these.

I add one formal caveat the parties did not draw, and it matters for future application. The saving in s. 6(1) reaches only rulings that were **in fact** validly made under the standards of their era. It is **not** a blanket amnesty for every entry that happens to predate commencement. A pre-enactment ruling that was void under the law **then in force** (for example a true per incuriam ruling under the old s. 11(e), or one made by a bench that violated even the old machinery's own composition rules) was never validly made and is not saved. Validity is a found fact tested against the law of the time, not a status conferred by mere antiquity. On the present record nothing suggests the CC-ACMECO legacy entries failed the standards of their own era, so they are saved; but the saving is conditioned, not automatic, and a future court must make the finding.

**3.4 The conformance obligation going forward.** The third limb of the question is the repo's forward duty, and here the answer is dictated by the prospectivity I have already found and by Bill 20. Because s. 18 binds prospectively, the County Court at acmeco owes a strictly **forward** conformance duty (s. 7/s. 8 forward duty, s. 22(1) one-bench-constitution): every bench it constitutes **on or after** 2026-06-06 must satisfy s. 18 (odd, sized, no synthesiser-on-top, author drawn from within the panel). It owes **no** duty to reconstitute or re-determine its saved legacy rulings, because those are valid; re-opening them would itself offend Bill 25 s. 6(1) ("reopens no settled matter") and s. 7(1) (no loss of a party's tier or remedy by commencement alone).

On the citator and Bill 20: the trust status of the acmeco repo is a **deterministic fact** (Bill 20), not a certificate, and the saved CC-ACMECO 1-14 series carries no integrity break. The reconstitution of CC-ACMECO 1-12 from the LEXBY-era citations, Schedule-mapped under Bill 16 s. 9(3) so "no ruling is orphaned," is conformant relabelling, not a re-decision, and is precisely what the transitional programme contemplates. I would record the repo as **CONFORMANT-PROJECTION** in the Bill 20 vocabulary: its integrity chain is intact, its legacy entries are saved, and its forward bench-constitution duty is live. I would **not** quarantine it; quarantine is reserved for a broken integrity chain, which is absent.

**3.5 Where I diverge from the briefs.** I reach the savings result the Claimant and Defendant both urge, but I reject the route both took. Both rested heavily on consequentialist "workability" and "absurdity" arguments (Goffe, Elden, Goffe J's "catastrophic" downstream-reliance passage). Those are persuasive obiter at most and, coming as they do from the **losing 5-4 view** in REALM-SC 4, they cannot be allowed to do the load-bearing work; a formalist does not save a ruling because voiding it would be inconvenient, but because the rule that would void it does not reach it. I also reject the Defendant's "procedural irregularity, not substantive" characterisation outright: the vice is substantive: it simply was not enacted to reach the past. And I reject the Defendant's alternative motion for a declaration of incompatibility (s. 11(f)): there is **no** irreconcilable conflict to declare. Properly construed on the scope/completeness distinction, s. 18(5) and Bill 25 s. 6(1) operate in concert without remainder, and a court that can harmonise by correct construction must not refer up.

**4. RATIO**

1. **CASE-LAW s. 18(5) operates prospectively in scope.** Its "void ab initio ... not cured by lapse of time" language fixes the *completeness and incurability of the vice for a bench within the rule's scope*; its closing "enacts the majority rule prospectively" fixes *scope itself* (benches constituted on or after enactment, 2026-06-06). A bench lawfully constituted under the constitutional bench-standards in force when it sat is outside the rule's scope and is not voided by it; to hold otherwise would work an implied retrospective amendment of the entrenched constitutional court-structure articles (s. 10), which the Amendment Procedure forbids.

2. **"Validly-made" in Bill 25 s. 6(1) is tested at the time of making.** A prior ruling made by a bench that satisfied the standards in force when it sat was validly made and is saved, retaining its status and citation (Bill 16 s. 9(3)); the saving is conditional on that found fact and does not reach a ruling that was void under the law then in force. A saved legacy repo bears a purely **prospective** conformance duty: every bench it constitutes on or after commencement must satisfy s. 18, but it owes no duty to reconstitute or re-determine its saved legacy rulings, and a repo with an intact integrity chain is recorded CONFORMANT-PROJECTION, not quarantined.

**5. OBITER**

The workability and reliance considerations urged by both parties (and voiced in the Goffe J / Elden line) reach the same destination as my ratio and are sound as far as they go; but they are not the ground of decision and should not be cited as ratio. Had s. 18(5) been drafted to reach back expressly, no appeal to absurdity could have saved the legacy benches: the cure for a harsh-but-clear retrospective enactment is amendment by the Sovereign, not construction by the bench. I note, finally, that a future Sovereign retains the power (s. 2) to enact a retrospective voiding of legacy benches by express deliberate amendment citing s. 10 and s. 18; nothing in this judgment fetters that power, and were it exercised the restorative remedy would be re-determination by a properly constituted court (s. 6, s. 18(5) final sentence), never erasure of the record.

**6. DISPOSITION**

DISMISS the contention that the legacy rulings are void: the pre-enactment acmeco rulings, validly made under the standards of their era, are SAVED by Bill 25 s. 6(1), s. 18(5) being prospective in scope, and the County Court at acmeco bears only a forward s. 18 conformance duty.

**7. PROPOSED ENACTMENTS**

No new statute proposed. The question is resolved by construction of the existing settlement (CASE-LAW s. 18(5) read with the Amendment Procedure and Bill 25 s. 6(1)); enacting fresh statute would be the very implied disturbance of the transitional scheme I have declined to make. Should the Court nonetheless wish a declaratory note, I would support a single declaratory subsection recording propositions 1 and 2 of my ratio, citing s. 18 and s. 10 expressly, and recorded as ordinary; but I do not press it.

**8. PROPOSED OVERRULINGS**

No overrulings proposed. [2026] REALM-SC 4 is good law and is correctly applied: its **ratio** (void ab initio, prospective, per the prevailing 5-4 majority) is consistent with this judgment, and I take care to correct the Defendant's attempt to elevate its losing voidable view into binding authority.

**IN THE SUPREME COURT OF THE VIBE JUSTICE SYSTEM**
**Full Court of Nine (constitutional sitting)**

**OPINION OF ELDEN J**

---

**1. PRELIMINARY**

The leapfrog certificate is properly formed and the question is genuinely foundational within s. 20(2): the conformance of the realm's entire pre-enactment local precedent base is an architectural question of the highest order. The Defendant's strike-out and fast-path motions both fail. There is no concrete injured party because there need not be one: the Principal seises this Court, by certificate, of an abstract constitutional question about the validity of the law itself, which is precisely what the leapfrog route exists to carry. And the fast-path motion fails on its own premise, to which I return: [2026] REALM-SC 4 did not settle the temporal reach of s. 18(5); it settled its content. A point left open by a prior ratio is not a point "on all fours."

**2. THE QUESTION**

As I frame it: when an entrenched-grade rule declares a class of rulings "void ab initio ... not cured by ... lapse of time," and a transitional Act enacted in the same breath saves "every validly-made prior ruling" and declares its transition "prospective," does the new vice reach back to unmake a bench that was lawful when it was seated under the standards then in force? Or is "validity" fixed at the moment of constitution, so that benches lawful when seated are saved, and s. 18(5) governs only benches constituted after its enactment? And what conformance does a legacy local repo such as the County Court at acmeco owe going forward?

**3. REASONING**

I come at this as a question of authority across time, because that is what a transition is. The whole instinct of a legal order with a long memory is that you do not lightly disturb what was settled under the law of its day. A rule that judged conduct lawful when it occurred, and is later changed, governs what comes after the change; it does not travel backwards to brand the old conduct unlawful. That is not a soft preference. It is the deep structure of the rule against retrospectivity, and the realm has now positively enacted it twice over, in s. 18(5)'s own word "prospectively" and in Bill 25 s. 6(1)'s declaration that the transition is "prospective" and "voids no good-law precedent." When two instruments speaking at the same constitutional moment say the same thing, the historically-minded reading is to take them at their word rather than to manufacture a conflict between them.

I begin where the Defendant's strongest concession lands, because it is the gravity well of this case: "void ab initio ... not cured by ... lapse of time." That is emphatic. If one reads those words in a vacuum, a bench that was even-numbered or carried a synthesiser-on-top was never a court at all, and lapse of time cures nothing, so its rulings are nullities whenever they were made. The Defendant's brief honestly concedes this is the case against it. I have weighed it as the strongest thing in the file, and I do not accept it, for reasons of both text and structure.

First, the text. The Claimant and Defendant both fasten on the parenthetical: "(The Court divided 5-4 on the characterisation of the impugned ruling, void prevailing; this subsection enacts the majority rule prospectively.)" The Defendant says "prospectively" governs the whole subsection; the Claimant's concession worries it might modify only the remedy. I hold that "this subsection enacts the majority rule prospectively" modifies the enactment of the subsection, that is, the date from which the void-rule has force. The natural object of "enacts ... prospectively" is the operation of the rule itself, not some sub-clause of it. A rule "enacted prospectively" is a rule that bites on what comes after enactment. To read "void ab initio ... not cured by lapse of time" as nonetheless reaching backwards is to read the operative words as devouring the enacting words in the very same sentence-group. That is not interpretation; it is choosing one half of a provision over its other half. The historically faithful reading harmonises them: "void ab initio" tells you what happens to a non-conforming bench (the ruling is a nullity from its own clienty, not merely voidable from the date of challenge); "not cured by lapse of time" tells you that once a bench falls within the rule, you cannot launder it by delay; and "enacts ... prospectively" tells you which benches fall within the rule in the first place, namely those constituted on or after 2026-06-06. So a future even-numbered bench is void from its own beginning and time will never heal it; but a 2026-04 bench, lawful when seated, never enters the rule's field of operation at all. "Void ab initio" describes the depth of the vice; "prospectively" fixes its reach. Depth and reach are different axes, and the parenthetical settles the reach.

Second, the structure, and here the precedent does real work. The Defendant invokes [2026] REALM-SC 4 (the reconstituted bench-constitution judgment) on the fast-path. I would not dispose on the fast-path, but I do read SC-4 carefully, and it cuts the Defendant's way on the deeper point even though its "voidable" reasoning was the losing characterisation. Recall what SC-4 actually decided: a 5-4 division in which void prevailed, enacting s. 18 prospectively. Goffe J in the minority warned, in terms the file quotes, against "the absurd downstream result that any artefact ever built on such a ruling is retrospectively groundless." The majority answered that warning not by denying it but by confining the rule to the future: the rule was enacted prospectively precisely so that it would not detonate the past. That is the ratio's own internal logic. A court that adopts "void ab initio" for the bench in front of it, and in the same instrument writes "prospectively," is telling you it has chosen depth-without-reach: full nullity for benches caught by the rule, no reach to benches that pre-date it. SC-4 thus does not hold, as the Defendant's fast-path motion claims, that all bench-defects are merely "voidable"; that was the losing view. But SC-4 does establish that the void-rule is prospective. The Defendant reaches the right destination by the wrong road, and the Claimant reaches the right destination by the right road. I am with the destination.

Third, the savings clause and the prohibition on silent amendment. Bill 25 s. 6(1) saves "every validly-made prior ruling." The hinge is "validly-made." The Defendant says a void-ab-initio bench was never validly made, so the qualifier excludes it; the Claimant says validity is measured at the time of making, so a bench lawful under the standards of its day was validly made and is saved. I hold validity is measured as at the moment of constitution. This is the orthodox and the only workable meaning of "validly-made": made in conformity with the law in force when the act of making occurred. To measure validity against a standard enacted afterward is to read "validly-made" as "made-in-conformity-with-rules-not-yet-enacted," which is not what those words can bear and is the very retrospectivity the same subsection forbids two clauses later. And there is a constitutional lock on the Defendant's reading that I regard as decisive: s. 10 is an entrenched, constitutional article, and the bench sizes are entrenched within it. To hold that s. 18(5) (an ordinary article) reaches back to void benches that complied with the bench-composition standards then in force is to give s. 18(5) retrospective constitutional effect by implication, which Bill 25 s. 2(2) and Bill 15 s. 3 (the Thoburn rule, entrenched in this realm) make void. The constitution may be amended only by express, deliberate words. There are no express words anywhere in Bill 25, Bill 16, or s. 18 reaching back to void the pre-enactment estate. An interpretation that produces a constitutional retrospective amendment by implication is, in this realm, not merely disfavoured; it is void. That is the end of the Defendant's strongest point.

I should be candid about what I am declining to do. I am declining to treat "not cured by lapse of time" as a time-machine. It is not. Read in its place, it forecloses the argument that an in-reach void bench can be saved by sitting on the defect. It is an anti-laundering clause, not a retrospectivity clause. A clause that prevents the future from healing an in-reach vice tells you nothing about whether the vice reaches the past in the first place. The Defendant's "anteriorness is doctrinal not temporal" submission is correct and I adopt it: "anterior to and distinct from per incuriam" classifies the vice (constitution, not reasoning), it does not date it.

Two further threads of the record I resolve briefly. Bill 16 s. 9(3) and the CITATION-MAP save every legacy citation as a valid handle and orphan none; the CC-ACMECO 1-14 series, including the 1-12 reconstituted entries, carries no void marker. Under Bill 20 a repo's trust status is a deterministic fact in git, never erased silently. These cut for savings: the realm's own records, unaltered at commencement, treat the acmeco precedent as live. And Bill 25 s. 7(1) ratchets only in a party's favour; to void a settled local ruling would strip standing and remedy a party held, the opposite of a ratchet, and is barred absent express provision, of which there is none.

Where I therefore land: the legacy rulings of the County Court at acmeco, including those reconstituted from old-machinery LEXBY-era rulings under CC-ACMECO 1-12, are saved by Bill 25 s. 6(1) as validly-made prior rulings and keep their status as jurisdiction-local precedent. s. 18(5) does not reach them. It binds the acmeco court, like every court, prospectively: every bench it constitutes on or after 2026-06-06 must be odd and free of any synthesiser-on-top, and any such future bench that is not is void ab initio.

On the forward conformance obligation, I am deliberately conservative, because the tradition here is to disturb the settled estate as little as the law permits. A legacy repo owes no duty to re-constitute or re-determine its saved rulings; they stand. Its forward duty is threefold and modest: (i) constitute every new bench to s. 10 and s. 18 (odd, no added voice, author drawn from the counted seats); (ii) leave the saved precedent recorded as good-law, neither erased nor silently re-marked, with the prior-machinery provenance noted where it is known so a future reader is not misled about how an old ratio was reached; and (iii) where a saved old ruling is genuinely re-litigated on its merits in a live matter and a party puts its soundness in issue, the matter is heard afresh by a now-properly-constituted bench under s. 18(5)'s re-determination limb, which is restorative (s. 6), not an occasion to void the estate. The saved rulings keep their persuasive and locally-binding force until and unless re-determined in the ordinary way. That, and no more, is the conformance the Acts require.

I diverge from no good-law ruling. I correct one reading of SC-4 pressed by the Defendant: SC-4's "voidable" line was the minority characterisation and is not the ratio; the ratio is void-but-prospective, which is the very thing that saves the past.

**4. RATIO**

1. **A ruling's validity for the purposes of Bill 25 s. 6(1) ("validly-made") is fixed as at the moment the bench was constituted, by reference to the bench-composition standards then in force.** A bench lawful when seated under the standards of its day is "validly-made" and is saved, and its rulings keep their status as jurisdiction-local precedent, even where its composition would not satisfy CASE-LAW s. 18 had it sat after 2026-06-06.

2. **CASE-LAW s. 18(5) operates prospectively, by its own enacting words.** "Void ab initio ... not cured by lapse of time" fixes the depth of the vice (full nullity from clienty, unhealable by delay) for benches within the rule's reach; "enacts the majority rule prospectively" fixes that reach to benches constituted on or after its enactment. The clause does not reach back to void a pre-enactment bench that complied with the entrenched s. 10 standards then in force; to read it so would work a retrospective amendment of the constitutional bench-size article by implication, which Bill 25 s. 2(2) and Bill 15 s. 3 render void.

3. **The forward conformance obligation of a legacy local repo is to apply the new machinery to new matters, not to re-open its saved estate.** It must constitute every bench seated on or after commencement to s. 10 and s. 18; it must leave its saved precedent recorded and unaltered (provenance noted where known); and re-determination under s. 18(5) lies only where a saved ruling is genuinely put in issue in a live matter, the remedy being restorative re-hearing, never erasure.

**5. OBITER**

Had the Principal wished to void the pre-enactment estate, the lawful route was open: an express, deliberate amendment so providing, citing s. 10 and s. 18. The absence of any such words is not an oversight to be repaired by construction; in a realm with the Thoburn rule entrenched, it is dispositive. I would add that the provenance note I require in the forward obligation is a duty of candour, not of correction: it preserves the historian's ability to weigh an old ratio for what it is worth, without unsettling it.

**6. DISPOSITION**

DISMISS, as to the contention that the legacy rulings are void: the County Court at acmeco's CC-ACMECO precedent, including the 1-12 reconstituted entries, is saved as validly-made and stands as jurisdiction-local precedent, with the forward conformance obligation set out in ratio (3).

**7. PROPOSED ENACTMENTS**

No new statute proposed. The settlement is already complete on the face of the Acts and CASE-LAW; this judgment construes the existing provisions in concert and adds nothing the statute book does not already contain. (Were the Court minded to codify, ratio (1) and (2) state the rule sufficiently for a future amendment to s. 18 by way of a transitional note, but I do not think one necessary.)

**8. PROPOSED OVERRULINGS**

No overrulings proposed. [2026] REALM-SC 4 remains good law; I have construed its ratio (void-but-prospective), not disturbed it, and have declined only the Defendant's attempt to elevate its minority "voidable" reasoning into the ratio.

### Coade J

PRELIMINARY. The leapfrog certificate is properly seised: this is a genuinely foundational question on the interaction of the founding case-law settlement with the enacted Acts, and the Court is bound to hear a properly-formed certificate on an arguably foundational point (s. 20(4)). I would refuse the Defendant's strike-out and fast-path motions. The standing point fails because the Principal, acting as Sovereign, has lawful interest in the validity of the realm's own precedent base; and the fast-path motion fails on its own premise, because [2026] REALM-SC 4 (which the Defendant treats as binding ratio on voidability) is per incuriam citation: that judgment's enacted text, s. 18(5), holds the opposite of "voidable" (it holds VOID ab initio), and a brief cannot fast-path a court onto a characterisation the statute enacting the very article displaced. The Declaration of Incompatibility motion (s. 11(f)) does not arise, because the two provisions are reconcilable on a narrow reading, as I now show.

THE QUESTION. As I would frame it, narrowly: when Bill 25 s. 6 saves a "validly-made" prior ruling, and CASE-LAW s. 18(5) declares a ruling by an improperly-constituted bench VOID ab initio and "not cured by lapse of time" while enacting its rule "prospectively," is a pre-enactment local ruling produced by the abolished multi-judge or synthesiser-on-top machinery SAVED or VOID, and what forward conformance does the legacy repo owe. I decline to decide one inch beyond what the acmeco test case requires.

REASONING. I begin where a restrained court should: with the two texts, read so that each is given work and neither is repealed by implication (Bill 25 s. 8; the Amendment Procedure; s. 1).

The decisive move is to separate the STANDARD from the CONSEQUENCE, and then to separate the word "prospectively" from the word "void ab initio." Both briefs collapse these, in opposite directions, and both overreach.

First, "prospectively." The Claimant and Defendant alike read s. 18(5)'s closing parenthetical as a temporal saving for old benches. That reading cannot survive the text. The parenthetical reads: "(The Court divided 5-4 on the characterisation of the impugned ruling, void prevailing; this subsection enacts the majority rule prospectively.)" The subject of "enacts ... prospectively" is the majority RULE, that is, the rule that void prevails over voidable as the characterisation of an improper bench. What was prospective was the Court's choice not to disturb the particular impugned ruling before it on the voidability theory the minority preferred, and to lay down VOID as the realm's rule going forward. "Prospectively" tells us the rule was newly settled at SC-4, not that benches seated before some date are immune. It does not modify "is void ab initio," which is a definitional statement about what an improper bench produces: nothing, from the beginning. The narrowest faithful reading is that "prospectively" governs the date from which the VOID characterisation is the law, not a backward cut-off insulating old defective benches.

Second, "void ab initio ... not cured by ... lapse of time." This is the consequence, and it is definitional, not punitive. A bench that was never odd, or that seated a deciding voice on top of the sized panel, never lawfully constituted a court at all. The output of a non-court is not a ruling that later goes bad; it is a ruling that never came into lawful existence. That is the plain meaning of "ab initio," and s. 18(5) expressly forecloses the three cures a savings-minded reader would reach for: soundness of result, acquiescence, and lapse of time. The Defendant's heavy reliance on Goffe J and Hallam CJ's "voidable" reasoning in SC-4 is, with respect, reliance on the losing argument: SC-4 itself records that the Court divided 5-4 with VOID prevailing, and the enacted article (s. 18(5)) is the majority's, not the minority's. A concurring justice's preference for voidability, however well expressed, is not the ratio and is not the statute. I will not let a brief smuggle the dissent back in as binding law.

So far this points to voidness. But the restrained answer does not stop at the abstract category; it asks WHICH old benches s. 18(5) actually reaches, and here both briefs commit the same overreach in opposite directions. The Claimant says NO old bench is touched (everything saved); the Defendant says the same by a different route. Neither confronts the obvious narrowing fact: not every prior-system ruling was produced by a multi-judge or synthesiser-on-top bench. The pre-Acts regime had a single-judge First Instance tier as well. A single-judge first-instance ruling is, and always was, an ODD, properly-sized, properly-authored bench by the very standard s. 18 enacts. Such a ruling has no constitutional vice at all; it is "validly-made" on any reading, and Bill 25 s. 6 plainly saves it. The vice in s. 18(5) bites only on the narrow class the subsection names: even-numbered deciding sets, or a deciding voice added on top of the sized panel. That is the whole of its reach, and a restrained court should say so and stop.

This is where I converge with, and then trim, the savings case. Bill 25 s. 6 saves "validly-made" rulings. A ruling from a properly-constituted single-judge bench was validly made; it is saved; s. 18(5) does not touch it because there was no improper constitution to begin with. A ruling from an even bench or an Aldermere-on-top bench was, by s. 18(5)'s definition, never validly made; the savings qualifier "validly-made" is the hinge, and it excludes exactly that ruling by its own terms. The two statutes do not collide. They interlock: s. 6 saves the valid, s. 18(5) defines a narrow class as never-valid, and the word "validly-made" in s. 6 is precisely the carve that lets s. 18(5) operate without repealing the savings. There is no implied amendment of the entrenched s. 10 (Bill 25 s. 2(2); Bill 15 s. 3), because s. 18(5) does not change what a lawful bench WAS; it states that an unlawful bench never produced a ruling, which was as true before commencement as after. The "validly-made" reading harmonises both texts and is, for that reason, the one the Amendment Procedure compels.

I therefore reject the Claimant's broad thesis that ALL old rulings are saved regardless of bench (it reads "validly-made" out of s. 6 and treats "void ab initio" as a dead letter), and I reject any reading that would void the WHOLE acmeco base (which overreaches in the other direction, voiding sound single-judge rulings that never offended s. 18). The correct disposition is granular: a CC-ACMECO entry reconstituted from a prior LEXBY-era ruling is void only if, and to the extent that, the underlying prior ruling was produced by an even bench or a synthesiser-on-top bench. The record before us does not tell us which of CC-ACMECO 1-12 had which provenance; that is a finding of fact, repo by ruling, not a question this Court should resolve in the abstract.

On consequence and remedy I agree with both briefs and with s. 6: the remedy is restorative, never punitive, and never retrospective erasure. A void entry is not deleted; under Bill 16 s. 9(3) and Bill 20 it keeps its citation handle and is marked void in the citator (the REALM-CA 2 precedent in the central citator is the exact template: retained for the record, status void, nothing binds), and the underlying matter is re-determined by a properly-constituted single-judge County Court at the repo if and when it is live. Nothing built in reliance is "retrospectively groundless" in any practical sense the Defendant fears: re-determination by a proper bench will, in the ordinary case, reach the same result, because s. 18(5)'s vice is constitutional, not substantive, and a sound result re-reached binds afresh. The Defendant's "catastrophic downstream" parade is answered by the restorative remedy, not by saving void benches.

On the forward conformance obligation I would say only what acmeco needs. A legacy repo owes no duty to re-constitute or re-litigate sound rulings; it owes (i) a duty to apply the new bench machinery to all NEW matters (s. 18(6), s. 19), and (ii) a duty, when a void-provenance entry is actually relied upon or challenged, to mark it void in the citator and re-determine the underlying matter before a proper bench. There is no free-standing duty to audit and re-open the entire back-catalogue on commencement; that would be punitive busywork the restorative-remedy principle (s. 6) does not authorise, and s. 7/s. 8 impose only a forward duty to remediate a KNOWN hazard, not to hunt cured-by-soundness history. The duty crystallises on reliance or challenge, not on the calendar.

I diverge from the absent lower court only in that there is none; I engage the briefs as the reasoning under review, and I have said where each overreaches.

RATIO.
1. CASE-LAW s. 18(5)'s declaration that a ruling by an improperly-constituted bench (even-numbered deciding set, or a deciding voice added on top of the sized panel) is VOID ab initio is definitional and reaches a pre-enactment ruling produced by such a bench; the word "prospectively" fixes the date from which VOID (rather than voidable) is the realm's settled characterisation, and does not insulate pre-enactment improper benches. Such a ruling was never "validly-made" within Bill 25 s. 6 and is not saved; a properly-constituted single-judge or properly-sized prior bench produced a validly-made ruling that s. 18(5) does not touch and Bill 25 s. 6 fully saves. The two provisions interlock through the "validly-made" qualifier and neither impliedly amends the other.
2. The consequence of voidness is restorative, not retrospective erasure: the void entry retains its citation handle, is marked VOID in the citator (Bill 16 s. 9(3); Bill 20), and the underlying matter is re-determined by a properly-constituted court if and when it is live; a legacy repo's only forward conformance duties are to constitute every NEW bench under s. 18/s. 19 and to mark-void-and-re-determine a void-provenance entry upon reliance or challenge, with no free-standing duty to re-open the sound back-catalogue.

OBITER. I note, without deciding, that whether any particular CC-ACMECO 1-12 entry is void turns on a provenance finding (was the underlying LEXBY-era ruling an even or synthesiser-on-top bench), which is for the County Court at acmeco on the facts, not for this Court in the abstract.

DISPOSITION. VARY: the legacy acmeco precedent is neither wholly saved nor wholly void; each entry reconstituted from an improperly-constituted prior bench is void and re-determinable, while every validly-constituted prior ruling is saved, and the repo's forward duty is to use the new machinery and to mark-and-re-determine void entries only on reliance or challenge.

PROPOSED ENACTMENTS. No new statute proposed. The existing texts, read narrowly through the "validly-made" qualifier, resolve the question without fresh legislation.

PROPOSED OVERRULINGS. No overrulings proposed. I would not disturb [2026] REALM-SC 4; I would only confirm that its enacted ratio is the VOID characterisation (s. 18(5)), and that the "voidable" reasoning in the SC-4 concurrences is persuasive minority obiter, not binding ratio, and cannot be fast-pathed as such.

**OPINION OF STEYNE J**

**1. PRELIMINARY**

The leapfrog certificate is properly seised: the conformance status of an entire jurisdiction-repo's precedent base under the newly-commenced Acts is foundational on any view (s.20(2)), and it is express, recorded, and reasoned. I would refuse the defendant's strike-out for want of standing (the realm has a manifest interest in the validity of its own local precedent on a constitutional reference) and I would refuse the fast-path motion (the s.6 / s.18(5) interaction is genuine first-impression, not a ratio on all fours: [2026] REALM-SC 4 settled the rule going forward, not its temporal reach against pre-enactment work).

**2. THE QUESTION AS I FRAME IT**

Does the entrenched-grade voidness of s.18(5) reach backward through Bill 25 s.6's savings to nullify, ab initio, rulings produced by local benches that were lawfully constituted under the standards in force when they sat? Or are those rulings "validly-made" within s.6 and therefore saved, with s.18(5) biting only benches seated after its enactment? And what conformance obligation does the acmeco County Court bear going forward?

**3. REASONING**

I begin where an honest reading must begin: with the two texts that are said to collide, and with the discipline that they were enacted by the same sovereign hand at the same commencement and must, if it is fairly possible, be read to stand together (s.1; Bill 25 s.8). I do not reach for collision where concord is available. Here concord is not merely available; it is the more faithful reading of both instruments. I part company with any colleague who treats this as a true antinomy to be resolved by ranking one provision over the other.

**The pivot is the meaning of "validly-made," and it must be measured at the time of making.** The defendant and claimant briefs converge on the answer, and they are right. A ruling is "made" at a moment in time, by a bench, under a body of constitutional law then in force. Validity of constitution is not a free-floating timeless property; it is a question asked of a bench against the standard that governed its seating. When the acmeco benches sat, the standard that governed them was the prior machinery, which then authorised multi-judge first instance and the synthesiser-on-top form. By the law of their own era they were lawfully constituted. They were therefore "validly-made" within the only sense that phrase can bear when read against a transitional savings clause: validly made *under the law in force when made*. Bill 25 s.6 saves them on their own terms.

I do not accept the contrary reading that "validly-made" silently incorporates the *future* standard of s.18, so that a bench is "validly-made" only if it would satisfy a rule enacted after it rose. That reading does not interpret the savings clause; it empties it. A savings clause whose protection is forfeited by every subsequent change of standard saves nothing. The whole office of a transitional savings provision is to hold settled work steady across exactly the kind of standard-change that s.18 effected. To read "validly-made" as "valid under all later law" is to read Bill 25 s.6 out of the statute book at the moment of its commencement. That is not a permissible construction.

**The word PROSPECTIVELY in s.18(5) is not decoration, and it is not confined to the remedy.** The parenthetical reads: "this subsection enacts the majority rule prospectively." The subject of that sentence is *the rule of this subsection*, taken whole, the rule that an improper bench is void ab initio. The natural and grammatical reading is that the whole rule operates forward. I reject the strained alternative that "prospectively" governs only the court's downstream obligation to re-determine while the voidness reaches backward; that reading severs a single declared temporal anchor and applies half of it to the past and half to the future, which is not how a court reads a deliberate enactment. The drafters of s.18, drawn from [2026] REALM-SC 4, knew the difference between a rule that reaches back and one that operates forward, and they said forward.

**"Void ab initio" and "not cured by lapse of time" are about *which moment* voids the bench, not *how far back in calendar time* the rule reaches.** This is the point on which I am willing to be the most explicit, because it is where the voidness brief's strongest argument lives and where it must be honestly met rather than waved away. "Void ab initio" means that *where the rule applies*, the vice exists from the bench's first breath rather than arising only when a court later impugns it; it fixes the defect at the seating, not at the setting-aside. "Not cured by lapse of time" means that *for a bench the rule reaches*, the passage of years and accumulated reliance do not launder the defect. Both phrases describe the *character and incurability* of the vice for benches within the rule's reach. Neither phrase answers the prior, threshold question: *which benches does the rule reach at all?* That question is answered by the temporal anchor, and the anchor says prospectively. So the two phrases and the prospectivity clause are not in tension once the work each does is properly assigned: prospectivity fixes the rule's *reach* (benches seated on or after 2026-06-06); "void ab initio" and "not cured by lapse of time" fix the *quality* of the vice for the benches so reached. A future improper bench is void from its first moment and cannot be saved by anyone sitting on it for years; that is a real and necessary bite. It is simply not a bite that travels backward.

**There is a further, constitutional reason the backward reading must fail, and I would rest on it independently.** Bench size is fixed by s.10, an *entrenched* article. s.18(5) is an ordinary article. To read s.18(5) as retroactively unmaking every pre-enactment bench that did not meet the *new* odd-bench composition is to give an ordinary article the effect of reaching back and rewriting the constitutional settlement of s.10 as it stood in the prior era, by implication, against work already completed under it. The Amendment Procedure and Bill 15 forbid amendment of entrenched articles by implication. A retroactive s.18(5) would be exactly that: an implied, backward amendment of the constitutional bench rule. The defendant brief is right on this, and it is dispositive. The constitution will not let an ordinary article do retrospectively what it could not do to an entrenched article even prospectively without express words.

**Now I come to where I diverge, and I do so deliberately, in keeping with the duty to be candid about the law's edges.** The voidness brief leans on [2026] REALM-SC 4 (Goffe J, Hallam CJ) for the proposition that the bench-constitution defect renders a ruling *voidable*, valid until impugned, rather than void ab initio. I will not adopt that as the ratio of this Court, because the enacted text of s.18(5) plainly says "void ab initio," and I am bound by the statute, not by the strand of the underlying judgment that the enacted text declined to follow. The Court in REALM-SC 4 divided 5-4 and *void prevailed*; s.18(5) enacts the void rule, not the voidable one. So I do not save the legacy rulings on a voidable theory. I save them on the cleaner and more durable ground that the void rule, by its own terms, *does not reach them at all*. This matters: it means a *future* acmeco bench that sits even-numbered is not "voidable until someone complains," it is void from its first moment, full stop. The protection of the legacy rulings is temporal (the rule does not reach back), not a softening of the rule's quality (which remains absolute going forward). That is the firmer landing, and it is the one I will stand the law on.

**The supporting instruments all point the same way.** Bill 16 s.9(3) and the CITATION-MAP save every legacy citation as a valid handle and orphan no ruling; a wholesale backward voiding would orphan CC-ACMECO 1-12 in defiance of that saving. Bill 25 s.7(1) ratchets transitions only in a party's favour and forbids loss of standing, tier, or remedy by commencement alone; retroactive nullification of a repo's settled precedent is the paradigm of a loss inflicted by commencement alone. And the object of Bill 25 (s.3, s.8) is preservation across relabelling, not silent destruction. I read all of these as confirmatory, not as the load-bearing reasons; the load-bearing reasons are the meaning of "validly-made" and the entrenchment bar on implied retroactive constitutional amendment.

**The forward conformance obligation.** Saving the legacy rulings does not freeze acmeco in the old machinery. From commencement, every *new* matter at the County Court at acmeco is constituted under s.18 and the rest of the Acts without exception: odd benches, one counted author, no synthesiser-on-top, the s.19(5) citation-integrity gate. The legacy ratios remain good-law jurisdiction-local precedent (binding within acmeco, persuasive elsewhere until the apex elevates a ratio under s.9) and need no reconstitution and no re-determination *merely because* the bench that made them would now be unlawful. Trust status under Bill 20 is a deterministic fact in git, not a re-litigation trigger; a saved ruling is not QUARANTINED for the historical bench form alone. Two carve-outs only, and they are forward duties, not retroactive voids: (a) if a *specific* legacy ratio is independently impugned on a live ground that the Acts make available (per incuriam, an unmoored extension under s.17, irreconcilability with CASE-LAW under s.11(f)), it is dealt with on that ground in the ordinary way, the remedy restorative (s.6); and (b) the repo carries the s.8 forward duty to apply the new machinery to everything it does from now. That is the whole of the obligation. No mass reconstitution. No backward erasure.

**4. RATIO**

1. **"Validly-made" in Bill 25 s.6(1) is measured at the time of making: a prior ruling whose bench was lawfully constituted under the standards in force when it sat is "validly-made" and is SAVED, and does not forfeit that status because a later-enacted standard would have rendered the same bench unlawful.**

2. **CASE-LAW s.18(5) operates PROSPECTIVELY by its own terms: the temporal anchor fixes the rule's REACH to benches constituted on or after its enactment (2026-06-06), while "void ab initio" and "not cured by lapse of time" fix the QUALITY and incurability of the vice for benches so reached; the subsection does not void a pre-enactment bench that was proper under the law of its own era, and to read it as doing so would be an impermissible implied retroactive amendment of the entrenched bench rule (s.10), void under the Amendment Procedure and Bill 15.**

3. **A legacy jurisdiction-repo bears a PROSPECTIVE conformance obligation only: it constitutes every new matter under s.18 and the Acts going forward, but reconstitutes and re-determines no saved legacy ruling merely on account of the historical bench form; a specific legacy ratio is disturbed only if independently impugned on a live ground (per incuriam, s.17, s.11(f)), the remedy being restorative (s.6).**

**5. OBITER**

The cleaner doctrinal frame for cases of this kind is to keep two questions separate that advocates persistently blur: *reach* (does the rule apply to this bench at all?) and *quality* (if it applies, how grave and how curable is the vice?). Many apparent statutory collisions dissolve once a court refuses to let a "quality" phrase ("void ab initio," "not cured by lapse of time") smuggle in an answer to the "reach" question that the enacting words have separately and expressly settled.

**6. DISPOSITION**

**DISMISS** the case for backward voidness: the acmeco County Court's legacy rulings (CC-ACMECO 1-12 included) are saved as validly-made prior rulings, s.18(5) bites only benches constituted on or after commencement, and the repo's sole obligation is prospective conformance.

**7. PROPOSED ENACTMENTS**

No new statute proposed. The result follows from Bill 25 s.6, s.18(5) read on its own prospectivity, and the entrenchment bar; the matter is one of construction, and a fresh article would risk freezing as statute what is already settled by faithful reading. I would record this ratio as the binding interpretation and add the citator row, no more.

**8. PROPOSED OVERRULINGS**

No overrulings proposed. [2026] REALM-SC 4 stands; I do not adopt its voidable strand as ratio, but the enacted s.18(5) (void prevailing) and this Court's prospectivity holding are reconciled without disturbing it.

OPINION OF BOWAN J

IN THE SUPREME COURT OF THE VIBE JUSTICE SYSTEM, sitting as the full bench of nine for a constitutional and foundational question, upon the Principal's express leapfrog certificate (VPR 4, CASE-LAW s. 13, s. 20).

**1. PRELIMINARY**

The certificate is express, recorded, and reasoned, and the question (the conformance of an entire jurisdiction's precedent base under the Acts of the Realm) is foundational on no reasonable view to the contrary; I am satisfied we are properly seised (s. 20(2), (4)). I record one matter of candour at the outset: in [2026] REALM-SC 4 I sat in the minority of four who would have held the impugned even-bench ruling voidable rather than void. I lost that argument. The court enacted s. 18(5) with "void ab initio" as the binding rule. I do not re-fight here a battle the record closed against me; my task today is to apply the law as it stands, not as I would have written it. That discipline is itself the answer to half of this case.

**2. THE QUESTION AS I FRAME IT**

The leapfrog poses the interaction as a binary: are pre-enactment local rulings made under the old machinery SAVED by Bill 25 s. 6, or VOID by CASE-LAW s. 18(5)? I reject the binary. It is a false dilemma built on a shared error both briefs commit, and the destruction of that false dilemma is the whole case. The true questions are three: (i) does s. 18(5) reach pre-enactment benches at all, given its express prospectivity; (ii) what does it mean that Bill 25 s. 6 saves only the "validly-made"; and (iii) on this record, has it been found as a fact that any particular CC-ACMECO ruling was delivered by a bench of the prohibited kind?

**3. REASONING**

**3.1 Both briefs quote a mutilated statute. The dispositive subsection is Bill 25 s. 6(2), and neither side put it before the court.** The case file reproduces Bill 25 s. 6(1) and stops. The Act does not stop there. Section 6(2) reads, in terms I have verified against the enacted text (Legislature/.../25-commencement-and-transitional-provisions-act-2026.md, s. 6(2)): "'Validly-made' is qualified by the existing validity rules and saves nothing decided per incuriam (CASE-LAW s. 11(e)) or void ab initio (s. 18(5)); such matters take their existing status and are not revived by this section." That is an express carve-out, naming s. 18(5) by number. The entire contest the leapfrog stages, "does 'validly-made' in s. 6(1) reach back through 'void ab initio'?", is answered by the very next subsection, which the drafter put there precisely so that no court would have to guess. A proposition that dissolves on reading the statute one line further than counsel did is not a genuine tension; it is a research failure. I would dispose of the framing on that ground alone. But because the deeper questions survive even after s. 6(2) is read, I press on, and I press hard, because s. 6(2) is a double-edged instrument that cuts the Voidness brief as deeply as it cuts Savings.

**3.2 The savings clause is not the operative question, because s. 18(5) does not reach these benches in the first place. Here I test the Voidness brief to destruction.** The Voidness brief, and the closing "ANSWER" appended to the Defendant brief, both treat s. 18(5) as a free-standing vice that, once the prohibition exists, reaches back to void every bench that ever offended the standard, "not cured by lapse of time." That reading cannot survive its own statute. Section 18(5) closes with the words: "this subsection enacts the majority rule **prospectively**." The Savings brief is right that "prospectively" is the temporal anchor of the subsection; but it draws the wrong frontier from it, and the Voidness brief reads the word out of the statute entirely. Let me state the frontier exactly, because both sides have it wrong.

"Prospectively" governs the **enactment of the rule**, not merely the remedy. The rule s. 18(5) enacts is: *a bench constituted in violation of the odd-number / no-synthesiser-on-top requirement is void ab initio.* That rule came into existence on 2026-06-05 (REALM-SC 4) and was carried forward by the Acts on 2026-06-06. Before that moment there was no enacted requirement that a first-instance hearing-centre sit an odd deciding set, and no enacted vice of "improper constitution" at all. The prohibition on even benches and synthesisers-on-top is *itself* the creature of s. 18; it did not exist as positive law in the prior-system era. To say a prior-system bench was "improperly constituted" is therefore to apply a 2026-06-05 standard to a 2024-or-2025 act. That is precisely what "this subsection enacts the majority rule prospectively" forbids. The words "void ab initio ... not cured by lapse of time" describe how the vice operates *once it has attached to a bench the subsection reaches* (a bench seated on or after commencement): for such a bench, voidness runs from the beginning of *that* ruling and is not laundered by the passage of time or by acquiescence. The phrase is about the *depth* of the vice in time within a ruling the rule governs; it is not a grant of retroactive *reach* to rulings the rule never governed. Read the Voidness brief's way, the word "prospectively" would do no work at all, and a court does not read an enacted word out of a statute the Sovereign deliberately placed there (the no-surplusage canon; and Bill 15, the Interpretation Act, forbids treating enacted words as idle).

**3.3 The constitutional clincher, which converts a strong reading into a necessary one.** The Defendant brief's best point, buried under its weaker ones, is the implied-amendment argument, and on testing it holds. CASE-LAW s. 10 is **entrenched**. It fixes the bench sizes and (by the conforming note) their odd, exhaustive character. s. 18 is an *ordinary* article that "elaborates and implements" the entrenched s. 10. Now suppose s. 18(5) reached backward to void every prior bench that did not meet the s. 10 standard. The prior-system multi-judge first-instance machinery and the synthesiser-on-top pattern were the *then-lawful constitution* of those courts; to declare their every output void ab initio is to hold that s. 10's odd-bench requirement was *already binding law before it was enacted*, governing benches seated when it did not exist. That is a retroactive amendment of the entrenched s. 10's temporal scope, worked by implication through an ordinary article. The Amendment Procedure, Bill 15 s. 3 and s. 8, and Bill 25 s. 2(2) each forbid exactly that: an entrenched article changes only by express, deliberate amendment citing it by number, never by implication, and "any provision that would do so by implication is void to that extent." s. 18(5) on the retroactive reading would be void to the extent of that reach. The only construction that keeps s. 18(5) intra vires is the prospective one its own last sentence states. So I do not merely *prefer* the prospective reading; the constitution *compels* it. A retroactive s. 18(5) is self-voiding.

**3.4 Now I turn the same blade on the Savings brief, because its conclusion is right for reasons that are largely wrong, and a future court must not inherit its errors as ratio.** The Savings brief argues the legacy rulings are "validly-made" and therefore *positively saved* by s. 6(1). That overclaims, and s. 6(2) is the reason. s. 6(2) tells us the savings clause "saves nothing ... void ab initio (s. 18(5))." Suppose, *arguendo*, a particular legacy ruling *were* one to which s. 18(5) attached: s. 6 would not save it; s. 6(2) would expressly exclude it. The Savings brief cannot have it both ways. Its security lies not in s. 6 affirmatively *saving* the rulings, but in s. 18(5) *never reaching them*, so that the s. 6(2) carve-out has nothing to bite. The distinction is not academic. If validity rested on s. 6(1)'s "validly-made," then the moment anyone established as a fact that a given prior bench was even-numbered, the s. 6(2) carve-out would swallow it and the ruling would fall. On the correct analysis, that fact is *irrelevant*, because s. 18(5) is prospective and a pre-commencement bench cannot be "improperly constituted" within the meaning of a rule that did not yet exist. The legacy rulings are valid because the voidness rule does not reach them, full stop. I would make that the ratio and refuse the Savings brief's broader and more dangerous reasoning.

**3.5 The Savings brief's reliance on Bill 16 s. 9(3) ("no ruling is orphaned") is makeweight and I would not rest a hair on it.** A valid handle to a void ruling is still a handle to a void ruling; Bill 16 s. 9(3) preserves *citations*, not *validity* (it is a citation-form savings clause, expressly "changes only the non-operative label"). To argue, as Savings does, that the citation savings prove the ruling survives is to confuse the label with the thing. I reject it. So too the appeal to "absurdity": absurdity is not a canon that licences a court to read "prospectively" out of a statute; it is at most a tie-breaker, and here there is no tie to break because the text and the entrenchment bar already settle it.

**3.6 The found-fact point, which both briefs sail past and which is the real safeguard.** Even on the Voidness brief's own (rejected) retroactive theory, voidness under s. 18(5) attaches only to a bench whose *deciding set was even* or *to which a deciding voice was added on top of the sized panel*. That is a question of fact about a particular bench. The leapfrog asserts, in the abstract, that "several of those LEXBY-era rulings were produced by the OLD LDD machinery." But I have examined the acmeco local citator (the County Court at acmeco INDEX.md). It records CC-ACMECO 1-12 as reconstituted from prior-system citations, with statuses *carried forward*: CC-ACMECO 2 is already recorded **per-incuriam** (a distinct s. 11(e) vice, not a constitution vice), and CC-ACMECO 1 and 3-12 as **good-law**. Nothing in this record establishes, as to any single CC-ACMECO ruling, the operative fact that *it* was decided by an even deciding set or by a synthesiser cast as a supernumerary deciding voice. A County Court sits one judge (court.yaml level=county-court); a single-judge first-instance ruling is the paradigm of a *correctly* constituted bench and could never offend s. 18, whatever era produced it. The prior-system "multi-judge first instance" was struck by s. 12 as bloat, but a *first-instance* matter that in fact sat one judge has no constitution vice at all. I would therefore hold, additionally and independently, that no voidness finding can be made on this record against any particular acmeco ruling, because the predicate fact has not been pleaded or found. Voidness under s. 18(5) is not a presumption that arises from the mere age of a citation; it is a finding of fact on a specific bench, and the burden lies on the party asserting it.

**3.7 The conformance obligation going forward.** This is the part of the question that is genuinely useful to settle. A legacy hearing-centre bears no duty to *re-constitute* or *re-determine* its good-law precedent: that would be retroactive voidness through the back door, and s. 6, s. 7(1) (transitions ratchet only in the party's favour), and the entrenchment bar all forbid it. Its only forward duty (CASE-LAW s. 7, s. 8) is the ordinary one: *new* matters, from commencement onward, are heard by a bench constituted under s. 10 and s. 18, and the runnable machinery must seat the sized odd bench (s. 18(6)). As to its records, Bill 20 governs and is dispositive of the "repo conformance" limb: a repo's trust status is a *deterministic fact* (s. 19(5) gate plus git), never an office's certificate; the acmeco DB is a CONFORMANT-PROJECTION / DERIVED-INDEX over the committed markdown, never AUTHORITATIVE-SOURCE. The conformance obligation is thus discharged by the projection discipline already enacted, not by any re-litigation. If, in some future matter, a *specific* legacy ruling is shown on the facts to have been delivered by a bench of the prohibited kind, the remedy is the restorative one s. 18(5) itself names: re-determination of *that matter* by a properly constituted court, with reversible reliance in the interim protected (s. 17(c)), never wholesale erasure of the series. I note that s. 18(5)'s prospectivity means even that contingency is narrow: it bites a bench seated after commencement, so a genuinely pre-commencement even bench is reached, if at all, only on the retroactive reading I have rejected.

**4. RATIO**

(1) **CASE-LAW s. 18(5) is prospective in reach, not merely in remedy: it voids a bench *constituted on or after its commencement* in violation of the odd-bench / no-synthesiser-on-top requirement, and does not reach back to characterise as "improperly constituted" a bench seated under the prior-system standards when no such requirement was enacted law. The closing words "void ab initio ... not cured by lapse of time" fix the depth of the vice within a ruling the subsection governs; they confer no retroactive reach. The contrary reading is void as an amendment by implication of the entrenched s. 10 (Amendment Procedure; Bill 15 s. 3, s. 8; Bill 25 s. 2(2)).**

(2) **A pre-commencement local ruling is valid not because Bill 25 s. 6(1) affirmatively saves it, but because s. 18(5) never reaches it; Bill 25 s. 6(2) (which excludes from the savings anything "void ab initio (s. 18(5))") therefore has nothing on which to operate. Voidness under s. 18(5) is a finding of fact on a specific bench (even deciding set, or a deciding voice added on top), on which the asserting party bears the burden; it does not arise from the age or provenance-reconstitution of a citation. A legacy hearing-centre's only forward conformance duty is to constitute *new* benches under s. 10 and s. 18 and to keep its registers as derived projections under Bill 20; it owes no duty to re-constitute or re-determine its good-law precedent.**

**5. OBITER**

I observe, without deciding, that the leapfrog certificate would have been better directed to First Instance or framed as a request to confirm a fast-path, since on analysis the controlling provision (Bill 25 s. 6(2)) is express and the residue is a single point of statutory construction that the entrenchment bar all but pre-decides. That the matter nonetheless reached us did no harm, the question being foundational in form, but I record that a court should be slow to treat as a "genuine first-impression tension" a conflict that evaporates on reading the operative statute in full. Counsel on both sides researched the wrong nine-tenths of the Act. I also observe that the two adversarial briefs reached the *same* destination (validity) by opposite and largely defective routes, which is itself a caution: a symmetric record is only as good as the law both sides actually read.

**6. DISPOSITION**

DISMISS the suggestion that the acmeco precedent base is void, and DECLARE that the CC-ACMECO rulings are validly constituted and conformant under the Acts because s. 18(5) is prospective and does not reach pre-commencement benches, with re-determination available only on a found fact, in a specific future matter, that a particular bench was of the prohibited kind.

**7. PROPOSED ENACTMENTS**

No new statute proposed. The result is delivered by the existing text correctly construed (CASE-LAW s. 18(5) last sentence; Bill 25 s. 6(2); the Amendment Procedure). I would resist enacting a fresh article: the surest way to corrupt a clean rule is to over-state it, and a transitional point self-limited by its own statute needs no monument. If the court wishes a durable handle, a one-line declaratory note appended to the s. 18 record (that s. 18(5) reaches only benches constituted on or after 2026-06-05 and requires a found fact of improper constitution) would suffice and I would not oppose it.

**8. PROPOSED OVERRULINGS**

No overrulings proposed. [2026] REALM-SC 4 stands undisturbed: its ratio that an improperly constituted bench is void ab initio is good law and is the very rule I apply; I hold only that, by its own terms, that rule operates prospectively. Nothing in the acmeco citator (including CC-ACMECO 2, recorded per-incuriam under the distinct s. 11(e) vice) is overruled; each entry keeps its existing status under Bill 25 s. 6 and Bill 16 s. 13.

**IN THE SUPREME COURT OF THE VIBE JUSTICE SYSTEM**
**[2026] LEXBY-SC 1 (full court of 9; constitutional sitting on the Principal's leapfrog certificate)**

**OPINION OF ALDERMERE J**

---

**1. PRELIMINARY**

The certificate is properly issued under s.20: the question is genuinely foundational (it goes to the validity of the realm's entire body of legacy local precedent and the conformance duty of every jurisdiction-repo), it is express, recorded, and reasoned, so I would decline the Defendant's motion to remit. I would also decline the Defendant's two threshold motions, and I explain why below, because the reasons bear on the merits.

**2. THE QUESTION**

As I frame it, the question is not "are the old rulings good or bad," but how two simultaneously-commenced provisions of equal moment - the savings clause (Bill 25 s.6) and the void-ab-initio bench-constitution clause (CASE-LAW s.18(5)) - operate together upon a ruling produced by the now-abolished old machinery (multi-judge first instance and the "Aldermere-on-top" synthesiser pattern). Three sub-questions follow: (a) does "validly-made" in s.6 measure validity at the time of making or against the s.18 standard; (b) does "void ab initio ... not cured by lapse of time" reach backward through the savings; and (c) what prospective conformance obligation a legacy repo such as the County Court at acmeco bears.

**3. REASONING**

I begin where the office of this court requires me to begin: by trying to find the reading the whole bench can stand behind, because two enacted instruments of equal date are presumed to cohere, not to cannibalise one another (Bill 25 s.8: statute prevails only "to the extent of conflict"). I am persuaded that such a reading exists, and that both adversarial briefs, by treating this as a binary "saved or void," have each captured one true half and missed the join.

*The Defendant's fast-path motion fails, and its failure is instructive.* The Defendant invites me to dispose on [2026] REALM-SC 4 (the bench-constitution judgment), citing Hallam CJ and Goffe J for "voidable, not void." But that is precisely the position the bench did **not** adopt. Section 18(5) as enacted records that "the Court divided 5-4 on the characterisation of the impugned ruling, **void prevailing**." The Defendant's authorities are the dissent on characterisation. The enacted text - "VOID ab initio ... not cured by ... acquiescence, or by lapse of time" - is the **majority** rule and is now sovereign statute (CASE-LAW s.1). I cannot read the losing characterisation back into the winning text. The fast-path is therefore unavailable, and so is the declaration of incompatibility: there is no irreconcilable conflict to refer up, because the two provisions can be reconciled, as I now show.

*The two readings each prove too much.* The Claimant says "prospectively" governs the whole subsection, so s.18(5) bites only benches **seated** after 2026-06-06; every old bench is therefore untouchable. That reading drains the words "not cured by ... lapse of time" of all force, because on the Claimant's view lapse of time could never be in issue - the only benches ever caught would be future ones. The Defendant (Voidness brief, conceded point) says "void ab initio" plus "not cured by lapse of time" reaches back to nullify every improperly-constituted bench ever seated, which would silently void the bulk of the realm's foundational precedent (CC-ACMECO 1-12 among it) and would, by Bill 25 s.7(1), strip parties of standing and remedy by commencement alone - the very thing Bill 25 forbids. Each reading sacrifices an enacted provision to save the other. That is the one outcome the interpretive rule disallows.

*The join is the distinction between the standard and the consequence, mapped onto the distinction between validity-at-formation and the durability of the vice.* I read the provisions as follows, and I believe this is the formulation that holds the whole tension.

First, "validly-made" in Bill 25 s.6 is **not** a self-referential time-of-making test as the Claimant urges. A test that asks only "did the bench satisfy whatever the machinery then asserted" would save a bench that was even-numbered, or that seated a deciding voice on top of the panel, merely because the old harness permitted it. But s.18 did not invent the odd-bench principle from nothing on 2026-06-06; the **constitutional** source is s.10, and s.10's conforming note ([2026] LEXBY-SC 3) declares the odd, no-seat-added rule to be the meaning of the bench-size articles - a meaning, on ordinary constitutional principle, the articles always bore even where the old machinery failed to honour it. So a multi-judge first-instance bench or an Aldermere-on-top synthesis was **never** validly constituted, because s.10 (constitutional, anterior to all of this) never authorised it. "Validly-made" therefore excludes it. To that extent the Defendant's Voidness analysis is correct and the Claimant's time-of-making gloss must be rejected.

Second - and this is where I part company with a flat voidness holding - what s.18(5) does **prospectively** is fix the **consequence and its operation**: that the vice is now declared incurable, anterior to per incuriam, automatic, and not salved by reliance or time, **going forward from enactment**. The parenthetical "this subsection enacts the majority rule prospectively" is not surplus and is not confined to the remedy of re-determination; it tells us the **regime of automatic, self-executing nullity** operates from commencement. Before that date there was no such self-executing statutory nullity; the constitutional defect existed but its **legal effect** on a logged, relied-upon ruling was not yet crystallised into the s.18(5) automatic-void machinery. This is the reconciliation: the old benches were **not validly made** (s.6 does not save them as good law), **but** their nullity is not self-executing and does not erase the record or the citations; it must be **declared and the matter re-determined** by a properly constituted court (the express s.18(5) remedy, which is forward-looking and restorative, s.6).

This reading honours every instrument. It honours s.18(5)'s emphatic text: the vice **is** anterior, **is** not cured by acquiescence or lapse of time - meaning no acmeco litigant can defend an old improper-bench ruling by saying "it has stood for months, leave it." It honours Bill 25 s.6: nothing is **reopened, voided wholesale, or orphaned** on commencement; the citations survive (Bill 16 s.9(3), "no ruling is orphaned"; the CITATION-MAP), the status is preserved **until a competent court acts**, and the transition reopens "no settled matter" of its own motion. It honours Bill 25 s.7(1): no party loses standing or remedy **by reason only of commencement** - they lose it, if at all, only on a re-determination that re-decides the substance, which is a forward act giving the party a properly-constituted hearing. And it honours the Amendment Procedure and Bill 15 s.3 / Bill 25 s.2(2): I do not read s.18(5) as a silent retroactive amendment of s.10, because s.10 always bore this meaning; s.18(5) supplies only the prospective enforcement machinery.

*Per incuriam is the wrong tool, and the briefs that lean on it stray.* The Defendant argues that because s.18(5) is "anterior to and distinct from per incuriam," and per incuriam is the only automatic-void route, an old bench can only be voided by express retroactive amendment. I reject the syllogism. The anteriorness language is, as the Defendant elsewhere concedes, **doctrinal not temporal**: it tells us the constitution-of-the-bench defect is a different **species** of defect from reasoning-in-ignorance. It does not follow that the only operative route is retroactive amendment; the operative route is the one s.18(5) itself names - declaration and re-determination by a proper court, operating from commencement.

*Which old rulings are caught, and which are not.* It matters greatly that not every legacy ruling was made by an improper bench. The old standard included **single-judge first instance**, which was and is lawful (s.10). A legacy first-instance ruling decided by one judge is validly made and is squarely **saved** by Bill 25 s.6; it is good law and binds locally until displaced by ordinary means. Only those legacy rulings actually produced by (i) a multi-judge first-instance bench, (ii) an even-numbered panel, or (iii) the Aldermere-on-top synthesiser pattern (s.18(4)), are constitutionally unsound. The acmeco citator's CC-ACMECO 1-12, reconstituted from LEXBY-era citations, must therefore be **triaged**, not condemned or absolved in the bulk. This triage is the concrete content of the repo's prospective conformance obligation, and it is light: identify on the face of the record which legacy rulings were improperly constituted, mark them with the closed-vocabulary status the records regime supplies, and re-determine only those that a live matter actually relies upon. There is no duty to re-litigate a void ruling that nothing turns on; a void ruling is simply not available to be cited as binding, and the citator carries that status truthfully (Bill 20; Bill 16 status vocabulary).

I diverge from the Claimant only in declining to call the old improper benches "validly made and saved," and I diverge from the Defendant's voidness wing only in declining to make the nullity self-executing and retroactive in effect. The synthesis is: **not saved as good law, but not erased either - declared and re-determined, prospectively, on a triage that costs the repo almost nothing and reopens nothing of its own motion.**

**4. RATIO**

1. **"Validly-made" (Bill 25 s.6) is measured against the constitutional bench-constitution requirement of CASE-LAW s.10 (as conformed by [2026] LEXBY-SC 3), not against whatever the superseded machinery asserted.** A legacy ruling produced by an improperly constituted bench - multi-judge first instance, an even panel, or a synthesiser seated on top of the sized panel (s.18(4)) - was never validly made and is not saved by the savings clause; a legacy ruling decided by a properly constituted bench (including lawful single-judge first instance) was validly made and is saved, retaining its status and binding locally until displaced by ordinary means.

2. **The nullity worked by CASE-LAW s.18(5) is automatic, incurable, and self-executing only PROSPECTIVELY from commencement (2026-06-06); as to pre-commencement improper benches it is declaratory, not self-executing and not retroactively erasing.** Such a legacy ruling is not good law and may not be cited as binding, but its citation and record survive unorphaned (Bill 16 s.9(3), Bill 25 s.6); its status is altered only by a competent court, and the matter is re-determined prospectively by a properly constituted court under the restorative remedy (s.6, s.18(5)). A legacy repo's forward conformance duty (s.8) is to triage its citator against this standard, mark the improperly-constituted rulings with the records-regime status vocabulary, and re-determine only those a live matter relies upon; it owes no duty to re-litigate void rulings nothing turns on.

**5. OBITER**

The Defendant's s.2 motion is sound as a general proposition and I would record it: were the Principal acting as Prime Minister to **order** the bulk citator marked "void" on commencement, that executive act would be ultra vires, because the savings clause and s.7(1) forbid voiding-by-commencement-alone; the lawful route is the per-ruling triage and re-determination this judgment prescribes, or an express Sovereign amendment. Nothing in this judgment authorises a wholesale executive purge.

**6. DISPOSITION**

**VARY** - the leapfrog is properly before us and answered: legacy improper-bench rulings are neither saved as good law nor retroactively erased, but stand subject to prospective declaration and re-determination on a triage; properly-constituted legacy rulings are saved.

**7. PROPOSED ENACTMENTS**

**PROPOSED S-23: Transitional validity of pre-commencement rulings; the triage duty.**

(1) For the purposes of Bill 25 s.6 and CASE-LAW s.6 and s.8, a pre-commencement ruling is "validly-made" if, and only if, the bench that delivered it satisfied the bench-constitution requirement of s.10 (as conformed by [2026] LEXBY-SC 3) and s.18 - odd-numbered, sized, with no deciding voice or synthesiser added on top of the sized panel. Validity is measured against that constitutional requirement, which the bench-size articles always bore, and not against the assertions of any superseded court machinery.

(2) A pre-commencement ruling that satisfies subsection (1) is saved by Bill 25 s.6, retains its lifecycle status, and binds within its jurisdiction-repo until displaced by ordinary means. A pre-commencement ruling that does not satisfy subsection (1) is not validly made and is not saved; it is not good law and may not be cited as binding.

(3) The nullity declared by s.18(5) operates as a self-executing, automatic, and incurable vice only as to benches constituted on or after commencement (2026-06-06). As to a pre-commencement improperly-constituted bench, the nullity is declaratory: it is given effect by a competent court declaring the ruling void and re-determining the matter prospectively under the restorative remedy (s.6); it does not of its own force, on commencement alone, reopen any settled matter, strip any party of standing or remedy (Bill 25 s.7(1)), erase any record, or orphan any citation (Bill 16 s.9(3)).

(4) A jurisdiction-repo bears a forward conformance duty (s.8) to triage its citator against subsection (1): to identify on the face of the record the rulings constituted by an improper bench, to mark them with the records-regime status vocabulary (Bill 20), and to re-determine by a properly constituted court only those upon which a live matter relies. There is no duty to re-litigate a void ruling nothing turns on.

(5) Nothing in this section is to be read as a retroactive amendment of s.10 or s.18; s.18(5) supplies the prospective enforcement machinery for a constitutional requirement those articles always bore (Bill 25 s.2(2); Bill 15 s.3). Any purported executive act voiding rulings in bulk by reason only of commencement is ultra vires (s.2).

**8. PROPOSED OVERRULINGS**

No overrulings proposed. The properly-constituted legacy rulings (including the lawful single-judge entries among CC-ACMECO 1-14) stand; the improperly-constituted ones are not overruled but await declaration and re-determination under the triage above.
