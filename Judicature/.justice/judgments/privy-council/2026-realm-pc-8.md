## Judgment
# [2026] LEXBY-LEGACY-BENCHMARK-FI 3 - Re Domain-Specific Courts vs One Spine

Tier: First Instance (Goffe J, sitting alone, s. 18). Decided: 2026-06-05.
Status: ruling delivered; certified for an appeal panel (needs_appeal_panel: true) - the entrenchment question touches the constitutional s. 9 and s. 14 and only the Supreme Court can enact a constitutional re-constitution (s. 9). Pending the Principal's leapfrog certificate (VPR 4) or ordinary progression. Correctives below are ordered but held pending the higher court.

Question (Principal): should VJS be re-organised into domain-specific courts (a real-estate court, a legal court, a coding court), mirroring the agents-final department model, or stay unified? Evidence: agents-final (~31 departments, one shared shape per domain).

# Disposition: one spine, domain divisions (NOT separate domain courts)

- Characterisation. Two things wear one robe: (1) VJS governance (SPEC-LAW, VPR, duty of care, bench constitution) - within s. 14; and (2) the benchmark agent's legal-analysis "court" - a product feature that borrows the deliberation pattern, NOT governance (s. 14 excludes substantive client legal advice). The confusion between them is the mischief.
- Refused: fully-separate domain courts. Separate courts each owning substantive law = the "competing sovereigns" s. 9 forbids; would duplicate the one spine; fails the SPEC-LAW-3 split test.
- Held (the law): the English-High-Court model (one court, many divisions - King's Bench / Chancery / Commercial) is already latent in s. 9. A repo is a jurisdiction; the domain supplies only the content of the matter and the form of the output, never a parallel statute or court. Divisions over one spine. No amendment needed to permit divisions; carving into sovereign courts is what would need (and is refused).
- agents-final is the right pattern for the product (per-department skills/outputs), and wrong for the judiciary (unitary, not a department).

Shared spine (must not fork): SPEC-LAW; the VPR; bench constitution (s. 10/s. 18); duty of care (s. 4-8, 15, 16); progression (s. 10, 13); one governance citation series + citator; jurisdiction-local caselaw as the s. 9 mechanism.

Domain-specific (may vary): the substantive content of a matter; the output FORM (architecture ratio vs risk memo vs remediation order); agents-final department capability; the benchmark legal-analysis product; per-repo local precedent.
