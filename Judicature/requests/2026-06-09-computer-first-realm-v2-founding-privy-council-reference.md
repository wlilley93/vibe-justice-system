## Preliminary: what this reference is, and what it is not

The V2 Kernel Team has built a compact, machine-checkable successor system, "VJS V2" (the repository `agent-universe-v2`), intended to govern AI-assisted software engineering through a deterministic kernel and a small lawpack rather than long-form prose. V2 has reached a structural point at which it cannot lawfully proceed without a constitutional settlement from the realm's own courts.

Lexby files this reference in its enacted offices (advocate, advisor, engineer: CASE-LAW s.3 as amended by Bill 28) and as the s.18(4) registrar who will reduce the bench's already-formed decision to writing. **Lexby does not decide this matter and may not.** The bench receives a symmetric, two-sided researched case file with no access to Lexby's preference (CASE-LAW s.19(1); the case file is filed alongside this reference). Consistent with [2026] REALM-SC 8 and [2026] REALM-PC 12, every V2 record that purports to be law is a **void first draft** carrying no force on its own account until the proper organ adopts it; nothing in this reference, and nothing in the V2 lawpack, becomes binding by the fact of being written.

## The Question

> Whether all law of V2 - including law drafted, proposed, voted on, compiled, or maintained by AI agents or by a computational legislature - requires Sovereign assent, either specifically to the instrument or through a prior bounded lawmaking route whose maker, scope, form, validation, review, and commencement conditions are fixed by Sovereign-assented law; and, anterior to that, whether V1 may lawfully found V2 as a computer-first successor system that supersedes V1 as live runtime law while preserving V1 as the Gazette and Archive of the realm.

The Question has three limbs:

1. **The competence limb (largely settled).** May an AI agent, or a standing computational legislature, hold law-making authority on its own account in V2? Or is its output void as an instrument until adopted by the lawful organ?
2. **The founding limb (first-impression, foundational).** May V1 lawfully found V2 as a computer-first successor realm - superseding V1 as live runtime law, preserving V1 as Gazette and Archive, and relaying its own founding authority to V2?
3. **The continuity limb (first-impression on the cross-estate point).** Does the V1-to-V2 handover preserve a single Gazette of the realm with two estates (V1 Archive, V2 Current), without the Gazette becoming the source of V2's runtime force?

## Proposed Doctrine (for the bench to settle, vary, or reject)

Lexby advances, as advocate, the following doctrine and invites the bench to test it against the contrary case set out in the symmetric file:

**On the competence limb.** AI may draft, deliberate, propose, consolidate, and operate delegated lawmaking machinery, but:
- may not be sovereign;
- may not expand its own competence;
- may not amend the assent rule;
- may not create legal force from its own output.

This is, on Lexby's case, nothing more than [2026] REALM-SC 8 / [2026] REALM-PC 12 and Bill 14 s.9 ("capability is not authority") applied to a standing machine instead of a single draft: the *source of the artefact's force* is the promulgating organ, never the agent's keystrokes. A "computational legislature" is the Bill 14 s.9 agent at scale; its drafts acquire force only when a named gate fires, and the sole constitutive gate for an Act remains Royal Assent (Bill 2 s.21).

**On the founding limb.** V1 may lawfully found V2 because the founding force comes not from V2's self-assertion but from V1's lawful organs: a Committee draft and the Sovereign's Royal Assent to a constitutional Act (proposed "Bill 32, the Computer-First Realm Act 2026"), settled on a Supreme Court ruling. V2 does not authorise itself; V1 authorises V2, and V2 receives that settlement as founding provenance.

**On the continuity limb.** One Gazette, two estates. Publication in the Gazette does not create runtime force; V2's force derives from the V2 lawpack and the kernel-recognised status of its records, not from Gazette publication. V1 is preserved, honoured, and citable as archive, never deleted.

## Why this is for the court, and not for Lexby or the V2 kernel to settle

Lexby is a party to this process, not its author (CASE-LAW s.3, s.19; the nemo iudex discipline affirmed in [2026] REALM-PC 12 Part IV). The V2 kernel is, by its own charter, a deterministic clerk that "never legislates, never adjudicates, never creates legal force by computation." If V2 were to treat its own draft founding records as binding, that would be the precise vice [2026] REALM-SC 8 condemns: an agent self-authorising law. The lawful route is to put the question to the realm's courts and the Sovereign. That is what this reference does.

## On all fours, or genuinely first-impression?

Lexby candidly divides the Question for the bench:

- The **competence limb** is, on Lexby's submission, governed on all fours by [2026] REALM-SC 8 and [2026] REALM-PC 12 (VPR 2 fast-path may dispose of it on citation). The bench should confirm or correct that characterisation.
- The **founding limb** and the **continuity limb** are **genuinely first-impression and foundational**. No binding ratio decides whether one realm may found a computer-first successor realm, supersede itself as live law, and relay its founding authority across an estate boundary. This is constitutional in the fullest sense and engages the s.10 trigger for the full court of nine.

For that reason Lexby asks the Privy Council to hear the reference at first instance, to settle what is on all fours, and - finding genuine constitutional tension on the founding and continuity limbs - to **leapfrog the matter to the Supreme Court of nine** (Bill 2 s.18(1)(b); CASE-LAW s.13, s.20; the route taken in [2026] REALM-PC 12 -> [2026] REALM-SC 8). Royal Assent to any resulting Bill remains reserved to the Sovereign Founder and is neither sought nor presumed by this reference (Bill 2 s.21).
