# Record 0009 — the archive rename defeated the publication boundary

Caught at the moment of publishing v3, before any push.

Archiving v2 copied its working runtime `.vjs/` to `archive/v2/dot-vjs-runtime/`.
v2's `.gitignore` kept `.vjs/private/`, `submissions/`, `court/`, `orders/`,
`logs/`, `permits/` and `invocation/` out of this public repo; the rename matched
none of those patterns, so 650 of 665 files became tracked. Among them
`private/pseudonym-register.md` — the map from pseudonym to referent, the one
artefact the whole redaction apparatus exists to withhold.

`scripts/promote-canonical.sh` did not catch it either: its private-path guard
matches `^\.vjs/private/`, a literal path, and the path had moved.

Cured before publication by excising the tree from all four unpushed commits —
a deletion commit would have left the blobs reachable forever — and by ignoring
the path so the runtime stays on disk, untracked. Nothing of legal weight is
lost: v2's own `.gitignore` records that the load-bearing law was transferred to
`lawpack/v2/` on 2026-08-06, and that is archived in full at
`archive/v2/lawpack/`, 227 of 227 files, blob-identical to master.

The doctrine, which is not new: **a guard that matches a path does not survive a
rename.** The boundary must be stated over the content's classification, not
over where it happens to sit today.
